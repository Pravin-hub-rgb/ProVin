## Phase 1 — Financial Literacy (Weeks 2–7)
> Goal: Learn the "language of business." You cannot skip this.

### 1.1 — The 3 Financial Statements
- [ ] **Income Statement (P&L)** — Are we making money?
  - Resource: Khan Academy → [Accounting & Financial Statements](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/core-finance/accounting-and-financial-stateme)
- [ ] **Balance Sheet** — What do we own vs owe?
  - Resource: Same Khan Academy series (free, ~2 hrs total)
- [ ] **Cash Flow Statement** — Where is the actual money?
  - Resource: Same series
- [ ] **How one transaction changes all 3 statements** *(critical exercise)*
  - Task: Open Google Sheets. Record 5 sample transactions (buy inventory, make a sale, pay rent, take a loan, buy equipment). Track how each changes all 3 statements manually.

### 1.2 — Core Finance Concepts
- [ ] **Time Value of Money (TVM)** — ₹100 today > ₹100 tomorrow
  - Resource: Khan Academy → search *"time value of money"*
- [ ] **Present Value (PV) & Future Value (FV)**
  - Task: Use Excel `=PV()` and `=FV()` functions after learning the concept
- [ ] **Revenue vs Profit vs Cash** — these are NOT the same thing
- [ ] **Fixed Costs vs Variable Costs**
- [ ] **Break-even Analysis**
  - Task: Calculate break-even for your brother's company (even roughly)

### 1.3 — Revise Your Stock Market Basics
- [ ] P/E Ratio, EPS, Market Cap — what do they actually mean for a company
- [ ] EBITDA — you'll hear this word 1000 times as CFO
- [ ] Debt vs Equity — two ways to raise money, very different consequences
  - Resource: Read `guide.md` → Section: "Paytm, Zepto and How Startups Raise Money"

**✅ Phase 1 Complete when:** You can read a basic P&L and explain if a company is doing well or not.

## Phase 2 — Accounting Mechanics (Weeks 8–13)
> Goal: Understand how money is actually recorded. You don't need to become an accountant — but you need to know enough to review an accountant's work.

### 2.1 — Bookkeeping Basics
- [ ] **Debits & Credits** — the most confusing thing in accounting, explained simply
  - Resource: YouTube — *"Debits and Credits for beginners"* by Accounting Stuff (channel name)
- [ ] **Double Entry System** — every transaction has 2 sides
- [ ] **Chart of Accounts** — the master list of all financial categories

### 2.2 — Indian Accounting Basics
- [ ] **GST Basics** — what it is, how it works, input tax credit
  - Resource: ClearTax website → GST basics section (free, India-specific)
- [ ] **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)** — when you pay someone, sometimes you deduct tax
- [ ] **PAN, TAN, GSTIN** — what these are and why your company needs them
- [ ] **Invoicing rules in India** — what a valid GST invoice looks like

### 2.3 — Set Up Real Books for Your Brother's Company
- [ ] Install **GnuCash** (free, open source)
  - Resource: GnuCash docs → [Getting Started Guide](https://www.gnucash.org/docs/v4/C/gnucash-guide/)
- [ ] Create Chart of Accounts for the company
- [ ] Record first 10 real transactions
- [ ] Generate first P&L report from GnuCash

**✅ Phase 2 Complete when:** Your brother's company has actual books set up and first month recorded.

## Phase 3 — Excel & Financial Modelling (Weeks 14–19)
> Goal: Build real financial models. This is where your tech background helps a lot.

### 3.1 — Excel/Google Sheets for Finance
- [ ] **Pivot Tables** — summarise large data instantly
- [ ] **XLOOKUP / VLOOKUP** — look up values across sheets
- [ ] **IF, SUMIF, COUNTIF** — conditional calculations
- [ ] **Data Validation & Drop-downs** — clean data entry
  - Resource: YouTube — *"Excel for Finance"* by Leila Gharani (channel)

### 3.2 — Build a 3-Statement Model
> This is the core skill of any finance person. Build one from scratch.
- [ ] Income Statement projection (next 12 months)
- [ ] Balance Sheet projection
- [ ] Cash Flow Statement (linked to above two)
- [ ] Connect all 3 so changing one number updates everything
  - Resource: YouTube — *"3 statement model Excel tutorial"* by Kenji Explains (channel)

### 3.3 — Budgeting & Forecasting
- [ ] **Zero-Based Budgeting** — justify every expense from scratch each period
- [ ] **Rolling Forecast** — update predictions every month, not once a year
- [ ] **Variance Analysis** — Actual vs Budget, why did we over/underspend?
  - Task: Build a simple budget for your brother's company for next 6 months

**✅ Phase 3 Complete when:** You have a working 3-statement model for your brother's company in Excel/Sheets.

## Phase 4 — Startup CFO Toolkit (Weeks 20–27)
> Goal: The actual day-to-day job of a startup CFO.

### 4.1 — Cash Management
- [ ] **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** — how long between spending money and getting it back
- [ ] **Accounts Receivable** — money people owe you, how to track and chase it
- [ ] **Accounts Payable** — money you owe others, how to manage payment timing
- [ ] **Runway** — how many months can the company survive on current cash?
  - Task: Calculate your brother's company's runway right now

### 4.2 — Startup Metrics & KPIs
- [ ] **Burn Rate** — how fast you're spending money monthly
- [ ] **Gross Margin** — revenue minus direct costs, as a percentage
- [ ] **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — cost to get one new customer
- [ ] **LTV (Lifetime Value)** — total revenue one customer brings over their life
- [ ] **LTV:CAC Ratio** — the most important ratio for startups (should be >3:1)
  - Resource: Read `guide.md` → Section: "How Zepto Thinks About Money"

### 4.3 — Fundraising Basics
- [ ] **Bootstrapping vs Raising Money** — tradeoffs
- [ ] **Angel Investors vs Venture Capital** — who they are and what they want
- [ ] **Term Sheet basics** — what investors ask for
- [ ] **Cap Table** — who owns what percentage of the company
  - Resource: YouTube — *"startup fundraising explained"* by Y Combinator (channel)

### 4.4 — Financial Reporting
- [ ] Build a **Monthly Financial Dashboard** in Google Sheets
- [ ] **MIS Report (Management Information System)** — 1-page monthly summary for founders
- [ ] Present first monthly financial report to your brother

**✅ Phase 4 Complete when:** You're producing monthly financials for the company and tracking KPIs.

## Phase 5 — India-Specific Finance & Compliance (Weeks 28–31)
> Goal: Stay legal. This is non-negotiable for any Indian company.

### 5.1 — Company Registration & Structure
- [ ] **Private Limited vs LLP vs Sole Proprietorship** — which is your brother's company?
- [ ] **ROC (Registrar of Companies)** — annual filings required
- [ ] **MCA Portal** — where company filings happen in India

### 5.2 — Tax Compliance
- [ ] **GST Filing** — GSTR-1, GSTR-3B (monthly/quarterly)
  - Resource: ClearTax → GST filing guides (free)
- [ ] **TDS Filing** — quarterly TDS returns
- [ ] **Advance Tax** — paying tax in installments through the year
- [ ] **ITR for the company** — annual income tax return

### 5.3 — Payroll & Employee Finance
- [ ] **PF (Provident Fund)** — mandatory once you hire employees
- [ ] **ESI** — employee state insurance
- [ ] **Salary structuring** — HRA, LTA, basic — how to structure CTC smartly

### 5.4 — Banking & Payments
- [ ] Open a **current account** for the company (if not done)
- [ ] Set up **UPI/Razorpay/Cashfree** for receiving payments
- [ ] Understand **NEFT vs RTGS vs IMPS** for payments

**✅ Phase 5 Complete when:** Company is fully compliant — GST registered, books maintained, filings up to date.

## Phase 6 — Corporate Finance & Strategy (Weeks 32–39)
> Goal: Start thinking like a CFO, not just an accountant. This is where you build toward Corporate CFO.

### 6.1 — Valuation
- [ ] **DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)** — most important valuation method
  - Resource: YouTube — *"DCF valuation explained"* by Aswath Damodaran (the best teacher on this)
- [ ] **Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)**
- [ ] **Precedent Transactions**
- [ ] **How Indian startups are valued** — GMV multiples, revenue multiples

### 6.2 — Capital Allocation
- [ ] **CapEx vs OpEx** — buying an asset vs paying an expense
- [ ] **WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)** — what it costs to fund the business
- [ ] **NPV & IRR** — how to evaluate whether an investment is worth it
  - Task: Evaluate one real investment decision for your brother's company using NPV

### 6.3 — M&A Basics
- [ ] **Why companies acquire other companies**
- [ ] **Due Diligence** — what to check before buying/merging
- [ ] **Synergies** — the financial logic of M&A

### 6.4 — Risk Management
- [ ] **Financial Risk types** — market, credit, liquidity, operational
- [ ] **Internal Controls** — preventing fraud and errors
- [ ] **Insurance basics** — what coverage a company needs

**✅ Phase 6 Complete when:** You can value your brother's company using at least 2 methods and present it to an investor.

## 📚 Core Books (Read in this order)
1. **Financial Intelligence for Entrepreneurs** — Karen Berman & Joe Knight *(Start here, plain English)*
2. **The Lean CFO** — Anders Liu-Lindberg *(Startup-focused)*
3. **Principles of Corporate Finance** — Brealey, Myers & Allen *(When you're ready for depth)*
4. **The Intelligent Investor** — Benjamin Graham *(For investment thinking)*

## 🎓 Certification Path (When Ready)
| Certification | What it covers | When to do it |
|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| CMA (Cost & Management Accountant) | Corporate strategy, management accounting | After Phase 6 |
| CFA Level 1 | Investment analysis, capital markets | After Phase 6 |
| CA Foundation | Indian accounting, tax, law | Optional — very rigorous |

> **Note:** CMA is more relevant for CFO work than CA. CA is for auditors/tax practitioners.
