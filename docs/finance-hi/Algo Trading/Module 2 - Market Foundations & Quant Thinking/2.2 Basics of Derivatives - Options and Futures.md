# 2.2 Basics of Derivatives — Options and Futures

Pichli file mein humne spot market ka engine dekha — orders kaise match hote hain, price kaise banta hai. Ab ek aur duniya dekhte hain jo spot se derive hoti hai — **derivatives**. Futures aur Options kya hote hain, kaise kaam karte hain, margin/MTM/settlement kya hai, aur algo traders inhe practically kaise use karte hain — yeh sab is file mein upar-upar se cover karenge taaki foundation ban jaye.

Ek baat pehle hi clear kar doon: agar tune aaj tak kabhi options trade nahi kiya aur bilkul first time dekh raha hai, toh pehle YouTube pe "options basics" type karke 1-2 videos dekh aa — phir yeh file padhna, kyunki yahan basics fast pace mein jaayenge.

## Derivative Kya Hota Hai

"Derivative" word derivation se aaya hai — matlab **kisi cheez ki value ko DERIVE karna**.

Derivative ka ek underlying instrument hota hai — wo commodity ho sakta hai, stock ho sakta hai, index ho sakta hai — koi bhi cheez.

Core definition:

> **Derivative khud ki value NAHI rakhta. Uske underlying instrument ki ek value hoti hai — us value se derive hokar derivative ki value aati hai.**

English mein: "Derivatives are financial instruments whose value derives from an underlying asset."

Example se samajh: Index ka derivative = Future. Future ki value kahan se derive hoti hai? Index se. Index ki spot value apni original value hai, aur usi value se derive hokar future ki value nikalti hai. Option bhi isi tarah derive hota hai.

Underlying asset ke examples: Nifty, Bank Nifty, stocks, commodity, currency, interest rates.

Interest rate derivatives ka ek note — ye basically hedge funds/professional/proprietary bade firms use karte hain, retail ke liye nahi hain. India mein ye band ho gaya hai; agar India ke bahar ho toh currency derivatives useful hain.

Derivatives majorly 2 types ke hote hain:

1. **Futures**
2. **Options**

## Derivative Market Kyun Exist Karta Hai

Yeh sawaal pehle uthta hai — jab spot market hai hi, toh derivatives kyun?

**1. Risk Management:** Risk manage karne ke liye. Practically badi ulat cheez lagti hai, but yahi core reason hai.

**2. Hedging:** Hum log normally use nahi karte, par mutual funds use karti hain. Iski kahani thodi interesting hai — future contract actually HEDGING ke liye hi aaya tha. Lekin kya hua? Leverage ki wajah se log speculation karne lage — jaldi paisa kamane ke liye. Mutual funds (jaise HDFC MC) aaj bhi derivative use karti hain for hedging — inke exposure mein jaake dekh sakte ho kitne % holding, kitne % options trade. Ye option buy bhi karti hain aur call short bhi. SEBI ke naye rules ke hisaab se certain limit tak hi derivative positions bana sakte hain, aur naya instrument launch hua hai jisme investor ke liye 10 lakh minimum ticket size hai aur usme 100% tak derivative use ho sakta hai.

**3. Speculation:** Hum sab yeh use karte hain — leverage ke liye.

**4. Arbitrage:** Do exchanges ke beech price discrepancy kam karne ke liye. Bina derivative contracts ke arbitrage possible nahi hai.

Formula yaad rakh:

> **DERIVATIVE = CONTROLLED RISK + LEVERAGE**
> *(controlled risk wala part theoretically theoretical hai)*

## Part 1 — Futures

### Definition

> **"A future contract is an agreement to buy and sell an asset at a pre-determined price on a future date."**

### Gold Contract Example

Isse samajhne ke liye classic example lete hain — Amit aur Deepti, asset = gold.

Example ka time gold ka bhav ≈ ₹1,39,720 chal raha tha. Deepti Amit se bolti hai: *"Main tumse 1 kilo sona lungi, ₹1,35,000 ke rate par"* — pre-determined price. Pre-determined future date pe (suppose 2 months baad) settlement hoga — us din Amit ko Deepti ko itna sona pre-determined price pe dena padega.

Ye theoretically ek future contract hai.

### Future Contract Ki Properties

- **Standardized contract:** Exchange-defined terms
- **Lot size fix:** Har contract ka pehle se fix (e.g., 50 units) — ek lot ka matlab pata hota hai
- **Expiry fix:** Weekly ya monthly expiry — time period fixed
- **Leverage contract:** Poora contract value nahi dete, sirf margin dete hain

### Leverage Ka Sach

> **You don't pay the full contract — you pay only MARGIN (15%-20%).**

Nifty calculation se dekho:

```
Nifty level:        22,000
Lot size:           50
Contract value:     22,000 × 50 = ₹11,00,000 (11 lakh)
Margin required:    ≈ ₹1,20,000 (approx)

→ Leverage ≈ 9x
```

Stock example: stock ₹200 ka chal raha hai → 1 quantity = ₹200. Future mein lot size fix hota hai; normally 5x se 8x leverage milta hai. Agar 5x leverage → ₹4,000 mein ₹20,000 ka maal mil gaya!

**Future = ek leverage contract jo future date mein trade kar sakte ho.**

### Index Pe Max Leverage Kyun Milta Hai

Notice kiya — index futures mein ~9x leverage milta hai, stocks mein sirf 4-5x. Kyun?

Index ek tarah ka portfolio of stocks hai. Saare stocks ek raat mein zero nahi ho sakte. Over time achhe stocks andar aate rehte hain, bakhiya stocks bahar nikalte rehte hain. Isliye index = low risk → sabse zyada leverage (9x).

Stocks mein high risk hai — company ke saath kuch bhi ho sakta hai — isliye stocks 4x-5x se zyada leverage nahi hote.

### Future Mein Profit/Loss Kaise Hota Hai

```
BUY Nifty futures @ 22,000
        ↓
Price goes to 22,200 → PROFIT = 200 points × lot size 50 = ₹10,000 ✅
Price drops to 21,800 → LOSS   = 200 points × lot size 50 = ₹10,000 ❌
```

Simple math hai — points × lot size. Aur ye settlement daily M2M (Mark to Market) hoti hai — iska matlab is file ke end mein dekhenge.

### Long vs Short

- **Long Future:** Tu expect karta hai market RISE karega 📈
- **Short Future:** Tu expect karta hai market FALL karega 📉

Farmer wala classic example bhi yaad rakh — farmer ko apne wheat/commodity ka price future date pe settle karna hai. Wo futures market mein SHORT kar deta hai usi rate pe. Future date pe jab settlement chahiye → usi price pe milega jis price pe short kiya tha. Isse uska risk hedge ho jata hai.

## Part 2 — Options

### Sabse Bada Difference — Obligation vs Right

Futures mein (Amit-Deepti example) DONO parties pe OBLIGATION tha — predetermined price, predetermined date, predetermined quantity pe settle karna hi padega.

Option ek DYNAMIC contract hai:

- Buyer ke paas RIGHT hai — option use kar sakta hai YA use nahi karna chahta toh nahi karega
- Buyer pe koi obligation nahi

### Bayana Analogy — Options Ka Best Explanation

Options samajhne ka sabse easy tareeka:

> Maine tujhe ₹5,000 ka bayana de diya ₹1 lakh ke ghar ka.
> - Ghar 6 mahine baad mujhe pasand aaya → main lunga (right use kiya)
> - Pasand nahi aaya → bayana jaayega, main nahi lunga (right drop kiya)
>
> **Bayana = PREMIUM. Yehi option hai!**

### Call Aur Put

**CALL OPTION:** Buyer expects price to go UP 📈 | Seller expects price to stay below strike.

Example: Nifty 22,000 pe chal raha hai. Maine 22,500 strike ka call option purchase kar liya (~₹1,500 premium). Nifty expire hua 23,000 pe. Matlab mera option 500 points ITM (In The Money) ho gaya ✅. Ab main apna option exercise karunga.

**PUT OPTION:** Buyer expects price to go DOWN 📉.

Example: Buy Nifty 22,000 put @ premium × lot size 50 = initial investment. Nifty 21,700 pe aa gaya → intrinsic value ₹300 ban gayi → profit zone.

Option mein 3 cheezein hoti hain:

1. Premium
2. Expiration Date
3. ITM hai ya OTM (status)

### Option Chain Samjho

Option chain ek table hoti hai jismein:

- Beech mein **strike price** column hota hai — 21800, 21900, 22000, 22100...
- Ek taraf **CALL side**, dusri taraf **PUT side**
- Dono taraf columns hote hain: Ask Quantity, Bid, Ask, **LTP**, **OI (Open Interest)**, Change in OI
- **LTP column mein premiums** likhe jaate hain — premium = bayana ka rate. Poora contract nahi, sirf premium trade hota hai
- **OI** = total kitne contracts khade hain us strike pe

Change in OI + OI use karke aage Zero-to-Hero strategy banayenge — detail mein aage aayega.

### Option BUYER vs SELLER — Risk Analysis

| | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| Kya hai | **RIGHT** — predetermined price pe buy karna hai ya nahi | **OBLIGATION** — us time pe contract dena HI padega |
| Risk | **Limited** (premium tak) | **Theoretically UNLIMITED** ⚠️ |

Seller ka unlimited risk example: Maine 22,000 ka call SELL kiya. Theoretically 22,000 se UPAR jitna bhi market jayega, UTNA MERA LOSS 😱. Buyer ko koi obligation nahi — uska loss sirf premium tak. Seller ki obligation: Nifty 22,000 se upar chalegi toh mujhe contract dena padega.

Aur ek interesting fact — market sirf EK strike pe close hota hai. Ek side call, ek side put — dono mein se max dono ZERO ho sakte hain. Baaki saari strikes (~8 upar + ~8 neeche) expire worthless ho jaati hain. Isiliye seller ka win rate zyada hota hai.

Key line: **Seller ka risk zyada hai → isliye SEBI ne seller se HIGHER MARGIN demand kiya hai.**

Isi wajah se option selling zyada popular hai — high win rate, time decay se benefit, premium income. Buyer ke liye limited loss toh hai par break-even hurdle high hai aur time decay uska nuksan karti hai.

## Part 3 — Option Concepts

### Strike Price

> **"Pre-determined price at which option is based."**

Do cheezein hoti hain: SPOT (underlying ka current price) aur OPTION ka strike price.

SBI stock example: SBI chal raha hai ₹967 pe. Option chain mein strikes fixed difference pe hoti hain — SBI mein 5-5 rupaye ka difference:

```
...955, 960, 965, [967 spot], 970, 975, 980, 985...
```

Har specific strike pe alag contracts trade hote hain. Har strike ka premium (bayana/LTP) alag hota hai. Inhi premiums ko trade kiya jaata hai.

### ITM / ATM / OTM

- **ATM (At The Money):** Current market rate ki nearest strike price
- **ITM (In The Money):** Option mein real/intrinsic value wali region
- **OTM (Out of The Money):** Bahar ki strikes

Call option ke liye rules:

- Spot > Strike → **ITM**
- Spot = Strike → **ATM**
- Spot < Strike → **OTM**

ATM dono (call/put) ke liye same hota hai; OTM call/put opposite sides pe change hota hai. Example: Nifty 26,050 pe chal raha hai → 26,000 call mein ₹50 ITM (intrinsic) value hai.

### Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value

> **PREMIUM = INTRINSIC VALUE + TIME VALUE (extrinsic value)**

- **Intrinsic Value:** REAL value — jo actually milegi exercise pe
- **Time Value (extrinsic):** Real value ke UPAR jo extra premium hai

Example 1: Mera 22,000 ka call option hai, Nifty chal raha hai 22,300 pe. 22,300 − 22,000 = 300 points REAL value (intrinsic). Premium trade ho raha hai ~400+ pe → 300 ke upar jitna hai wo TIME VALUE.

Example 2 (real option chain — Nifty 9 December): Nifty chal raha hai 26,050. 26,100 call = ₹149 premium. 26,000 call = ₹208 premium. 26,000 call mein ₹50 intrinsic value (26,050 − 26,000) + baaki sab time value.

⚠️ Important: **OTM options mein koi real value NAHI hoti — poori ki poori TIME VALUE hoti hai.**

### Option Greeks — Brief Intro

Greeks option pricing ke sensitivity measures hain. Abhi brief intro, detail aage:

- **Delta:** Sensitivity to price movement — ₹1 spot move hoga toh premium kitna move hoga. Call delta positive, put delta negative.
- **Theta:** Time decay — ek din mein kitni time value decay hogi. Sellers ke liye benefit ✅, buyers ke liye nuksan ❌.
- **Vega:** Effect of volatility — volatility 1% upar/neeche toh option price kitna move karega. High IV = expensive premium, low IV = cheap premium.
- **Gamma:** Delta CHANGE hone pe option price kitna change hoga. Second order effect (delta first order hai).

Padhne ke shaukeen ke liye: internet pe CFA Level 1 ki Derivatives book mil jayegi — poora deeply explained hai.

## Part 4 — Margin, M2M, Expiry and Settlement

### Margin

Future contract trade karte waqt margin requirement hoti hai. Example: contract value ₹18,00,000 (Nifty) → margin required ≈ ₹2.5 lakh. Margin = initial amount jo poora contract uthane ke liye jama karana padta hai.

Options mein bada difference hai:

- **Option BUYER** sirf PREMIUM deta hai — kyunki uska risk limited hai
- **Option SELLER** ko MARGIN jama karna padta hai — kyunki uska risk theoretically unlimited hai

Amit-Deepti example wapas laao: Deepti = option buyer, Amit = option seller. Nifty option, lot size 75, premium ₹10. Deepti (buyer) deta hai: 10 × 75 = ₹750 sirf! Amit (seller) ko jama karna padega ~₹2.5 lakh margin — kyunki wo unlimited risk pe trade kar raha hai.

### MTM Settlement (Mark to Market)

NSE ne saare futures contracts ka DAILY settlement rakha hai. Daily jo party loss karegi → uske account se paisa KATEGA. Jo party profit karegi → uske account mein paisa JUDEGA. Isliye futures mein PnL roz adjust hota hai, expiry ka wait nahi karna padta.

### Expiry

Weekly expiry kayi baar change hui hai, isliye latest data yaad rakho: **NIFTY expiry = TUESDAY | SENSEX expiry = THURSDAY.**

### Physical Settlement

Index physically settled NAHI hota. Stock futures aur ITM options PHYSICALLY settled hote hain — shares dena/hona padta hai. Agar expiry pe trade square-off nahi kiya, toh saamne wali party ko physical shares arrange karke dene padenge.

Isiliye bahut saare traders expiry se PEHLE square off kar lete hain.

## Practical Use Cases

Theory complete. Ab yeh sab kaam kaise aata hai:

**1. Hedging (portfolio protection with puts):** Maine Nifty ETF ₹1 crore ke buy kar rakhe hain. Nifty spot 20,000 pe hai. Main jaake 1 saal ki Nifty 20,000 PUT purchase kar leta hun (~₹4 lakh ki). Agar Nifty gir gaya (kisi bhi tarah), meri puts ki value bahut zyada ho jayegi → portfolio ka loss cover ✅. Isiliye hedging ke liye puts ka use hota hai.

**2. Speculation:** Mujhe lag raha hai Nifty (abhi 26,000) 27,000 pahunchegi. Toh main 27,000 ka OTM CALL option khareed lunga. Nifty 27,000 pahunchi → faida ho gaya ✅.

**3. Income generation strategies:**

- **Short Strangle:** OTM ke call + put bechna
- **Covered Call:** Mere paas underlying stock pada hai + uske upar call option SHORT kar raha hun (favorite strategy!)
- **Iron Condor:** 2 strikes buy + 2 strikes sell (4 legs)

In sabko aage course mein detail mein banana seekhenge — use cases ke saath.

## Nutshell

Derivative = jiski value underlying asset (stock/index/commodity/currency) se derive hoti hai. 2 major types: **Futures** (dono pe obligation + leverage, sirf margin pay karte ho) aur **Options** (buyer ke paas right, seller pe obligation). Index futures mein max leverage (~9x) kyunki index low-risk portfolio hai; stocks 4-5x tak. P&L simple hai: points × lot size, daily M2M settlement. Option buyer = right (sirf premium); option seller = obligation (margin + unlimited risk → SEBI ne higher margin rakha hai). Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value; OTM mein sirf time value. Greeks: Delta (price sensitivity), Theta (time decay — seller's friend), Vega (volatility), Gamma (delta ka delta). Use cases: hedging (protective puts), speculation (OTM calls), income strategies (short strangle, covered call, iron condor).

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Option buying = easy money nahi.** Haan risk limited hai (premium tak), par time decay tera dushman hai aur break-even hurdle high hai. Isliye sellers ka win rate zyada hota hai.
- ❌ **Futures mein full paisa nahi lagta.** Contract value ₹11 lakh ho, tu sirf margin (~15-20%) deta hai — leverage isi se aata hai. Aur leverage double-edged sword hai — profit bhi 9x, loss bhi 9x.
- ❌ **Expiry pe sab kuch settle nahi hota cash mein.** Index cash-settled hai, par stock futures aur ITM options physically settle hote hain — shares arrange karne padte hain. Isliye expiry se pehle square off karna seekho.
