**In Your Own Words**

1. Core difference between indicator thinking and SMC thinking?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Indicator thinking REACTS to DERIVED data ("9-EMA crossed") — which comes AFTER price (lag!). SMC thinking searches for the REASON of price movement: which side LIQUIDITY was there, which side INSTITUTIONAL orders! Meaning: signal CHASING vs CONTEXT READING. Both goal same (predict direction), but information source DIFFERENT: calculations vs ORDER FLOW logic!

</details>

2. "Price moves from liquidity to liquidity" meaning?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Big players need COUNTERPARTIES for execution — and counterparties most visible as STOP LOSSES/panic orders at OBVIOUS levels (above/below old highs/lows!). Price ATTRACTS toward these zones (fuel!), SWEEPS them (stops trigger = liquidity release!), then next zone... Hence "random" wick hits can actually be ENGINEERED fuel collection!

</details>

3. Why fair to call SMC "rebranded price action," yet worth learning?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** FAIR because: order blocks ≈ supply/demand zones (Wyckoff era!), market structure ≈ Dow theory swings, liquidity sweeps ≈ old "stop hunt" concept! Only PACKAGING new! Still worth because rebrand gave DISCIPLINED FRAMEWORK — defined terms (OB/FVG/BOS) make RULE-BASED systems easier to build, and our goal is rule-based + CODEABLE trading (9.7)! New names, old math — accept BOTH!

</details>

4. Why don't institutions place big order in one click?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Two reasons: (1) SLIPPAGE — such big order pushes PRICE itself up; every share fills at EXPENSIVE rate (2.1 microstructure!). (2) VISIBILITY — everyone sees big buying happened → others RUN before! Hence institutions in SMALL orders, SLOWLY, where LIQUIDITY available — retail stops/panic orders become THEIR counterparties!

</details>
