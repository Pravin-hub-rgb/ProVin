# 2.4 Position Sizing, Risk & Leverage

In the previous file we saw crypto's dark side — 20% crashes, exchange failures, no circuit breakers. There was one line there that now needs to be understood with full weight: **keep risk management double strict.** And the real truth is — in trading the most important part isn't the strategy, it's **risk management.** Good strategy + poor risk control = fail. Sounds strange to hear, but by the end of this file it'll be clear why.

Until risk is defined, there's no success in trading. So today we'll learn answers to three questions: how much to trade, how much risk to take, and how much leverage to use.

## The 3 Pillars of Position Sizing

Position sizing means — how much money to put in each trade. It has 3 pillars:

**Pillar 1 — How much to trade?** How many trades should happen — per day, per month. More trades ≠ more profit.

**Pillar 2 — How much risk to take?** What % of your capital to risk without mental damage. This pillar is the most important.

**Pillar 3 — How much leverage to use?** The surprising thing — you don't even need to decide this. When Pillar 2 (risk) is defined, leverage comes out automatically. Risk first, then leverage — not the other way around.

One concept to grab here — **mental drawdown.** Emotional drawdown is more dangerous than account drawdown. If a loss mentally breaks you, the next trade will be in revenge mode — and that's where a series of losses begins. So keep risk low enough that mental drawdown doesn't happen. And yes — if capital is small you can take a slightly higher % risk; a professional trader is professional precisely because they take a lower % risk.

## The 6 Types of Trading Risk

Risk isn't just one — there are 6 types. Understand each:

**1. Market Risk:** Loss from unfavorable price moves. Example — Nifty hits 10% circuit and your overnight position gets stuck. Gap-up/gap-down fear falls in this category.

**2. Leverage Risk:** Most people die here. Think — you used 0.5 lot and thought "it's only ~₹50,000 exposure." Wrong! Nifty future's contract value is ~₹18 lakh. A 10% gap move = ₹1.8 lakh loss — 80-90% wipeout of your value. Leverage turns a small position into a nuclear bomb.

**3. Liquidity Risk:** You entered illiquid option strikes or small-cap stocks, now can't exit. In panic you can't get out without paying impact cost (remember 2.1).

**4. Systematic Risk:** RBI policy announcement, elections, US market crash. This hits the whole system at once — **not diversifiable.**

**5. Strategy Risk:** Market conditions changed or the strategy was overfit — strategy goes into drawdown phase.

**6. Execution Risk:** Slippage, API delay, order not filled. Good news — this can be minimized (smart execution techniques, seen in 2.1).

All of these are interconnected — market risk (circuit hit) → leverage risk (overnight position wipeout). And their common solution is one: **diversification.** Diversify instruments, diversify days, diversify strategies.

## Risk Levels — Where to Set Limits

Now practical rules. Risk must be limited at 4 levels:

### Level 1 — Per Trade

| Trader Type | Risk Per Trade |
|---|---|
| Retail Trader | 0.5% - 1% |
| Professional Trader | 0.25% - 0.75% |

If capital is small, take the slightly higher side. If you want to increase winning probability, reduce risk per trade — simple math.

### Level 2 — Per Day

Here's the trap that kills everyone. Suppose you're running 5 strategies, each taking 1% risk. All stop losses hit in one day → **5% gone in one day.** 10 such days = entire capital!

That's why **define maximum risk per day — never above 3-5%.**

And one personal lesson to take for free: 5 strategies were running, all on different instruments (Nifty, BankNifty, FinNifty, Sensex). Thought everything was diversified. Then a phase came where **all strategies' drawdown came together** — combined drawdown was 100%+, barely survived. Different instruments can also be correlated — this would've been very expensive to learn with money.

### Level 3 — Per Strategy

Don't put **more than 25% capital in any single strategy.** Ideal range is 5-10% per strategy. More concentration = when that strategy goes into drawdown, mental drawdown also goes deeper.

### Level 4 — Per Instrument

Don't put everything on just BankNifty. Keep spread — Nifty + BankNifty + FinNifty. Diversification is a partial solution to every problem, apply it at every level.

## Drawdown Math — What Should Scare You

Look at this table and pay attention. How much gain is needed to recover after a loss:

| Loss | Recovery Needed |
|---|---|
| 20% | 25% gain |
| 50% | **100% gain** |
| 70% | **233% gain** |

See the pattern? After every fall, the speed of getting back up becomes **exponentially** harder. After 50% loss you have to double your money — just to get back. So taking a small loss is cheap, taking a big loss is expensive.

And one bitter truth — in trading there's **no such thing as certainty.** What saves you? Only discipline and diversification.

## Coding the Risk System

An algo trader's advantage is right here — all these rules can be enforced in code. A manual trader could never do this. Your bot should have these elements:

- **Maximum loss limit:** Daily/strategy max loss boundary — trading stops if crossed
- **Maximum trade count:** Only this many trades per day, no more
- **Maximum position sizing:** Lot size cap
- **Emergency stop loss:** One master switch to stop everything
- **Volatility filter:** Block entries during high volatility
- **Time-based shutoff:** Auto-stop per day
- **Limit order usage:** To reduce slippage
- **Slippage control:** Smart slicing — break order into small chunks, execute at right price

Python makes all of this easy. Code has no emotions — it won't break a rule even if you want it to.

## Nutshell

Risk management is bigger than strategy. Position sizing's 3 pillars: how much to trade, how much risk, how much leverage (which comes automatically from risk). 6 risk types — market, leverage, liquidity, systematic, strategy, execution. Remember limits: per trade 0.5-1%, per day max 3-5%, per strategy max 25%, diversify at every level. Drawdown math will scare you — 50% loss needs 100% gain. And an algo trader's superpower: the entire risk system can be coded in Python.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Risk management is not something that reduces profit.** Opposite — it keeps you in the game longer. The one who plays longest learns and wins.
- ❌ **Leverage is not free money.** An ₹18 lakh contract comes at ₹1.2 lakh margin, but loss is on full contract value. Don't take the leverage risk pillar lightly.
- ❌ **Diversification is not a cure for systematic risk.** Elections/crash-type systematic risk hits everything at once — diversification manages the other 5 risks, not this one.
