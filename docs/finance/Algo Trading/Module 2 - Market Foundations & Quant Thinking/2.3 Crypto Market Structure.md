# 2.3 Crypto Market Structure — with Bitcoin Example

So far we were in the Indian stock market's world — NSE, SEBI, circuit breakers, 9:15 to 3:30 market. Now let's look at a different world that's a golden opportunity for algo traders — **crypto**. Everyone's heard of Bitcoin, but from a trader's perspective how its market is structured, how it differs from the stock market, and what's special about it for algo trading — we'll understand all this in this file.

## Crypto vs Indian Stock Market — Core Differences

Compare using the concepts from previous files. Same concepts, new rules:

**1. Market never closes.** NSE opens at 9:15 AM, closes at 3:30 PM. Crypto? **24×7×365.** BTC will be trading even at 2 AM Saturday night. Direct implication for algo trading — your bot never waits for "market close", and monitoring is just as important because there's no gap-up/gap-down concept — the market can move every second.

**2. Exchange = Broker (both together).** Remember the exception from 1.4 — *"Binance is BOTH an exchange AND a broker."* Now it'll become clear. In the stock market you use Zerodha which talks to NSE. In crypto **the exchange is everything for you** — Binance, Coinbase, Delta Exchange (India). They run the matching engine themselves and give you the API themselves. The middle layer (broker) concept has effectively merged inside the exchange in crypto.

**3. Regulation is different.** In India SEBI regulates everything — algo classification, margins, circulars (remember 1.5?). In crypto there's no central regulator like SEBI. Global guidelines are followed, but every exchange makes its own rules. Advantage: fewer restrictions, faster start. Disadvantage: less protection too — that's why risk management (next file's topic) is even more critical.

**4. No circuit breakers.** When a 10% circuit hits on Nifty, trading stops. Nothing like that in crypto. Bitcoin can fall 10-20% in a day and the market won't stop. Your stop loss is your real protection.

## Understand Through Bitcoin Example

Let's see everything practically through Bitcoin (BTC).

### Volatility

Nifty moves ~1% on average in a day. Bitcoin? **5-10% is a normal day.** On extreme days 20%+ has been seen. Example — think BTC went from ₹50 lakh to ₹45 lakh in one night. In the stock market this would be called a crash; in crypto it was a Tuesday.

Implication for an algo trader: both position sizing and stop loss distance should be volatility-adjusted. Don't copy stock parameters.

### Trading Pairs and USDT

In crypto you don't buy coins directly in rupees. Every coin has a **base pair** — the most common is **USDT** (a stablecoin that stays equal to $1).

- `BTC/USDT` = how many USDT for 1 Bitcoin
- `ETH/USDT`, `SOL/BTC` — such pairs

So when people say "BTC $60k", that's actually the BTC/USDT price. When writing algo code, you must understand this symbol format.

### Order Book — Same Microstructure

Everything from 2.1 — bids, asks, best bid/best ask, spread — **works the same in crypto too.** Order book structure is identical. The only differences:

- Liquidity depends on the exchange — BTC spread on Binance is very tight (many buyers-sellers), but wide on small coins
- Because of 24/7, liquidity drops at night — spread widens, just like Bank Nifty options after 3 PM (remember 2.1)

### Perpetual Futures — Crypto's Special Derivative

In 2.2 we read about futures — fixed expiry, M2M settlement. Crypto has its own version: **Perpetual Futures (Perps).**

- Same as futures contract, but **no expiry date** — hold as long as you want
- Leverage available — sometimes up to 100x (stock futures max was ~9x!)
- **Funding rate** is charged to keep the position open — a balance mechanism between longs and shorts

Clear warning here — 100x leverage means the whole account is gone on a 1% adverse move. Perps are high-risk territory for beginners.

## Why Crypto is Best for Algo Learning

This course focuses on crypto also because:

- **Data fetching is easiest.** From platforms like Delta Exchange, BTC/ETH historical data comes without an API key, just via `requests` library. Compare — how heavy Zerodha Kite Connect setup was.
- **No paperwork.** Account creation takes minutes, no SEBI-type compliance.
- **24/7 practice.** Built a bot, can live-test even on weekends.
- **Strategies transfer.** Logic learned on crypto — indicators, backtesting, execution — applies in the Indian market too. Just volatility parameters need adjusting.

## Risks Not Present in Stock Market

For a fair picture, understand the downsides too:

- **Exchange risk:** The exchange itself can get hacked or collapse (FTX example — billion-dollar exchange gone in one night). Your money is on the exchange, not with any regulated clearing corporation.
- **Extreme volatility:** Stop loss can get missed due to slippage — price can gap straight below your SL, and there's no market-closing mechanism.
- **Regulatory uncertainty:** Tax rules and regulations on crypto in India are still evolving.

So the golden rule: **keep risk management double strict in crypto compared to the stock market.** And this is the perfect segue — the next topic is exactly that.

## Nutshell

Crypto market structure: 24×7 market (no close, no circuit breakers), exchange is the broker (Binance/Delta), no SEBI-style regulation. BTC example: 5-10% daily moves are normal, USDT pairing system, order book/microstructure same as 2.1, perps = expiry-free futures with extreme leverage. Best playground for algo learning — free data, easy APIs, instant practice. But risk is double — exchange risk, volatility risk, regulatory risk. So the next topic: position sizing and risk management.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Crypto is not a guaranteed profit machine.** Yes there are more opportunities, but losses are just as fast. Volatility works both ways.
- ❌ **Not all exchanges are the same.** Binance and some new small exchange — liquidity, security, API quality all differ. Choose an established exchange.
- ❌ **"No regulation = no rules" is not true.** Tax obligations still exist (crypto is taxed in India), and exchanges have their own KYC/rules.
