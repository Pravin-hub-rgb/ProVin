# 2.2 Basics of Derivatives — Options and Futures

In the previous file we saw the spot market's engine — how orders match, how price forms. Now let's look at another world that derives from spot — **derivatives**. What futures and options are, how they work, what margin/MTM/settlement is, and how algo traders practically use them — we'll cover all of this upar-upar (surface level) in this file so the foundation gets built.

One thing to clear first: if you've never traded options before and are seeing this for the very first time, first go to YouTube and search "options basics", watch 1-2 videos — then read this file, because we'll go through basics at a fast pace here.

## What is a Derivative

The word "derivative" comes from "derivation" — meaning **deriving the value of something**.

A derivative has an underlying instrument — that could be a commodity, a stock, an index — anything.

Core definition:

> **A derivative has NO value of its own. Its underlying instrument has a value — and the derivative's value derives from that value.**

In English: "Derivatives are financial instruments whose value derives from an underlying asset."

Understand through example: index's derivative = Future. Where does the future's value derive from? From the index. The index's spot value is its original value, and the future's value derives from that same value. Options derive the same way.

Examples of underlying assets: Nifty, Bank Nifty, stocks, commodity, currency, interest rates.

A note on interest rate derivatives — these are basically used by hedge funds/professionals/proprietary big firms, not for retail. In India these are banned; if you're outside India, currency derivatives are useful.

Derivatives are mainly of 2 types:

1. **Futures**
2. **Options**

## Why Does the Derivative Market Exist

This question comes up first — when the spot market already exists, why derivatives?

**1. Risk Management:** To manage risk. Sounds like a big opposite thing practically, but this is the core reason.

**2. Hedging:** We don't normally use it, but mutual funds use it. The story is a bit interesting — the future contract actually came into existence FOR hedging. But what happened? Because of leverage, people started speculating — to make money quickly. Mutual funds (like HDFC MC) still use derivatives for hedging — you can go into their exposure and see what % is holding, what % is options trade. They buy options and also short calls. As per SEBI's new rules, certain limits apply on derivative positions, and a new instrument has launched where the minimum ticket size for the investor is ₹10 lakh and up to 100% derivative use is possible in it.

**3. Speculation:** This is what we all use it for — for leverage.

**4. Arbitrage:** To reduce price discrepancy between two exchanges. Arbitrage isn't possible without derivative contracts.

Remember the formula:

> **DERIVATIVE = CONTROLLED RISK + LEVERAGE**
> *(the controlled risk part is theoretically theoretical)*

## Part 1 — Futures

### Definition

> **"A future contract is an agreement to buy and sell an asset at a pre-determined price on a future date."**

### Gold Contract Example

To understand, take the classic example — Amit and Deepti, asset = gold.

At the time of the example, gold's price ≈ ₹1,39,720. Deepti tells Amit: *"I will buy 1 kilo gold from you at ₹1,35,000"* — pre-determined price. Settlement happens on a pre-determined future date (suppose 2 months later) — on that day Amit must give Deepti that much gold at the pre-determined price.

This is theoretically a future contract.

### Properties of a Future Contract

- **Standardized contract:** Exchange-defined terms
- **Fixed lot size:** Pre-fixed for every contract (e.g., 50 units) — you know what one lot means
- **Fixed expiry:** Weekly or monthly expiry — time period fixed
- **Leverage contract:** You don't pay the full contract value, only margin

### The Truth About Leverage

> **You don't pay the full contract — you pay only MARGIN (15%-20%).**

See with Nifty calculation:

```
Nifty level:        22,000
Lot size:           50
Contract value:     22,000 × 50 = ₹11,00,000 (11 lakh)
Margin required:    ≈ ₹1,20,000 (approx)

→ Leverage ≈ 9x
```

Stock example: stock trading at ₹200 → 1 quantity = ₹200. In futures the lot size is fixed; normally 5x to 8x leverage is available. If 5x leverage → you get ₹20,000 worth of goods for ₹4,000!

**Future = a leverage contract that you can trade on a future date.**

### Why Index Gets Max Leverage

Notice — index futures give ~9x leverage, stocks only 4-5x. Why?

An index is like a portfolio of stocks. All stocks can't become zero in one night. Over time good stocks keep coming in, bad stocks keep getting removed. That's why index = low risk → highest leverage (9x).

Stocks have high risk — anything can happen with a company — that's why stocks don't get more than 4x-5x leverage.

### How Profit/Loss Happens in Futures

```
BUY Nifty futures @ 22,000
        ↓
Price goes to 22,200 → PROFIT = 200 points × lot size 50 = ₹10,000 ✅
Price drops to 21,800 → LOSS   = 200 points × lot size 50 = ₹10,000 ❌
```

Simple math — points × lot size. And this settlement happens daily as M2M (Mark to Market) — we'll see this at the end of the file.

### Long vs Short

- **Long Future:** You expect the market to RISE 📈
- **Short Future:** You expect the market to FALL 📉

Remember the farmer classic example too — the farmer has to settle his wheat/commodity at a future date. He goes SHORT in the futures market at that same rate. At the future date when he needs settlement → he gets the same price he shorted at. This hedges his risk.

## Part 2 — Options

### The Biggest Difference — Obligation vs Right

In futures (Amit-Deepti example) BOTH parties had an OBLIGATION — settle at predetermined price, predetermined date, predetermined quantity.

An option is a DYNAMIC contract:

- The buyer has a RIGHT — can use the option OR doesn't have to use it if they don't want
- No obligation on the buyer

### Bayana Analogy — The Best Explanation of Options

The easiest way to understand options:

> I gave you ₹5,000 as bayana (advance) for a ₹1 lakh house.
> - If the house appeals to me after 6 months → I'll take it (right used)
> - If it doesn't appeal → the bayana is gone, I won't take it (right dropped)
>
> **Bayana = PREMIUM. This is exactly what an option is!**

### Call and Put

**CALL OPTION:** Buyer expects price to go UP 📈 | Seller expects price to stay below strike.

Example: Nifty trading at 22,000. I purchased the 22,500 strike call option (~₹1,500 premium). Nifty expired at 23,000. Meaning my option went 500 points ITM (In The Money) ✅. Now I will exercise my option.

**PUT OPTION:** Buyer expects price to go DOWN 📉.

Example: Buy Nifty 22,000 put @ premium × lot size 50 = initial investment. Nifty came down to 21,700 → intrinsic value became ₹300 → profit zone.

An option has 3 things:

1. Premium
2. Expiration Date
3. ITM or OTM (status)

### Understanding Option Chain

Option chain is a table in which:

- In the middle there's a **strike price** column — 21800, 21900, 22000, 22100...
- On one side **CALL side**, other side **PUT side**
- Columns on both sides: Ask Quantity, Bid, Ask, **LTP**, **OI (Open Interest)**, Change in OI
- **LTP column has premiums** — premium = bayana's rate. Not the whole contract, only premium is traded
- **OI** = total how many contracts are standing at that strike

Using change in OI + OI, we'll build a Zero-to-Hero strategy ahead — detail comes later.

### Option BUYER vs SELLER — Risk Analysis

| | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| What they have | **RIGHT** — to buy at predetermined price or not | **OBLIGATION** — must deliver contract at that time |
| Risk | **Limited** (up to premium) | **Theoretically UNLIMITED** ⚠️ |

Seller's unlimited risk example: I SELL a 22,000 call. Theoretically, as much as the market goes ABOVE 22,000, THAT MUCH IS MY LOSS 😱. The buyer has no obligation — their loss is only up to premium. Seller's obligation: if Nifty goes above 22,000, I must deliver the contract.

And one interesting fact — the market closes at only ONE strike. On one side call, on one side put — both can become ZERO at max. The remaining strikes (~8 above + ~8 below) expire worthless. That's why seller's win rate is high.

Key line: **Seller's risk is higher → that's why SEBI demands HIGHER MARGIN from the seller.**

That's why option selling is more popular — high win rate, benefit from time decay, premium income. For buyers, loss is limited but break-even hurdle is high and time decay works against them.

## Part 3 — Option Concepts

### Strike Price

> **"Pre-determined price at which option is based."**

Two things exist: SPOT (underlying's current price) and OPTION's strike price.

SBI stock example: SBI trading at ₹967. Strikes in option chain come at fixed differences — in SBI the difference is ₹5:

```
...955, 960, 965, [967 spot], 970, 975, 980, 985...
```

Separate contracts trade at each specific strike. Premium (bayana/LTP) is different at each strike. These premiums are what get traded.

### ITM / ATM / OTM

- **ATM (At The Money):** The nearest strike price to the current market rate
- **ITM (In The Money):** The region with real/intrinsic value in the option
- **OTM (Out of The Money):** The outside strikes

Rules for call option:

- Spot > Strike → **ITM**
- Spot = Strike → **ATM**
- Spot < Strike → **OTM**

ATM is same for both (call/put); OTM call/put change on opposite sides. Example: Nifty trading at 26,050 → 26,000 call has ₹50 ITM (intrinsic) value.

### Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value

> **PREMIUM = INTRINSIC VALUE + TIME VALUE (extrinsic value)**

- **Intrinsic Value:** REAL value — what you actually get on exercise
- **Time Value (extrinsic):** The extra premium above real value

Example 1: My 22,000 call option, Nifty trading at 22,300. 22,300 − 22,000 = 300 points REAL value (intrinsic). Premium trading at ~400+ → the part above 300 is TIME VALUE.

Example 2 (real option chain — Nifty 9 December): Nifty trading at 26,050. 26,100 call = ₹149 premium. 26,000 call = ₹208 premium. 26,000 call has ₹50 intrinsic value (26,050 − 26,000) + rest is time value.

⚠️ Important: **OTM options have NO real value — the entire premium is TIME VALUE.**

### Option Greeks — Brief Intro

Greeks are sensitivity measures of option pricing. Brief intro now, detail later:

- **Delta:** Sensitivity to price movement — if spot moves ₹1, how much does premium move. Call delta positive, put delta negative.
- **Theta:** Time decay — how much time value decays in one day. Benefit for sellers ✅, loss for buyers ❌.
- **Vega:** Effect of volatility — if volatility moves 1% up/down, how much does option price move. High IV = expensive premium, low IV = cheap premium.
- **Gamma:** How much option price changes when delta changes. Second order effect (delta is first order).

For the keen readers: you'll find the CFA Level 1 Derivatives book on the internet — it's explained deeply throughout.

## Part 4 — Margin, M2M, Expiry and Settlement

### Margin

When trading a future contract, margin requirement exists. Example: contract value ₹18,00,000 (Nifty) → margin required ≈ ₹2.5 lakh. Margin = the initial amount you must deposit to take the whole contract.

Options have a big difference:

- **Option BUYER** pays only PREMIUM — because their risk is limited
- **Option SELLER** must deposit MARGIN — because their risk is theoretically unlimited

Bring back the Amit-Deepti example: Deepti = option buyer, Amit = option seller. Nifty option, lot size 75, premium ₹10. Deepti (buyer) pays: 10 × 75 = ₹750 only! Amit (seller) must deposit ~₹2.5 lakh margin — because he's trading on unlimited risk.

### MTM Settlement (Mark to Market)

NSE has DAILY settlement for all futures contracts. The party that loses daily → money gets DEDUCTED from their account. The party that profits → money gets ADDED to their account. That's why in futures PnL adjusts daily, you don't have to wait for expiry.

### Expiry

Weekly expiry has changed many times, so remember the latest data: **NIFTY expiry = TUESDAY | SENSEX expiry = THURSDAY.**

### Physical Settlement

Index is NOT physically settled. Stock futures and ITM options ARE physically settled — you have to deliver/receive shares. If you don't square off the trade at expiry, you'll have to arrange and deliver physical shares to the opposite party.

That's why many traders square off before expiry.

## Practical Use Cases

Theory complete. Now how all this is used:

**1. Hedging (portfolio protection with puts):** I've bought Nifty ETF worth ₹1 crore. Nifty spot is at 20,000. I go and buy a 1 year Nifty 20,000 PUT (~₹4 lakh). If Nifty falls (in any way), my puts' value becomes very high → covers portfolio loss ✅. That's why puts are used for hedging.

**2. Speculation:** I feel Nifty (currently 26,000) will reach 27,000. So I'll buy an OTM CALL at 27,000. Nifty reaches 27,000 → profit made ✅.

**3. Income generation strategies:**

- **Short Strangle:** Selling OTM call + put
- **Covered Call:** I have underlying stock + SHORT call option on it (favorite strategy!)
- **Iron Condor:** Buy 2 strikes + sell 2 strikes (4 legs)

We'll learn to build all of these in detail ahead in the course — with use cases.

## Nutshell

Derivative = whose value derives from an underlying asset (stock/index/commodity/currency). 2 major types: **Futures** (obligation on both + leverage, you only pay margin) and **Options** (right with buyer, obligation with seller). Max leverage in index futures (~9x) because index is a low-risk portfolio; stocks 4-5x. P&L is simple: points × lot size, daily M2M settlement. Option buyer = right (only premium); option seller = obligation (margin + unlimited risk → SEBI kept higher margin). Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value; only time value in OTM. Greeks: Delta (price sensitivity), Theta (time decay — seller's friend), Vega (volatility), Gamma (delta of delta). Use cases: hedging (protective puts), speculation (OTM calls), income strategies (short strangle, covered call, iron condor).

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Option buying is not easy money.** Yes risk is limited (up to premium), but time decay is your enemy and break-even hurdle is high. That's why sellers have higher win rate.
- ❌ **Full money isn't needed in futures.** Contract value ₹11 lakh, you give only margin (~15-20%) — that's where leverage comes from. And leverage is a double-edged sword — profit 9x, loss also 9x.
- ❌ **Everything doesn't settle in cash at expiry.** Index is cash-settled, but stock futures and ITM options are physically settled — you have to arrange shares. That's why learn to square off before expiry.
