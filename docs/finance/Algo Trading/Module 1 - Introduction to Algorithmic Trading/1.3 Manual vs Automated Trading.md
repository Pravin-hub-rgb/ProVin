# 1.3 Manual vs Automated Trading

In the previous file you saw that the market has already shifted from emotional trading to rule-based trading — 60%+ of FNO volumes are automated. Now let's understand the core of that shift: what exactly is the difference between manual trading and automated trading. Don't just memorize the definition; understand how both work, where they get stuck, and which one is right for you. Because this entire course's foundation rests on one line: **moving from emotional trading to rule-based trading.**

## What is Manual Trading

**Manual trading = human decision-making.** You analyze the chart, you take the decision, you decide entry/exit/stop loss/target. Every step of the trade is in your hands — under your mind and emotions.

Step by step, in manual trading you do all of this yourself:

1. Analyze the chart
2. Decide — take the entry or not
3. Decide stop loss (SL) and take profit yourself
4. Manage the trade
5. Take the exit yourself

And here's where the real problem comes. You have a strategy — that's not the problem. The problem is that while trading in the market, the real game comes down to these 4 things:

- **Fear**
- **Greed**
- **Overthinking**
- **Emotional decisions**

Because of these emotions, the strategy gets ignored. Rules aren't followed — you decide at the moment by looking at the chart. Everything happens according to your mood, not according to the rules.

This sounds like a small thing, but its effect is the biggest. See how it happens:

- Strategy says "SL is at 116" → you think *"let me push the SL a bit further"* → you shift the SL against the rule
- Confirmation hasn't come yet → you think *"this looks like a breakout, let me enter"* → FOMO entry without confirmation
- No plan today → but your mind says *"I have to make money today"* → greed-based trading, without a plan

If you go into the market with this mindset, you can never beat the market out of fear, and you can't even get the right profit/loss ratio.

So remember one line — **manual trading means YOU vs YOU.** You don't fight the market; you fight yourself — your own anger and emotions.

### Limitations of Manual Trading

Apart from emotions, manual trading has its own practical limitations:

- **Limited charts** — you can only monitor so many charts at a time. How much can one person watch?
- **Subscription costs** — you need separate subscriptions for multiple charts/data.
- **Full-time availability** — even with small capital, you must stay available full time with the screen.
- **Mentally exhausting** — every decision costs brain and emotion. It's a full-time job with no break.
- **Hard to maintain discipline** — even after building a strategy you break the rules, which makes the whole setup useless and can cause losses.

## What is Automated Trading

**Automated trading = rule-based trading.** Entry, stop loss, target — everything is defined in a set of rules beforehand, and the code executes trades following those rules. The human's role is only to build the system and monitor it.

How it works, step by step:

1. Your strategy is converted into rules (code is written)
2. The code continuously watches the market
3. When conditions match (like a breakout), the code places the order on its own
4. SL/target are also set automatically per the rules

Rules are flexible — any strategy can be used (breakout, reversal), indicator-based logic (EMA, RSI, volume), or your own custom conditions.

And the qualities of an automated system are exactly its strengths:

- **Precision** — works exactly per the rules
- **Always follows rules** — never breaks a rule
- **Never tires** — doesn't get tired
- **Non-emotional** — zero fear, zero greed
- **Mood-independent** — code runs no matter what your mood is today

## The Difference Point by Point

Now that both are understood, let's look at both side by side, point by point.

**Decision-making:** In manual, your mind decides — which depends on mood. In automated, your written rule decides — which is fixed.

**Speed:** A human takes seconds-minutes to look at the chart, think, fill the order form. Code takes milliseconds. In a fast-moving market, this difference alone decides profit/loss.

**Emotions:** Fear/greed is involved in every trade in manual. In automated, emotions have no role — only whether the condition is true or false.

**Scalability:** A human can track only 2-4 charts. With an automated system you can trade on multiple stocks/strategies at once.

**Monitoring:** In manual you can't leave the screen from entry to exit. In automated, code watches the market 24×7 — you're free.

**Execution & Slippage:** Manual orders have delay; by then the price changes — this is called slippage. In options this is a huge problem because premium moves fast. Automated execution is instant.

**Consistency:** A human's decision changes every day — confident today, scared tomorrow. An algorithm's decision never changes — same input, same output.

In one line, the whole comparison: **manual trading is effort-based, automated trading is system-based.** In manual you have to do everything yourself — analysis, entry, SL, monitoring, exit. In automated, once the setup is built and the strategy runs, you do nothing — just monitor.

## A Real Example — Same Trade, Two Ways

Now let's see theory on a small example. The strategy was a simple line breakout — "if this line breaks out, take an entry."

**In manual way:** breakout seen on chart → went to broker's platform → punched the order manually. Three steps, three manual efforts.

**In automated way:** that same strategy was defined in code → system ran → code connected to broker → condition hit → code placed the order → Order ID came (order successfully placed) → position checked → trade was running → then exited.

Both trades completed — but in the automated one, manual effort was zero. That's the only difference. And this small example shows how orders get placed through code.

## 5 Important Things About Automation

Automation sounds exciting, but get these clear first:

**1. Automation is not a magic machine.** Just writing code and forgetting won't make money. It's a tool that executes your strategy — the strategy itself must be good.

**2. Backtesting is still necessary.** You'll have to test the strategy on historical data. Backtesting is what verifies whether your logic works or not.

**3. You'll have to build the logic yourself.** You can use pre-built logic/templates, but ultimately the logic must be your own — because those rules must be yours.

**4. Automation is an amplifier.** If the logic is good, automation makes money. If the logic is bad, automation won't make money. Everything depends on your logic — that's why the FIRST step is having a good strategy. Automation is just a multiplier.

**5. Signal-generating tools ≠ auto-trading.** Some tools only generate signals — they do NOT automatically buy/sell. When a breakout happens the tool gives you an alert, but doesn't place the order — you have to place the order yourself. Signal tool = alert giver. Automated system = order placer.

## Nutshell

In manual trading you are the trader — decisions, emotions, monitoring, execution, all on you. In automated trading your rules in the form of code trade the market — without emotion, without fatigue, without mood. Automation amplifies the strategy, it doesn't replace it.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Automated trading is not a guaranteed profit machine.** It just executes your strategy. If the strategy is bad, automation will also give bad results — fast.
- ❌ **"Wrote the code, now forget it" is not how it works.** You still need to monitor the system — check logs, handle errors, review strategy when market conditions change.
- ❌ **Signal tools are not automated trading.** An alert-giving tool doesn't place orders. For order placement you need a broker API connection — we'll talk about that in the next topic.
