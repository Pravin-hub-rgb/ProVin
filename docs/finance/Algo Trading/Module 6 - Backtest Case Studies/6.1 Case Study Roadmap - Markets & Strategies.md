## Jargon Decoder — Options Names

Quick decoder for names in roadmap:

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATM** | Strike = closest to current price |
| **OTM** | "Currently useless" options (cheap) — call strike ABOVE price, put BELOW |
| **Straddle** | ATM Call + ATM Put both SOLD together |
| **Short Strangle** | OTM Call + OTM Put both SOLD — wider range |
| **"920"** ⚠️ | **TIME, not strike!!** 9:20 AM = entry window after market open |

That last one is most common confusion!

People seeing "920 Strangle" think strike is 920.

NO — means 9:20 entry strategy.

(Options basics in 2.2 — premium, strikes. Detail in case-studies.)

**In Your Own Words**

1. Why exclude SMC-style strategies now?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Because they need custom code to DETECT price-structure — draw trend lines yourself, make support zones, calculate fair-value gaps. For beginners this complexity is wrong place. Indicator-strategies have ready-made signals (EMA/RSI), so focus stays on backtest-mechanics: loops, flags, PnL, metrics. After foundation strong, SMC comes with Pine Script tooling.

</details>

2. What does "920 Strangle" mean?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** TIME — 9:20 AM! After market open (9:15) there's expiry-day option-selling entry window, hence name "920." Common confusion: people think strike-price 920. Decoder rule: in option-selling context "920" is almost always morning ENTRY TIME, never strike number.

</details>

3. Why aren't OHLC candles enough for options?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Because options strategy decides on UNDERLYING price but TRADE happens on specific strikes' PREMIUMS! Need: option chain data — available strikes, their CE/PE premiums, expiry dates. Nifty's OHLC will come but "26000 CE premium" won't — that must be fetched separately. Hence "different type of data" — Module 3's APIs are gateway.

</details>

4. ORB core rule in 2 lines?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** After open, mark first 15-minute candle's HIGH and LOW = "opening range." Then: price breaks UP → BUY (bullish day expected); breaks DOWN → SELL side. Logic: opening range defines institutional activity; break of it = signal of day's direction. Full coded version next file!

</details>
