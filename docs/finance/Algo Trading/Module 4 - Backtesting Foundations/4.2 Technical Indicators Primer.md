# 4.1 Backtesting vs Forward Testing

## First Imagine a Scene

You made a trading idea:

> *"When price is like this, BUY should be done."*

Will you directly put your real money?

**NO BRO NO!** 😱

First test whether idea makes money or loses it.

And there are two ways to test:

| Phase | Data | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Backtesting** | OLD data | "What would've happened in last 5 years?" |
| **Forward Testing** | NEW data (live) | "Does it work in REAL market now?" |

Both necessary. Skipping even one = disaster ready.

## An Interesting Truth — Your Mind Deceives You

Think how many days of market-moves can you remember?

```text
Last 15-30 days?        Yes remember
Last 6 months?          A bit
Last 2-4 years?         Forgot completely 😅
```

But market CYCLES are 2-4 years long!

Meaning your mind, seeing only small part, says

"bro this strategy is great!" — while whole picture could be different.

So rule:

> To confirm edge, backtest on **2-4 YEARS of data.**
> Don't decide on recent memory alone.

## Wait — What Happened So Far?

```text
✅ Idea must be tested, not put money directly
✅ Backtesting = test on old data (free + fast)
✅ Forward testing = test on new data (realistic)
✅ Mind remembers short-term → take 2-4 years data
```

Now first phase in detail.

## What is Backtesting

> **Backtesting = running your strategy on OLD data to see how it performs.**

Understand with example:

```text
Rule: "Buy when Super Trend GREEN, Sell when RED"

(SuperTrend = an indicator that makes line with price —
 green = going up, red = going down. Full detail in next file!)

Steps:
1. Load old data (OHLCV candles)
2. Write Python code that checks rule on each candle
3. Run on 3-5 YEARS of data
4. See result: profit made? → edge exists! ✅
```

### What All Backtesting Reveals

| Benefit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Edge confirm** | Profitable over 5 years? Not luck, something in idea |
| **Parameters check** | SuperTrend setting changed? Better result? |
| **Risk understanding** | Not just profit — HOW MUCH pain came with it |
| **Save time** | Bad strategy caught EARLY |
| **Long-term behavior** | Normal to stay in loss whole year or not |

That last point is underrated —

some strategies give loss for entire months, then recover in one jump.

Whether this is NORMAL or problem — backtest tells.

## Three Things for Good Backtest

```text
1. GOOD DATA     → correct prices, correct timeframe
2. PROPER CODE   → rules applied correctly on each candle
3. REALISTIC     → include real-world costs (explained ahead!)
```

Even one of three weak = can't trust results.

## ⚠️ Backtesting's 6 TRAPS

Now this file's most important section.

Backtest isn't perfect — 6 hidden traps:

### Trap 1: Overfitting (Curve Fitting)

Fitting strategy so perfectly on old data that it becomes "night story" of only that data.

```text
Example:
Nifty went only UP after 2020 crash.
You saw and made: "only BUY strategy!"
→ Perfect in past. Future? Unknown. 😬
```

This trap so big there's a whole file for it (5.4)!

### Trap 2: Survivorship Bias

Using only TODAY's stocks data —

companies that shut down (delisted) removed.

Real life: 100 companies, 10 broke. Only 90's data = falsely good results.

### Trap 3: Data Mining

Tried thousands of combinations until some successful combo found.

That success wasn't skill — coincidence!

Like millions play lottery, someone wins — but that's not "strategy."

### Trap 4: Execution Difference

Backtest says: *"bought at price 100."*

Real market: *"you gave order, by then price became 100.50"* ← this is **slippage**.

Small difference each trade.

### Trap 5: Ignoring Transaction Costs 💀

**Most people die HERE.**

See calculation:

```text
500 trades × per-trade cost
= up to 150% of entire capital as cost!
```

Meaning strategy showing +50% in backtest...

after costs −100%! All money went to costs.

That's why every serious backtest includes brokerage + slippage.

### Trap 6: Look-Ahead Bias

Using information in code that WASN'T AVAILABLE at that time.

```text
Wrong example:
Seeing candle's CLOSE, then trading in SAME candle.
(Close is known only AFTER candle completes!)
```

Peeking future = cheating yourself. Results become fantasy.

## One More Problem — Stop Loss Wick

Backtest code often checks stop-loss on candle's CLOSE.

But in real market price goes to **wick** (up-down finger) —

and there your SL hit, while close showed something else!

```text
Candle: High 105 | Close 102
Your SL: 104
Backtest says: "SL safe, close is 102" ✅
REALITY: Price WENT to 105 — SL HIT! ❌
```

In options, roughly 5% trades are where wick went so fast chart didn't even show tick!

Learning to model this realistically comes later. For now just know problem exists.

## Wait — Understood Backtest Limitations?

```text
✅ Overfitting — perfect fit on past ≠ works in future
✅ Survivorship — don't hide delisted companies' data
✅ Data mining — winning lottery isn't strategy
✅ Slippage — real execution differs slightly
✅ Costs — 500 trades × cost = disaster!
✅ Look-ahead — trading by peeking future = cheating
```

That's why after backtest, another test needed...

## Forward Testing — Real World Test

> **Forward testing = testing strategy on NEW data that's happening now.**

Backtest PASSED? Great! But no guarantee —

market keeps changing, what worked in past may not work in future.

### Things Only Forward Test Shows

| Factor | Backtest | Forward |
|---|---|---|
| Real spreads | ❌ | ✅ |
| Live slippage | ❌ | ✅ |
| Latency (speed delays) | ❌ | ✅ |
| Sudden volatility shocks | ❌ | ✅ |
| **YOUR HEART — how you feel in drawdown** | ❌ | ✅ |

That last point biggest:

−40% in backtest is just NUMBER.

−40% in live account **ruins sleep.** 😰

## Two Ways of Forward Testing

| Way | What | Good/Bad |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper Trading** | Live market + VIRTUAL money | Real conditions, zero risk; but emotions not tested (own money not invested!) |
| **Walk Forward** | Data in two parts: optimize first, TEST second | Regime-crossing proof; bit complex |

Walk-forward basic idea:

```text
Data: [2021-2022] [2023-2024]
       ↑ optimize     ↑ TEST (this data NOT used in optimization!)
```

If strategy works on unseen data too = ROBUST.

Full mechanics in Module 5 — for now grab concept.

## 15-20% Deviation Rule

Forward test numbers will differ slightly from backtest. How much difference is normal?

```text
15-20% deviation   → NORMAL ✅ healthy strategy
50%+ deviation     → RED FLAG 🚩 investigate!
```

Small difference comes from slippage/costs. Big difference means assumptions were wrong.

## Nutshell

Two testing phases: **backtest** (old data, free/fast — edge confirm) → **forward test** (new data — reality confirm). Mind remembers only recent so take 2-4 years data. 6 traps: overfitting, survivorship bias, data-mining, slippage, transaction costs (500 trades = 150%!), look-ahead bias (+ wick-stop problem). Paper trading = virtual money; walk-forward = train/test split repeat. 15-20% deviation healthy. Both tests pass → then live.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Profitable backtest ≠ guaranteed future profit.** Backtest only says "worked once." Market changes — hence forward test MANDATORY.
- ❌ **Forward testing ≠ live trading.** In paper trading no real money at risk — it's rehearsal. Emotions also not tested (virtual loss doesn't hurt).
- ❌ **One perfect backtest ≠ done forever.** Market keeps evolving — even good strategy needs periodic re-check. "Tested, forgot" not allowed.

# 4.2 Technical Indicators Primer

## What is an Indicator?

Raw candle data only gives numbers:

```text
"RELIANCE close: 2456.30"
```

Seeing this, no DECISION happens!

Decision needs context:

```text
Is 2456 good or bad?
Above average or below?
Moving fast or slow?
```

> **Indicator = calculation extracted from raw data that gives CONTEXT.**

Cricket analogy: don't just see runs —

see average, strike rate. Those are batsman's "indicators"!

## Remember Three Families First

| Family | Question It Solves | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Trend** | Which way market going? | EMA, SuperTrend |
| **Momentum** | How FAST? | RSI, MACD |
| **Volatility** | How much MOVING? | Bollinger, ATR |

Now all popular indicators one by one.

## 1. SMA — Simple Moving Average

Simplest. **Simple average of last N candles' close.**

Example — 5-day SMA:

```text
Closes: 18, 17, 16, 18, 17

(18 + 17 + 16 + 18 + 17) ÷ 5 = 17.2
```

New candle came? Window slides forward:

old number out, new in, then average.

**What it tells:** current price 19, SMA 17.2 —

price above its average = hint of uptrend.

**Problem:** old candles get same weight as new.

Yesterday's news and 5-day-old news equal?!

In trading FRESH info matters more.

Solution? EMA.

## 2. EMA — Exponential Moving Average

EMA is also average... but gives **MORE weight to NEW candles.**

Formula (recursive — each value built on previous):

```text
EMA_today = α × Close_today + (1 − α) × EMA_yesterday

where α (weight) = 2 ÷ (N + 1)
```

9-period EMA's alpha: `2 ÷ (9+1) = 0.2`

Meaning: **today's price brings 20% weight**, yesterday's EMA keeps 80%

(in which all past history is compressed).

### ⚠️ Most Famous Mistake — Catch Now

People write:

> *"EMA = average of last N candles"* ← **THIS IS WRONG!**

That's SMA! True EMA is recursive (formula above).

Why matters?

```text
You calculated signals with manual-average
Library calculated with recursive-EMA
→ NUMBERS WON'T MATCH
→ Crosses on different days
→ Different trades → Different results → CONFUSION 💥
```

So whenever "EMA" said, recursive one is meant.

### SMA vs EMA In One Line

```text
SMA = all candles EQUALLY important
EMA = NEW candles more important ✅ (this is what trading needs!)
```

## 3. Crossover — Indicator's Signal

Now how does TRADE SIGNAL come from EMA?

**Put two EMAs: fast (9) and slow (21).**

```text
Fast EMA crosses ABOVE Slow EMA → BUY signal 🟢
Fast EMA crosses BELOW Slow EMA → SELL signal 🔴
```

Analogy: two runners in race.

Fast runner overtaking slow = "speed is up now" = bullish!

This **EMA crossover** is world's most famous beginner strategy —

and our FIRST backtest (4.4!) will be on this.

### Event vs State — One Precision Point

```text
STATE:  currently fast > slow
        (could've crossed anytime)

EVENT:  JUST NOW crossed!
        previous candle: fast ≤ slow was
        this candle: fast > slow became ✨
```

Understand difference:

State-based entry can take new order every candle.

Event-based only once on CROSSING candle.

In code event detected like this:

```python
crossed_up = (fast_today > slow_today) and (fast_yesterday <= slow_yesterday)
```

Check both candles — yesterday no cross + today yes = EVENT!

## Wait — What Happened So Far?

```text
✅ SMA = simple average (all equal)
✅ EMA = weighted average (new more) — α = 2/(N+1)
✅ Crossover = fast/slow EMA overtake
✅ EVENT ≠ STATE — crossing moment vs position
```

Now momentum family...

## 4. RSI — Momentum's Speedometer

> **RSI = 0 to 100 number telling buying/selling PRESSURE.**

Like car speedometer — how fast going, in one glance:

```text
RSI > 70  → OVERBOUGHT  (bought too fast — fatigue possible)
RSI < 30  → OVERSOLD    (fell too much — bounce possible)
```

Inner math (just for intuition):

```text
In last ~14 candles:
how much UP move happened vs how much DOWN?

UP more → buyers strong → RSI high
DOWN more → sellers strong → RSI low
```

Formula: `RSI = 100 − 100/(1+RS)` where `RS = avg_gain ÷ avg_loss`

RATNA not needed — library will calculate (next file).

**Grab intuition:** scoreboard of buying vs selling pressure.

## 5. VWAP — Everyone's Average Purchase Rate

> **VWAP = Volume-Weighted Average Price** — at what AVERAGE rate did people buy throughout the day?

Simple average counts all candles equal.

VWAP says: *"where MORE volume happened, that price matters more!"*

```text
Where big players bought most
= their reference level
= price reacts there
```

Use case (intraday):

```text
Price ABOVE VWAP   → buyers control 🟢
Price BELOW VWAP   → sellers control 🔴
```

Institutions compare their executions with VWAP —

that's why price pauses/reverses near VWAP.

⚠️ VWAP **resets every morning** —

so it's intraday indicator, not used in swing trading.

## 6. MACD — Gap Between Two Trends

Three things combo (all EMA-family):

```text
MACD Line   = 12-EMA − 26-EMA     (fast trend minus slow)
Signal Line = MACD's 9-EMA        (its average)
Histogram   = MACD − Signal        (gap between both)
```

Intuition:

```text
MACD Line > 0    → fast EMA above → uptrend
Gap INCREASING   → momentum getting strong 🔥
Gap DECREASING   → momentum weakening 📉
```

Signals:

```text
MACD crosses Signal UPWARD   → bullish
MACD crosses Signal DOWNWARD → bearish
```

Basically packaged version of EMA-crossover with extra smoothing.

## 7. Bollinger Bands — Volatility's Boundaries

One SMA with **±2 standard deviation** lines above/below:

```text
Upper Band = SMA + 2 × StdDev
Middle     = SMA (20-period)
Lower Band = SMA − 2 × StdDev
```

Standard deviation (remember 2.5 file — data's spread) here is

**volatility measure:**

```text
Volatility increased → bands SPREAD 🌅
Volatility decreased → bands SQUEEZED ← "SQUEEZE" (warning of big move!)
```

Logic: statistics says data stays ~95% time within ±2SD.

```text
Price touches upper band → statistically EXPENSIVE
Price touches lower band → statistically CHEAP
```

## 8. ATR — Volatility's Thermometer

> **ATR = average RUPEE movement per candle.**

Simple example:

```text
Reliance moves ~₹15 daily   → ATR ≈ 15
MRF moves ~₹500 daily      → ATR ≈ 500
```

**Use case #1: Stop-Loss placement!**

Fixed ₹10 SL:

```text
On Reliance?  Fine ✅
On MRF?       Hits in noise! 😵
```

ATR-based SL: `SL = entry − 2 × ATR`

→ AUTOMATIC correct distance for every stock!

This is risk management's favourite tool.

**ATR formula (True Range):**

```text
Biggest among:
1. Today High − Today Low
2. |Today High − Yesterday Close|
3. |Today Low − Yesterday Close|

Then 14-candle average of these = ATR
```

(Other two handle gaps — yesterday close 100, today open 105?

That GAP is also volatility!)

## 9. SuperTrend — Traffic Light Line

That indicator from 4.1 example!

> **SuperTrend = line stuck to price that flips GREEN/RED** (using ATR).

Conceptual work:

```text
1. Make base line from Price ± (multiplier × ATR)
2. Price above base → line stays BELOW, GREEN 🟢 (uptrend)
3. Price falls below → line flips UP, RED 🔴 (downtrend)
```

Signals like traffic light:

```text
Buy when GREEN
Sell when RED
```

Benefit: follows trend, less whipsaw in sideways (because of ATR filter), visually crystal clear.

Exact calculation is technical — use library in code.

Intuition is enough now.

| SMA | Trend | Simple average — baseline |

## Nutshell

Indicators = CONTEXT derived from raw data. Three families: **Trend** (SMA/EMA/SuperTrend), **Momentum** (RSI/MACD), **Volatility** (Bollinger/ATR). Critical points: EMA is recursive (α=2/(N+1)) — saying "N's average" is wrong! Crossover EVENT ≠ STATE. ATR = hero of stop-loss placement (auto-adjust per stock). VWAP intraday-only. Every indicator is a lens — none alone perfect.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Indicator ≠ prediction machine.** RSI 70 doesn't mean "will definitely fall" — just probability hint. In strong trends RSI can stay 70+ for weeks while price keeps rising.
- ❌ **5 indicators ≠ 5 confirmations!** All made from same PRICE — seeing same thing through 5 lenses = 1 confirmation × 5. Mistaking redundancy for diversity is classic trap.
- ❌ **EMA and SMA not interchangeable.** Same window, different values → signals on different days → backtest results different. Whatever indicator written in strategy, test on SAME.

**In Your Own Words**

1. EMA vs SMA? What does α=2/(N+1) do?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** SMA = last N closes' equal-weight average. EMA = recursive: EMA_today = α×Close + (1−α)×EMA_yesterday — new candles heavy-weighted, reacts fast to fresh moves. α = 2/(N+1): on 9-EMA α=0.2 means today's close 20%, yesterday's EMA 80%. Classic mistake: "N candles' average" = SMA, not EMA! Manual-average vs library-EMA values won't match → signals shift → results differ.

</details>

2. Crossover EVENT vs STATE?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** State = currently fast > slow (position). Event = JUST crossed — yesterday fast ≤ slow WAS and today > became (moment). State-based entry can re-enter every bullish candle; event-based only once on crossing candle. Detection: (fast_today > slow_today) AND (fast_yesterday <= slow_yesterday) — must check both candles.

</details>

3. Why is ATR-based stop loss better than fixed-rupee?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Every stock's volatility differs — fixed ₹10 SL hits volatile stock in noise, unnecessarily loose for calm stock. ATR = average per-candle rupee move, so SL = entry − 2×ATR auto-adjusts for each instrument: volatile gets room, calm gets tight stop. Position-sizing also becomes predictable (risk-per-trade consistent).

</details>

4. What does Bollinger squeeze tell?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Bands = SMA ± 2 standard deviations; SD is volatility measure. Bands squeezed = volatility historically LOW = market compressed. Volatility is mean-reverting — low followed by expansion. Hence squeeze is early warning of "big move coming" for breakout traders (direction decided by band-break). Squeeze = calm before pressure cooker's whistle!

</details>

5. Why VWAP intraday-only?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Because VWAP calculates from day's START and RESETS daily — its meaning is "today's session average purchase rate." Yesterday's VWAP isn't relevant today (new session, new reference). Hence benchmark for intraday traders/institutions; swing/positional trading uses moving averages (which are continuous) instead.

</details>
