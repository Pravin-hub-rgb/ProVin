# 4.1 Backtesting vs Forward Testing

## First Imagine a Scene

You made a trading idea:

> *"When price is like this, BUY should be done."*

Will you directly put your real money?

**NO BRO NO!** 😱

First test whether idea makes money or loses it.

And there are two ways to test:

| Phase | Data | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Backtesting** | OLD data | "What would've happened in last 5 years?" |
| **Forward Testing** | NEW data (live) | "Does it work in REAL market now?" |

Both necessary. Skipping even one = disaster ready.

## An Interesting Truth — Your Mind Deceives You

Think how many days of market-moves can you remember?

```text
Last 15-30 days?        Yes remember
Last 6 months?          A bit
Last 2-4 years?         Forgot completely 😅
```

But market CYCLES are 2-4 years long!

Meaning your mind, seeing only small part, says

"bro this strategy is great!" — while whole picture could be different.

So rule:

> To confirm edge, backtest on **2-4 YEARS of data.**
> Don't decide on recent memory alone.

## Wait — What Happened So Far?

```text
✅ Idea must be tested, not put money directly
✅ Backtesting = test on old data (free + fast)
✅ Forward testing = test on new data (realistic)
✅ Mind remembers short-term → take 2-4 years data
```

Now first phase in detail.

## What is Backtesting

> **Backtesting = running your strategy on OLD data to see how it performs.**

Understand with example:

```text
Rule: "Buy when Super Trend GREEN, Sell when RED"

(SuperTrend = an indicator that makes line with price —
 green = going up, red = going down. Full detail in next file!)

Steps:
1. Load old data (OHLCV candles)
2. Write Python code that checks rule on each candle
3. Run on 3-5 YEARS of data
4. See result: profit made? → edge exists! ✅
```

### What All Backtesting Reveals

| Benefit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Edge confirm** | Profitable over 5 years? Not luck, something in idea |
| **Parameters check** | SuperTrend setting changed? Better result? |
| **Risk understanding** | Not just profit — HOW MUCH pain came with it |
| **Save time** | Bad strategy caught EARLY |
| **Long-term behavior** | Normal to stay in loss whole year or not |

That last point is underrated —

some strategies give loss for entire months, then recover in one jump.

Whether this is NORMAL or problem — backtest tells.

## Three Things for Good Backtest

```text
1. GOOD DATA     → correct prices, correct timeframe
2. PROPER CODE   → rules applied correctly on each candle
3. REALISTIC     → include real-world costs (explained ahead!)
```

Even one of three weak = can't trust results.

## ⚠️ Backtesting's 6 TRAPS

Now this file's most important section.

Backtest isn't perfect — 6 hidden traps:

### Trap 1: Overfitting (Curve Fitting)

Fitting strategy so perfectly on old data that it becomes "night story" of only that data.

```text
Example:
Nifty went only UP after 2020 crash.
You saw and made: "only BUY strategy!"
→ Perfect in past. Future? Unknown. 😬
```

This trap so big there's a whole file for it (5.4)!

### Trap 2: Survivorship Bias

Using only TODAY's stocks data —

companies that shut down (delisted) removed.

Real life: 100 companies, 10 broke. Only 90's data = falsely good results.

### Trap 3: Data Mining

Tried thousands of combinations until some successful combo found.

That success wasn't skill — coincidence!

Like millions play lottery, someone wins — but that's not "strategy."

### Trap 4: Execution Difference

Backtest says: *"bought at price 100."*

Real market: *"you gave order, by then price became 100.50"* ← this is **slippage**.

Small difference each trade.

### Trap 5: Ignoring Transaction Costs 💀

**Most people die HERE.**

See calculation:

```text
500 trades × per-trade cost
= up to 150% of entire capital as cost!
```

Meaning strategy showing +50% in backtest...

after costs −100%! All money went to costs.

That's why every serious backtest includes brokerage + slippage.

### Trap 6: Look-Ahead Bias

Using information in code that WASN'T AVAILABLE at that time.

```text
Wrong example:
Seeing candle's CLOSE, then trading in SAME candle.
(Close is known only AFTER candle completes!)
```

Peeking future = cheating yourself. Results become fantasy.

## One More Problem — Stop Loss Wick

Backtest code often checks stop-loss on candle's CLOSE.

But in real market price goes to **wick** (up-down finger) —

and there your SL hit, while close showed something else!

```text
Candle: High 105 | Close 102
Your SL: 104
Backtest says: "SL safe, close is 102" ✅
REALITY: Price WENT to 105 — SL HIT! ❌
```

In options, roughly 5% trades are where wick went so fast chart didn't even show tick!

Learning to model this realistically comes later. For now just know problem exists.

## Wait — Understood Backtest Limitations?

```text
✅ Overfitting — perfect fit on past ≠ works in future
✅ Survivorship — don't hide delisted companies' data
✅ Data mining — winning lottery isn't strategy
✅ Slippage — real execution differs slightly
✅ Costs — 500 trades = 150%!
✅ Look-ahead — trading by peeking future = cheating
```

That's why after backtest, another test needed...

## Forward Testing — Real World Test

> **Forward testing = testing strategy on NEW data that's happening now.**

Backtest PASSED? Great! But no guarantee —

market keeps changing, what worked in past may not work in future.

### Things Only Forward Test Shows

| Factor | Backtest | Forward |
|---|---|---|
| Real spreads | ❌ | ✅ |
| Live slippage | ❌ | ✅ |
| Latency (speed delays) | ❌ | ✅ |
| Sudden volatility shocks | ❌ | ✅ |
| **YOUR HEART — how you feel in drawdown** | ❌ | ✅ |

That last point biggest:

−40% in backtest is just NUMBER.

−40% in live account **ruins sleep.** 😰

## Two Ways of Forward Testing

| Way | What | Good/Bad |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper Trading** | Live market + VIRTUAL money | Real conditions, zero risk; but emotions not tested (own money not invested!) |
| **Walk Forward** | Data in two parts: optimize first, TEST second | Regime-crossing proof; bit complex |

Walk-forward basic idea:

```text
Data: [2021-2022] [2023-2024]
       ↑ optimize     ↑ TEST (this data NOT used in optimization!)
```

If strategy works on unseen data too = ROBUST.

Full mechanics in Module 5 — for now grab concept.

## 15-20% Deviation Rule

Forward test numbers will differ slightly from backtest. How much difference is normal?

```text
15-20% deviation   → NORMAL ✅ healthy strategy
50%+ deviation     → RED FLAG 🚩 investigate!
```

Small difference comes from slippage/costs. Big difference means assumptions were wrong.

## Nutshell

Two testing phases: **backtest** (old data, free/fast — edge confirm) → **forward test** (new data — reality confirm). Mind remembers only recent so take 2-4 years data. 6 traps: overfitting, survivorship bias, data-mining, slippage, transaction costs (500 trades = 150%!), look-ahead bias (+ wick-stop problem). Paper trading = virtual money; walk-forward = train/test split repeat. 15-20% deviation healthy. Both tests pass → then live.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Profitable backtest ≠ guaranteed future profit.** Backtest only says "worked once." Market changes — hence forward test MANDATORY.
- ❌ **Forward testing ≠ live trading.** In paper trading no real money at risk — it's rehearsal. Emotions also not tested (virtual loss doesn't hurt).
- ❌ **One perfect backtest ≠ done forever.** Market keeps evolving — even good strategy needs periodic re-check. "Tested, forgot" not allowed.

**In Your Own Words**

1. Core difference between backtest and forward test?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Backtest runs strategy on old data — fast, free, theoretical ("what if 5 years ago?"). Forward test on new/unseen data — slow but realistic ("what's happening in live conditions now?"). Backtest CONFIRMS edge; forward test confirms REAL-WORLD survival. Both ask different questions: "ever worked?" vs "still works?" Both must pass.

</details>

2. Transaction costs ignored — disaster with numbers?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Every trade has brokerage+taxes+slippage. Example: 500 trades × ~0.3% per-trade cost ≈ 150% of traded capital total cost! Meaning +50% gross-profit strategy can become net −100%. High-frequency strategies hurt most because more trades = more cost compounding. Hence cost-modeling mandatory in backtest — otherwise fantasy results.

</details>

3. What is look-ahead bias? Example?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Using information that wasn't available at decision time. Example: seeing candle's CLOSE then entering in SAME candle — close is known only AFTER candle completes, live entry impossible! Or hardcoding last year's events into strategy using today's knowledge. Fix: use only information that was available at that time — trade in next candle after close known.

</details>

4. Why can't paper trading test emotions?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Because real money isn't at risk. Virtual ₹50k loss = just number down, heart normal. Same loss in real account = sleepless, panic-exits, revenge trading. Psychology is trading's hidden component that activates only on own money — paper trading can't measure it. Hence paper-pass ≠ ready-for-live; small real capital still necessary step.

</details>

5. Walk-forward basic idea?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Data in two parts: optimize parameters on first part (training), VALIDATE on second (testing — this data NOT used in optimization!). Repeat across multiple slices. If performance reasonable on every unseen-slice = strategy robust, not curve-fit. This is structural cure for overfitting — full detail in Module 5.

</details>
