> *Same strategy can be highly profitable on one time frame, and completely unprofitable on another.*

# 5.2 Selecting Time Frame: Intraday vs Swing

## What is Time Frame?

**Time frame = granularity of price data** used for signal generation, stop-loss calculation, position sizing, exits.

Simple words: OHLC data in **what time bucket** collected. 1-min, 3-min, 5-min candles — or daily, weekly. Whatever TF chosen, that becomes your candle, your chart, your backtest (Module 4's entire engine ran on this OHLC data — in 4.4 we took 5-min Reliance).

## Small TF = Chain of More Problems

TF directly affects these:

| What Affected | Why |
|---|---|
| **Trade frequency** | Smaller TF → more signals → more trades |
| **Transaction cost** | More trades → more brokerage/taxes |
| **Slippage** | Entry-exit even on small moves → slippage's relative weight increases |
| **Drawdown** | Noise trades increase DD |
| **Capital requirement** | More positions, more margin churn |

**Simple rule:** smaller TF, more trades → cost + slippage + capital + psychological execution risk — all up.

Most dangerous version: **strategy edge + wrong TF = failure.** Example: trend-following system has genuine edge, but put on 1-minute — so many trades in a year that costs and noise completely **wash out** edge. Strategy wasn't bad, TF was wrong. This mistake isn't caught in autopsy because people blame strategy.

## Intraday Trading

> **Intraday = position open + square off same day**, before market close — **no overnight risk.**

Common TFs: **1, 3, 5, 15 minute.**

Traits: high trade frequency, low holding period, sensitive to slippage/cost, needs fast execution. One bitter truth: **surviving in intraday is hardest.** Positional system can stay profitable long-term — intraday system staying profitable long-term is very rare talent.

### TF-wise Use Cases

| Time Frame | Where Used |
|---|---|
| **1–3 min** | Scalping, high-frequency, mean reversion, order-flow strategies |
| **5–15 min** | Quick feedback — result in 5–45 min, learning cycle fast |
| **15 min** | Slower, fewer trades, **stronger signals** — breakouts & trend continuation |

**ORB special mention:** Mark first 15-min candle's high-low; whichever side breaks is day's trend. (We'll code this strategy fully in Module 6 backtest!)

### Intraday: Pros & Cons

✅ Advantages:
- **No overnight risk** — protection from gap-up/gap-down (Nifty history has 10% circuit 3 times — overnight gaps real danger!)
- **Capital free daily** — position zero every evening
- **Suitable for option selling**
- **Fast learning cycle** — immediate feedback

❌ Disadvantages:
- **High brokerage** (more trades)
- **Emotionally intense**
- **Strict risk rules** needed
- **Direct impact of news spikes**

One trade-off intraday avoids: overnight positions hedge → profit less, don't hedge → risk more. In intraday this dilemma doesn't exist — position closed by evening.

## Swing Trading

> **Swing = hold position for one swing** — multiple days to weeks, overnight carry. (Long-term investor different — holds years.)

Common TFs: **30-min, hourly, daily, weekly.**

Use cases:

| TF | What's Good |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Most common swing TF — low noise, strong trend ID. **Trend following + daily = killer combo** 🔑 |
| **Mean reversion / Breakout** | Valid on swing TFs too |
| **Weekly** | Long-term trend confirmation, positional holds |

### Swing: Pros & Cons

✅ Advantages: very low transaction cost, cleaner signals, less mental pressure, easier execution, better risk-reward. Best fit for those not mentally super-fast.

❌ Disadvantages — and solutions:

| Disadvantage | Solution |
|---|---|
| Overnight gap risk | Every instrument has **circuit limit** — size position according to it so worst-gap doesn't blow account (2.4's sizing logic!) |
| Large stop losses | Mentally accept overnight swings — don't put SL in daily noise |
| Higher capital requirement | Drawdown sharp, recovery takes days — keep capital cushion |

## How to Choose Right Time Frame

**Step 1 — Backtest (mandatory).** Whichever TF gives good AND reliable results (reliable in future too, not just past fit) — take that.

**Step 2 — Textbook mapping (by strategy type):**

| Strategy Type | Recommended TF |
|---|---|
| **Trend following** | Higher TF (daily/hourly) — go lower and edge washes |
| **Mean reversion** | Lower-to-mid TF — daily takes 6–8 months for cycle, patience fails |
| **Option selling** | Intraday — capital efficiency + controlled risk |

**Step 3 — Other factors:**

| Factor | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Capital** | Small → intraday utilization better; Large (crores) → swing (big orders infeasible intraday) |
| **Slippage sensitivity** | High slippage environment → daily TF safer |
| **Infrastructure** | Retail laptop → 5-min to daily; VPS/low-latency → 1-min possible |
| **Market condition** | High volatility → higher TF. Budget-day intraday = death; play daily |

If multiple opportunities visible, **multi-time-frame approach** also exists — larger TF decides trend, smaller gives entry (advanced technique later).

## Order for New Traders

If you're new trader, sequence is:

```text
First learn positional/swing → then understand intraday → then do intraday
```

Don't reverse order. Reason: swing/trend-following has **better probability profile** — win rate realistically shouldn't fall below 30–40% in good system, and costs less. Intraday demands professional-level execution from day one.

## Nutshell

TF = data's granularity — same strategy can give opposite result on different TF. Small TF → more trades → cost/slippage/DD/capital all rise; edge + wrong TF = washout. Intraday (1–15 min): no overnight risk, fast learning, but surviving hardest. Swing (30m–weekly): cheap, clean, mental-pressure-free; cost = gap risk (managed via circuit-based sizing) and capital. Selection: backtest reliability → strategy-type mapping (trend=higher, mean-rev=lower, opt-selling=intraday) → capital/slippage/infra/volatility factors. New → positional first.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Time frame ≠ chart setting, it's strategy's DNA.** Indicator settings, SL distance, holding period — all TF-dependent. Changing TF = making new strategy.
- ❌ **Intraday ≠ "more money faster."** More trades ≠ more profit — more COSTS and more chances of error. Frequency isn't proof of profit.
- ❌ **Swing ≠ "set and forget."** Overnight gaps, circuit risks, weekend news — holding isn't being passive. Sizing and SL planning needed beforehand.

3. Solution to swing trading's overnight gap risk?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** Circuit-limit based position sizing. Every stock/index has max daily move limit (e.g., 10%/20% circuits). Assume worst-case gap — if market opens at limit tomorrow, how much loss — calculate and size so that loss stays within acceptable % of account. 2.4's risk-per-trade principle applies: even in gap scenario total hit stays within acceptable %.

</details>

5. How does TF decision change on high-volatility day like budget-day?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

**Answer:** High volatility = intraday danger. Wild whipsaws hit stop-losses repeatedly, spreads widen, slippage extreme — small TF's math breaks. Hence such days prefer higher TF (daily) or avoid trading. General rule inverse too: calm markets make intraday viable. Market condition is an input factor in TF decision, not constant.
