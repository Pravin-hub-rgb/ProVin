# 5.1 Strategy Idea Generation & Hypothesis Building

## Guess Work vs Hypothesis-Driven Testing

Two ways to trade:

| Guess Work | Hypothesis-Driven |
|---|---|
| "Feels like going up now" | "If X happens, historically Y happened" |
| Sometimes profit | Measurable, repeatable logic |
| Different reasoning every trade | Same rules every time |
| Can't explain results | Accept/reject from data |

Consistent trading only possible **rules-based** — and before rules need a **clear trading idea + testable hypothesis**. This is one of course's most important lines:

> *"It starts with a clear trading idea and a testable hypothesis."*

Behind every good strategy is a hypothesis — capturing market behavior that happens **on average**.

## What is Idea Generation?

**Idea generation = process of finding market inefficiencies.**

Inefficiency means — where price discrepancy or price difference exists, opportunity exists. Classic example: **option and spot pricing difference** — sometimes option premium doesn't perfectly reflect spot move; this gap can become tradeable opportunity.

Full flow:

```text
Find market inefficiency
    ↓
Build idea to capture it
    ↓
Convert idea to hypothesis
    ↓
Design rules on hypothesis → Test → Deploy
```

One necessary filter: good idea must be **quantifiable**. "Market behaving weirdly" — that's a feel, not idea. "When VIX rises 10% in one day..." — that's in numbers, idea can form. Same rule at portfolio level: what can be measured over time, only that can become system.

## Five Sources of Ideas

Where do ideas come from? Five major sources:

| # | Source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market observation** | Watching sudden moves — noticing price move patterns |
| 2 | **Price action patterns** | Candlestick formations that repeat |
| 3 | **Technical indicators** | What learned in 4.2 — Super Trend gives buy signal; take that observation and test as idea |
| 4 | **Events & volatility** | Market behavior around events (budget, results, Fed) |
| 5 | **Academic papers** 🔑 | Most tested research papers on trend following — seriously underrated source |

Two notes:

- Don't stick to one source — **take ideas from multiple sources**, test all.
- Observations from sources must be made **actionable** — observation ≠ trading idea.

And yes — indicator source is special for you now. People heard RSI/Bollinger names before; you know their math. When SuperTrend flip seen, ask: *"is there edge in N candles after flip?"* — boom, hypothesis ready.

## Trading = Art + Science

> **"Trading is art plus science."**

| Part | What | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| 🎨 Art | Idea generation | Creative — thinking ideas from observations |
| 🔬 Science | Hypothesis building & testing | Statistical — formulate, measure, accept/reject |

First half creative (comes with practice), second half pure science (can be learned). Commerce background or engineering — both halves can develop. Science part you're already learning; art part improves with market-time.

## How to Judge Good Idea?

Judge every idea on four questions — don't just sit and discuss, evaluate with framework:

1. **Can system be built?** — Can this idea be converted into trading strategy/system?
2. **Is it quantifiable?** — Can it be measured in numbers over time? If not, system impossible.
3. **Is it consistent?** — Works repeatedly or only sometimes?
4. **Is it actionable?** — Can concrete buy/sell rules be made from it?

Only ideas passing these 4 filters become high-quality trading ideas.

## How Hypothesis Looks — Examples

### Example 1 — Expiry-Day Behaviour

On expiry day one thing happens with near-certainty: **ATM call and ATM put premium decay behaviour** (ATM = strike closest to current price). Grab that certainty and build system around it — add re-entry rules, stop loss rules. Interesting hypothesis becomes full system.

(Options basics in 2.2 — premium, strikes. Expiry-day strategies detail in case studies.)

### Example 2 — Event-Based Volatility

```text
"If VIX rises 10% in a single day,
then intraday volatility is higher for next 3 days."
```

What is VIX? **Volatility Index** — market's "fear meter." Like temperature tells how hot, VIX tells how scared market is (derived from option prices).

Now see how solid this hypothesis is:
- **Specific** — exact condition (VIX +10% in one day)
- **Measurable** — volatility can be measured in numbers
- **Testable** — take historical data, backtest; ✅ accept ❌ reject

Compare: *"market will go up"* — vague, unmeasurable, untestable. Not hypothesis, just guess.

## Final Workflow — From Idea to Backtest

```text
Observation
    ↓
Convert to Hypothesis
    ↓
Define Signal Rules
    ↓
Backtest          ← Module 4's engine!
    ↓
Accept ✅ / Reject ❌
```

This is a loop — if rejected, don't be sad, bring new idea, again. Rejection isn't failure; **cheap failure**. One wrong idea rejected on paper, real money saved.

Progression always this:

```text
idea generate → rules → backtest → trade → live trade
```

Test at small level, then scale — safe path. Next classes will detail each step — time frames, entry/exit rules, optimization.

## Nutshell

Algo trading doesn't start with coding — starts with **clear idea + testable hypothesis**. Idea generation = finding inefficiencies; sources: observation, price action, indicators, events, academic papers. Trading = art (ideas) + science (testing). 4 filters to judge idea: can-system-be-built?, quantifiable?, consistent?, actionable? Good hypothesis is specific + measurable + testable ("VIX +10% → next 3 days higher vol"), not vague statements. Workflow loop: observe → hypothesize → rules → backtest → accept/reject. Guessing stopped, testing started.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Hypothesis ≠ prediction.** "After X, Y happens" isn't future guarantee — statement of historical pattern. Data can reject it; that's the value.
- ❌ **More ideas ≠ more profit.** Collecting 50 ideas without testing is collection, not research. One tested-rejected idea more valuable than untested-50.
- ❌ **Coding is not first step.** Code is tool — comes after hypothesis. Writing code first usually means automating guess-work, not strategy.
