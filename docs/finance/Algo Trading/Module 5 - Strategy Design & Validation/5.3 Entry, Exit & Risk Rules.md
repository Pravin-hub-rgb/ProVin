Remember 4.4's strategy? We deliberately left without target and without stop loss — "advanced features coming later." They've arrived. Now design strategy's three core organs: **when to enter, when to exit, how much risk**. And first line itself changes every new trader's worldview:

> *"Entry, exit, and risk rules are core foundation of every profitable algo system — profitability is decided by RULES, not by INDICATORS."*

# 5.3 Entry, Exit & Risk Rules

## Rules > Indicators

Common misconception: "got good indicator, money will come." Wrong. Good idea + strong indicators + **bad entry/exit rules** = failed system. Because ultimately what decides — *when entered, when exited, how much lost, how much gained* — all rules; indicators are just inputs.

So rule design matters more than indicator selection. Let's see both sides.

## Entry Rules

> **Entry rule = how and when to enter trade.**

Four qualities of good entry rule:

| Quality | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Objective / clear** | Zero guessing — two people reading same rule take same trade |
| **Repeatable across ALL data** | Works only on last 6 months → wrong. Should work on 2 years data |
| **Codable & backtestable** | Can be written in Python, tested |
| **Logical** | Based on market idea (trend/reversion/volatility) — not just curve-fit |

### Five Families of Entry Rules

| Type | Examples | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Trend Following** | Range breakout, MA breakout, Higher Highs/Lows, RSI strength | Trend running, go with it |
| **Mean Reversion** | RSI < 30 buy, Bollinger lower band touch, gap fill, red candle + reversal candle | Gone too far, now back to average |
| **Volatility-Based** | ATR explosion, volatility contraction → breakout | Silence will break, then move comes |
| **Time-Based** | ORB (first 15-min range breakout), VWAP bounce, closing-basis trades | Behavior of specific time windows in day |
| **Indicator/Pattern** | EMA crossover (4.4!), MACD crossover | Classic signal combos |

One concrete example to understand: **RSI < 25 → BUY.** This is **mean reversion** family because market going down and you're saying "will reverse from here" — going counter-direction to market. If rule were "RSI 60+ cross → BUY" it'd be trend-following. Same indicator, opposite philosophy — hence *rule family* matters, not indicator.

## Exit Rules — The Forgotten 70%

⚠️ Biggest psychological trap: **traders obsess over entry, ignore exit.** Reality: **exit determines ~70% of performance.** No matter how good entry, wrong exit turns profit into loss.

Exit's job: **secure profit, reduce losses, control drawdown.**

### Four Types of Exit

| Type | What It Does |
|---|---|
| **Profit-target exit** | Book at fixed profit level |
| **Stop-loss exit** | Cut at fixed loss level |
| **Indicator-based exit** | EMA cross reverse / MACD reverse → exit (4.4 strategy's exit was this!) |
| **Trailing stop-loss** | SL slides up with price — profit lock |

### Trailing Stop-Loss in One Minute

Buy @ 100, initial SL @ 95. Price reaches 110 → shift SL to 105. Price 120 → SL 115. Now no matter where price falls, you have **minimum +15 locked**. If uptrend continues, profit continues too. Simple concept, powerful result.

### Full or Partial Exit?

- **Full exit:** close entire position at once
- **Partial:** book half, trail rest — let winner run, some profit in pocket too

Which is better? Not opinion — **data analysis** decides. Run both versions in backtest.

🔑 **Golden design principle:** *Best exit rules are SIMPLE.* More complicated the rule, lower probability of making profit (inversely proportionate). Complexity brings fragility, not flexibility.

## Case Study — SuperTrend Backtest

Let's make everything practical. System on SuperTrend (read in 4.2 — ATR-based flipping trend line):

```text
Rule (only 2 lines):
Buy when SuperTrend turns GREEN
Sell/Exit when SuperTrend turns RED
```

Started on Nifty daily chart — too few trades, switched to **1-hour**. Backtest results:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total trades | **183** |
| Winning trades | 47 |
| Losing trades | 136 |
| Win accuracy | **25.68%** 😱 |
| Net P&L | **+5,917 points** ✅ |

Pause and look. **75% trades LOST!** Yet net result solidly positive. How?

This is **typical trend-following profile**: many small losses (tight SL), but when trend caught, **big wins** — full momentum ride. 136 losses × small < 47 wins × big. 4.6's asymmetry lesson in reverse direction: win rate can be low when payoff ratio high.

And hypothesis clear: different regions/investors buy/sell at their own levels → **momentum** builds in moves. Trend following surviving strategy for 100+ years — hasn't faded.

### What Rule Design Taught

- **Repeatable** — same rule, entire data
- **Clear** — *"5-year-old would understand"* this simple
- **No jargon, full logic** — hypothesis visible behind (momentum)

## Where Are Risk Rules?

Notice? No separate "risk section" created. Because risk management is already in these:

```text
Risk control =
    controlled stop-losses        (exit rule)
  + trailing exits                (exit rule)
  + position sizing               (Module 2)
```

When exits disciplined and sizing circuit/gap-aware (5.2), risk automatically managed. No need to invent exotic "risk formula" — boring fundamentals work.

## Nutshell

Rules are foundation — not indicators. Entry rule qualities: objective, repeatable across all data, codable, logical. Five families: trend, mean-reversion, volatility, time-based, pattern. Exit ≈ 70% of performance — 4 types: target, SL, indicator-based, trailing (full vs partial = data decides). Simple exits > complex exits. SuperTrend proof-of-concept: 25.68% win rate, still +5,917 points — trend following = many small losses, few big wins. Risk = SL + trailing + sizing combined. Clear, repeatable, logical — that's all needed.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **Entry rule ≠ strategy.** "RSI < 30 buy" alone incomplete — exit, sizing, timeframe together make strategy. People think sharing entry = sharing strategy.
- ❌ **Low win rate ≠ bad system.** 25.68% system was profitable. Judge number is expectancy (4.6), not win rate alone.
- ❌ **Complicated rules ≠ professional rules.** Institutional-grade systems famously boring/simple. Complexity is symptom of backtest-fitting, not quality (5.4 has full name: overfitting).
