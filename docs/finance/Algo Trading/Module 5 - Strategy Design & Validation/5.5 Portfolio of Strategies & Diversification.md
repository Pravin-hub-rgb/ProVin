# 5.5 Portfolio of Strategies & Diversification

## Bank Analogy

Think bank: ₹10,000 crore to lend. Will it give all to one person? Never — if default, everything gone. Bank spreads across thousands of people.

Now your turn: are you putting entire trading capital on **one strategy?** That strategy is the "one person" who can default.

Why default? Because market always in some **regime:**

```text
Trending / Sideways / High-Volatility / Low-Volatility
```

And here's problem:

```text
❌ Trend strategy    → fails in sideways
❌ Mean reversion    → fails in trending
```

Different strategies work in different regimes. Rely on one and when regime changes, you become spectator.

## Why Single Strategy Fails

Three reasons:

**1. Regime change.** Table above — no single approach works in all seasons.

**2. Drawdown becomes unbearable.** Here a concept most people miss — drawdown has **two aspects:**

| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Total drawdown** | How much money fell (e.g., −15%) |
| **Time in drawdown** | How much time to recover (e.g., **8 months**) |

15% drawdown with 90% return sounds amazing. But if that 15% dip stays for **8 months?** Practical question: *"Can I watch my system in drawdown for 8 months — without questioning it, without shutting it?"* 10–15 losing streaks can come, three-month loss phase possible. On single strategy this psychological load is brutal.

**3. Over-reliance = career risk.** Your entire livelihood on one logic (say "support zone 3 touch → breakout"). Logic stops working? Everything gone — not just loss, confidence too.

## Portfolio of Strategies

> *"Portfolio of strategies = set of trading systems that operate independently and contribute differently to return/risk."*

Exactly like stocks-portfolio — instead of diversifying companies, **diversify trading systems.**

Index/mutual fund analogy perfect fit: Nifty 50 = 50 companies. One sinks, 49 run. Law of large numbers gives consistent return and total-failure chance almost zero. Passive index investing stands on this philosophy.

Robust portfolio should mix:

```text
Trend following + Mean reversion + Volatility strategy
+ Intraday + Positional + Different instruments
```

Why? *No single strategy works in all market conditions.* Period.

## Diversification & Correlation

> **Diversification = spreading capital across different UNCORRELATED strategies, so one's failure doesn't destroy capital.**

Diversification dimensions:

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| Across strategies | Trend + mean-reversion + volatility |
| Across timeframes | Daily positional + intraday |
| Across regimes | Trending-friendly + sideways-friendly |
| Across instruments | Equity, commodity, crypto, fixed income |
| Across logic types | Different hypotheses |

### Correlation — Hero of This Topic

In 2.5 (Statistical Thinking) learned correlation — now becomes trading's most important word. Relationship between two strategies/equities' returns:

| Correlation | Behavior | Diversification Value |
|---|---|---|
| **+1** | Identical — one up, other up | ❌ Zero — both lose together |
| **0** | No relationship | ✅ OK |
| **−1** | Opposite — one up, other down | 🔥 Best |

Real examples:
- **Gold vs Equity:** traditionally negative-ish — equity falls, gold rises
- **Fixed income vs Equity:** in Corona crash equity −40%, bonds up — negative correlation saved balanced portfolios

Goal: **combine strategies with lower/negative correlation.** Negative rare but best.

⚠️ Trap alert: combined 5 strategies, but if **all losing at same time** — portfolio NOT diversified. Not count, check correlation.

## Strategy Menu — For Mixing

| Type | Best Regime |
|---|---|
| Trend following | Trending |
| Mean reversion | Sideways |
| Option selling (straddle/strangle) | Low volatility |
| Breakout / long straddle | High volatility |
| Intraday (ORB/VWAP) | Daily regularity |
| Event driven | Budget day, expiry, Fed |

See pattern? **Opposite behaviors** — trend (wins in trending) + reversal (wins in sideways) + straddle (wins in low-vol). When one loses, other wins. Best diversification.

## Practical Example — Union of Two Strategies

Take two strategies, equal weights (50/50 — risk-normalized): ₹10L capital → ₹5L each.

### Strategy 1 — Trend Following (Nifty futures, daily, positional)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Capital journey (2 yr) | ₹10L → ₹14.9L |
| Total return / CAGR | 49% / **22.3%** |
| Sharpe | 0.95 |
| Max drawdown | **18.7%** |
| Trades (W/L) | 78 (30W/48L) — win rate **38.5%** |
| Avg win : avg loss | 4.6 : 1.3 (**3.5:1 ratio**) |

Sound familiar? This profile from 5.3's SuperTrend case study — **few wins, big wins** (3.5:1 payoff). Trend following wins BIG, not often.

### Strategy 2 — Options Selling Mean Reversion (Nifty, intraday, range-bound)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Capital journey (2 yr) | ₹10L → ₹16.18L |
| Total return / CAGR | ~68% / **30.2%** |
| Max drawdown | **27.5%** |
| Trades (W/L) | 460 (312W/148L) — win rate **67%** |
| Expectancy | 0.18 per trade |

Opposite personality: **many wins, small wins** — high win rate but small wins; one big loss eats entire month.

### Combined Portfolio (50/50)

| Metric | Mean Reversion | Trend Following | **Combined** |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAGR | 30% | 22.3% | **26%** |
| Max Drawdown | 27.5% | 18.7% | **12.3%** ✅ |
| Worst month | −13.8% | −9.5% | **−5.8%** ✅ |
| Return per unit risk | ~1.09 | — | **~2.1** ✅ |

See magic:

- CAGR fell slightly (best single's 30% → 26%) — small cost
- **Drawdown HALVED** (27.5% → 12.3%) — big reward
- Worst month also halved (−13.8% → −5.8%)
- Risk-adjusted return (Sharpe ~1.95) better than both singles

### Equity Curve Pattern

Most beautiful insight:

```text
Mean reversion:  smooth running + occasionally takes sudden clip
Trend following: stays flat + occasionally gives sudden jump
Portfolio:       SMOOTH STAIRCASE — each fills other's gaps
```

When mean reversion in bad month, trend following giving jump — and vice versa. Hence combined curve almost dip-free. Time-in-drawdown psychological torture (8-month question) practically disappears in portfolio.

Key lesson: individual strategy's drawdown depends on it; **portfolio use smooths it.**

## Nutshell

Single strategy dies from regime change, gives unbearable time-in-drawdown, becomes career risk. Portfolio = index of trading systems — law of large numbers on your side. Diversification dimensions: strategies, timeframes, regimes, instruments, logics. Correlation hero: +1 useless, 0 ok, −1 best; not count, check correlation. Proof: Trend (38.5% WR, big wins) + Mean Reversion (67% WR, small wins) → combined CAGR 26%, DD only 12.3%, smooth staircase equity. Small return sacrifice, massive stability gain — that's professional trading.

## What It Is NOT

- ❌ **More strategies ≠ diversified portfolio.** Five trend-following systems = one bet five times (+1 correlation). Diversification comes from uncorrelated LOGIC, not count.
- ❌ **Portfolio ≠ guaranteed profit machine.** Correlation shifts with regimes — in crisis "uncorrelated" assets can also fall together. Portfolio reduces risk, doesn't eliminate.
- ❌ **Combined CAGR lower ≠ worse.** 26% vs 30% unfair comparison when drawdown 27.5% vs 12.3%. Risk-adjusted lens (return per unit risk ~2.1x) shows real picture — and it favors portfolio.
