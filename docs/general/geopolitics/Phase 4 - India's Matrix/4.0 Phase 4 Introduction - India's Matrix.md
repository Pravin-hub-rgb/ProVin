# Phase 4 - India's Matrix

## What this phase covers, and why it is the hardest

India's foreign policy is genuinely harder to analyse than most, and the reason is worth stating before we start.

**Most countries are simple.** Look at the United States, or Japan, or Britain. They have alliances, and within those alliances they act with some consistency.

**India does not.** India has no alliance. India has to balance **China and the United States simultaneously** — two countries that are themselves rivals, both enormous, and both actively courting India.

**So India's problem is not "how do we get strong." It is "how do we stay undecided for as long as possible, without anyone deciding for us."**

That is a genuinely difficult position, and it produces the behaviour we will examine in detail: close to China on trade, cautious with China on security, close to America on defence, and defensive about America's pressure.

## The five questions this phase answers

**1. Why is India-China so difficult?** (4.1) Because they are neighbours with an unresolved war, competing for the same water, and trading with each other at a scale neither expected.

**2. Why is India-Pakistan unlike anything else?** (4.2) Because they have fought three wars, both have nuclear weapons, and the dispute is structural rather than negotiable.

**3. What does "Neighbourhood First" actually mean?** (4.3) And why is it so hard when your neighbours are smaller than you, dependent on you, and suspicious of you?

**4. How does India manage being courted by two rivals?** (4.4) What it gets from America, what it gets from Russia, and why the Ukraine vote offended the West.

**5. How does India keep the Indian Ocean open?** (4.5) The String of Pearls, the Necklace of Diamonds, and why China's answer to India's answer.

**6. How much of India's foreign policy is actually foreign policy?** (4.6) The uncomfortable question — how much of India's external behaviour is decided inside India.

## One thing to keep in mind throughout

**You will read this phase and want to be impressed.** India is genuinely a rising power, it genuinely has unusual strategic assets, and it genuinely deserves to be taken seriously.

**But you will also read it and want to be critical.** India has real weaknesses: no security guarantee, huge oil dependence, a neighbourhood it cannot fully control, and a domestic politics that sometimes produces incoherent foreign policy.

**Both reactions are honest. The skill is holding them at once.**

Because the person who only admires will miss the vulnerabilities. **And the person who only criticises will miss what is actually working**, which is quite a lot.
