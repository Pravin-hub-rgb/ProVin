The last file gave you the central idea of Phase 3: countries act on interest.

But it left a question open. If interest drives behaviour, **what are countries actually competing for?**

**There is no single definition of power.** There are several, they behave completely differently, and a country that is formidable in one can be helpless in another.

# 3.2 What Power Actually Is - Hard, Soft and Economic

## The common mistake

Most people have exactly one idea of what "power" means, and it is roughly: **the ability to make other countries do things they do not want to do.**

That is not wrong, but it is only about one kind of power, and it is the least effective kind in many situations.

**Here is the problem with thinking only about armies.** Plenty of countries have enormous armies and cannot get anything done. **And plenty of countries have small armies and get almost anything they want.**

So let us introduce the useful version. **There are three main kinds of power, plus a fourth newer one**, and they work in completely different ways.

## Power 1 — Hard power

**Hard power is military force.** It is the ability to threaten or use force.

**This is the oldest and most obvious form of power.** The United States has the largest military on Earth by a very large margin. China has the second largest, and is rapidly modernising. India, Russia, and a handful of others also have nuclear weapons.

**But here is the crucial limitation of hard power, and it is structural:**

**Hard power is expensive, it is slow, and using it has consequences.**

Three things follow from that, and they are worth holding onto:

**First, hard power works best when the stakes are high and clear.** If country A can destroy country B, A gets what it wants fairly reliably. **That is why the nuclear-armed states have never fought a full-scale war against each other** — because the consequence would be total.

**Second, hard power is blunt.** You can destroy a factory, but you cannot force a country to make a law change. **Military force is very good at stopping things and very bad at starting things.**

**Third, and most importantly: hard power is the only kind that is genuinely final.** Once used, it cannot be taken back. **A sanction can be lifted. A trade deal can be renegotiated. A missile cannot be un-fired.**

**That is why every country tries hard to avoid using it, and why most disputes are settled some other way.**

**And this is exactly why the last file matters.** Hard power is the easiest kind of power to explain through interest — you can threaten, and the other side has to comply. **Everything else is much harder.**

## Power 2 — Soft power

**Soft power is the ability to get what you want by making other countries want it.** Not by threatening, but by attracting.

**The phrase was coined by Joseph Nye, an American political scientist, in 1990.** And it is the more interesting idea of the two.

**How does it work?** Instead of saying "do this or I will punish you," soft power says "we are so attractive that you will want to do this anyway."

**And there is a crucial distinction here that people miss. Soft power is not propaganda.** Propaganda is when you try to deceive someone. **Soft power is when you are genuinely attractive and people want to associate with you.**

**The classic examples:**

**America's soft power** is Hollywood, American universities, the English language, and the appeal of the American consumer lifestyle. **Foreigners watch American films, send their children to American universities, and buy American products** — not because they were forced, but because they want to.

**Indian soft power** is more interesting because it is not the one India claims. It is: **Bollywood**, which is watched across South Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Southeast Asia. **Indian software exports**, which the world depends on. **Indian medicine** — generic drugs, which many countries buy precisely because they are reliable and cheap. **Indian classical music and dance**, which are genuinely studied worldwide.

**And Phase 4 will look at a specific and surprising case: Turkish television dramas**, which have become extraordinarily popular across the Arab world and are a very visible piece of Turkish soft power.

**Now here is the thing about soft power that makes it very different from hard power.**

**It works slowly, and it works in one direction only.**

You cannot quickly coerce a country into liking you. **And if a country dislikes you, your soft power will not overcome that — if anything, being associated with the country may damage your standing.**

**India has a specific problem here that is worth being honest about.** India's film and music industry is globally famous, but **India is not culturally close to most of the world.** Chinese cinema, Korean popular culture, and Japanese animation have wider global reach among younger audiences. **Indian soft power is real but narrower than India's economic size suggests.**

## Power 3 — Economic power

**Economic power is the ability to use money, trade, and finance to get what you want.**

**This is the most important kind of power and the one people think about least.** Here is why it is more powerful than hard power in most situations:

**It is continuous, reversible, and it does not kill anyone.**

**The main instruments are these:**

**Trade** — if you depend on somebody to buy your goods or sell you food, you have leverage.

**Finance** — the ability to freeze someone's assets, or to be the currency the world uses for transactions.

**Investment and debt** — who lends money to whom, and on what terms.

**And the single most important example is the US dollar.** The dollar is the currency in which most international trade is priced, the currency in which most commodities are sold, and the reserve currency that most countries hold. **This gives the United States enormous financial leverage** that has nothing to do with its army.

**And here is the crucial fact about economic power: it is largely invisible until it is used.**

Nobody notices sanctions working while they work. **You notice them when a foreign minister cannot travel abroad, or when a country cannot buy spare parts for its aircraft.** Economic power operates by changing what is *possible*, not what is *visible*.

## Power 4 — Sharp power

**The newest category, and the one most likely to matter in the next twenty years.**

The phrase **"sharp power"** was coined by the Canadian political scientist Wael Ghonim, and it describes power that sits between the traditional categories.

**What it means: using economic coercion, cyber attacks, and information operations to exert influence, without declaring war.**

**And the main users of sharp power today are China and Russia.** Both have used cyber operations extensively. Both have used economic leverage — for instance, in 2022 Russia cut off gas supplies to Europe, and Europe discovered how dependent it had become. Both use information operations to influence public opinion.

**Why does this category exist at all?** Because the traditional labels did not fit. **China's methods are not soft power (China is not particularly attractive to most countries), and not classic hard power (it does not invade).**

**It is something else: aggressive pressure through economics, technology and information, without the consequences of war.**

**And this is the category that will define the next phase of geopolitics, because it is cheap, deniable, and difficult to respond to.**

## How these powers combine

**Here is the crucial insight, and it is the whole point of this file.**

**A country weak in one kind of power can compensate with another. A country strong in all of them is formidable.**

**Let me use India as the example, because it is yours, and it is unusually instructive.**

| Kind of power | India's position |
|---|---|
| **Hard** | Nuclear-armed, large army, growing. But far behind the US and China militarily |
| **Soft** | Bollywood, software, generic medicine, classical arts. Real but narrower than India's size suggests |
| **Economic** | Fast growth, large market, young workforce. But dependent on importing oil |
| **Sharp** | Limited. India is a target of sharp power far more often than a user of it |

**Read that table honestly.** India is **not strong in all four**, and it does not pretend to be. **India's realistic strategy is to be strong in a couple, and to be impossible to ignore in the rest.**

**And that is a general truth.** Almost every country is strong in one or two kinds of power and weak in others. **China is strong in hard and sharp power, and depends on others' markets. The United States is strong in hard, financial and soft power. Russia is strong in hard and sharp power, and surprisingly weak economically.**

**Which brings us to the crucial idea: power is not a single score.**

There is no table anywhere that adds up hard power and soft power into one number, and **anyone who offers one is dressing up a guess.** Phase 0 said this about the four dials; it is true here too.

**What you can do instead is much more useful: for any given situation, work out which kind of power is actually relevant.** Because the answer changes completely depending on the question.

**A country with a small army and enormous financial leverage can still bankrupt another country. A country with the world's largest army and no economic leverage cannot buy anything it needs.** And a country that nobody wants to be associated with will find that its investments are unwelcome, regardless of its military strength.

## What this file is not

**Soft power is not propaganda.** Propaganda is deception. **Soft power is genuine attraction** — people choosing to associate with you because they want to, not because they were fooled. Confusing the two is one of the most common errors in this subject.

**Nor is soft power a substitute for hard power.** A country with enormous cultural appeal and a weak military has real influence, but it cannot stop anything. **Soft power can make things happen; hard power can stop things from happening.** Neither replaces the other.

**And economic power is not morally neutral.** The ability to cut off someone's access to food or medicine is a real and grave power, whatever the language used to describe it.

**Nor does "sharp power" mean anything goes.** The absence of a declared war is not the absence of consequences. The argument for sharp power is precisely that it has *fewer* consequences than war — which is also its main problem.

**And the most important limit of this whole file: influence is not the same as control.** You can be very influential in a country and still unable to make it do anything. **And a country can be enormously powerful and unable to achieve one specific thing it wants.** Phase 4 is largely about working through this for India.

## Bridge to the next file

You now have the vocabulary for power. But there is a piece of that vocabulary we have not examined: **power used together.**

Countries do not act alone. They sign treaties, form blocs, and promise to help each other. **And that is a decision with real costs, which is why some countries refuse to make it.**

India is one of the countries that has refused — deliberately, since 1949. **This file's framework explains why that choice has been so valuable, and the next file explains exactly what India gives up by staying out.**
