The last file of Phase 2 ended in 2020, with a fight in Ladakh and a note that you would have noticed something.

**Throughout Phase 2, you kept being asked one question: what did each party want?**

That was not an accident. **That is exactly what this phase formalises.**

Phase 3 is different from everything before it. Phases 0, 1 and 2 were about the world. **Phase 3 is about the rules the world runs on.** Not a country, not a map, not a date — but the *system*.

And why does the system matter? Because **once you understand the rules, most news stops surprising you.** You will read about a country doing something outrageous, and instead of asking "how could they?" you will ask "what interest made this sensible?"

That is a genuinely valuable skill, and it is the single most useful thing this course can give you.

## The five questions this phase answers

**1. Why do countries do what they do?** (3.1) The answer is almost never "because it is right." It is almost always interest. This sounds cynical and is not.

**2. What does power actually consist of?** (3.2) Not just the army. There is soft power, economic power, and a newer kind called sharp power.

**3. Why do countries join together, and what does it cost?** (3.3) Alliances, blocs, and why India refuses to join any of them.

**4. How do countries punish each other without fighting?** (3.4) Sanctions, tariffs, and trade wars — including the ones that have directly involved India.

**5. What does the world look like now?** (3.5) The Global South, multipolarity, and what India's "strategic autonomy" actually means.

## A warning before we start

**This phase will make you more cynical, and that is partly the point — but it should not make you more cynical than is useful.**

There is a real risk here. If you read enough about how countries act out of interest, you may end up concluding that everything is cynical and nothing is sincere. **That would be a mistake.**

**The honest version is this: interests explain most of what happens between countries, but not all of it, and understanding interests does not oblige you to approve of what you now understand.**

**You are allowed to know why a country did something and still think it was wrong.** In fact, understanding why is what makes it possible to disagree intelligently rather than just loudly.

**Another risk:** the moment you start explaining behaviour by interest, you can explain *anything*, including things that genuinely deserve condemnation. **So you need a check.** Phase 5 covers propaganda and how people misuse frameworks like this one. Keep that in mind now, and we will deal with it properly later.

## The bridge from Phase 2

Let us make the transition concrete, because it is easy to miss.

**In 1947, Britain signed a defence treaty with India and maintained a large military presence there.** India was a member of the Commonwealth.

**By 2020, Britain and India were signing trade and defence agreements, and the two countries had gone from near-enmity to what was called a "global partnership."**

**What changed in seventy-three years?** Not the characters — the countries are the same. **What changed was interests.**

Britain in 1947 needed India as a strategic position and a source of troops. Britain in 2020 needed India as a market and a partner in a world where China mattered. **Same actors, completely different behaviour, entirely explainable by interest.**

**That is the whole lesson of this phase, stated in one example.**
