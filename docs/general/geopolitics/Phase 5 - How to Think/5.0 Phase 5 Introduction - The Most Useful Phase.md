# Phase 5 - How to Think

## The problem this phase solves

Let me start with a trap, because it is easy to fall into and you may already be in it.

**Somewhere in the last four phases, you almost certainly found something that annoyed you.** A claim you disagreed with. An omission you noticed. A framing that felt one-sided.

**And your reaction was to discount the whole thing.**

**That reaction is completely natural and it is completely wrong.**

**Discarding a source because it got one thing wrong is like throwing away a map because one road is misdrawn. The map is still the fastest way to get somewhere.**

**The correct response is narrower and harder: trust this source *about this*, and be more careful *about that*.**

**That skill — calibrating your trust to the specific claim — is what this phase is for.**

## The three ways people get geopolitics wrong

**Before the techniques, the failures. Because you should recognise yourself.**

**Failure one: taking a side and then protecting it.**

Once you identify with a position — Indian, American, Chinese, or simply "against the government" — the information you accept starts being determined by your identity rather than by evidence. **And you will not notice this happening to you, because it will feel like being informed.**

**The test:** if you changed your position on a major issue, would you have to abandon something you like about yourself? If yes, that position has become part of your identity, and you are no longer thinking.

**Failure two: confusing confidence with correctness.**

People who are loud, certain and fluent get treated as more credible than people who are careful. **But certainty in geopolitics is nearly unrelated to accuracy.** Most confident people are confident because they have picked one side of a contested issue and no longer see the other side.

**The test:** count how many people who are publicly certain about a geopolitical question have been proven wrong in the last five years. The answer is a lot, and it includes almost everyone.

**Failure three: only consuming what you already agree with.**

This one is not about being stupid — it is about how every recommendation algorithm works. **You get shown more of what you engaged with.** So over six months you get a more and more extreme version of your own views, delivered by software optimising for your attention.

**The result is that your beliefs get sharper and more confident and no better supported.** That combination is genuinely dangerous, and it is why "I'm just reading the news" is not a defence.

## What this phase gives you

**Six files, and the practical skills in order of how much they will change how you read.**

**5.1 — Taking a headline apart.** Five questions that turn a headline into an actual understanding. **This is the single most useful file in the course** and the one you will use most often.

**5.2 — Reading both sides properly.** How to state an opposing view well enough that someone who holds it would recognise it. **And how to do that without pretending all evidence is equal.**

**5.3 — Propaganda and spin.** The specific techniques, plus the base-rate problem, which is the most important single idea in this file — because it is the one that catches people who are otherwise careful.

**5.4 — Sources and fact-checking.** Who to trust, how to check, and the research showing that most people's fact-checking instincts are wrong.

**5.5 — Your weekly analysis log.** The habit that makes all of this permanent, including the calibration trick that will make you better at prediction than almost anyone.

**And a warning that runs through all of it:**

**These skills can be used on you.** Propaganda works on people who know every technique in 5.3. **The people most confident they are immune are the most exposed, because immunity requires constant vigilance, not knowledge.**

## One thing to hold onto

**The goal of this phase is not to become cynical.** It is to become **accurate**.

There is a version of media literacy that ends in "everything is manipulation, everyone has an agenda, nothing is true." **That version is lazy and often wrong, and it lets actual misinformation through while giving you immunity-feeling.**

**The better version is more demanding: most of what you read is broadly accurate. Some of it is framed. A little of it is deliberately false. And you can usually tell which is which — if you know what to look for.**

**That is what the next six files are about.**
