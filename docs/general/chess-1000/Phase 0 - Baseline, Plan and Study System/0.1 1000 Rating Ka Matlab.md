# 0.1 What a 1000 Rating Means

## What rating is

A chess rating is a number that estimates a player's performance against opponents in recent results. It does not mean "a 1000-rated player plays the same every day"; rating is a moving estimate. Just as one exam can go well or badly, the rating goes up and down after a few games.

When you play 10+0 rapid games on Lichess, the rating updates based on results against players in that pool. So to judge your current rating you should look at your recent games, time pressure, and common mistakes. Just looking at the number and saying "I am weak" or "I am strong" is not useful feedback.

## What 10+0 means

In `10+0`, each player gets 10 minutes at the start. No extra seconds are added after each move, so saving time is also part of the game. In rapid games both decision speed and clarity matter, but losing a queen in a rush is also a practical problem.

In this time control, tactical vision is useful. A simple working definition of calculation for now is: thinking about your move and the opponent's forcing replies. In 10+0 you cannot calculate many variations on the board. A simple capture or check that you spot immediately can have more practical value than a complex theory line. Still, there is no rule to "calculate everything"; you need to identify your mistakes and reduce them.

## What a 1000 target means

A target around 1000 usually gives a beginner a baseline of practical patterns: checks, captures, knight forks, simple pins, and back-rank attacks. It does not mean "solve every position." It means scanning for an immediate problem even on a crowded board, then choosing a practical continuation. Here, a fork simply means "one move attacks two targets," a pin means "if the blocker moves, the king is exposed," and a back-rank attack means "the king is trapped on its own back rank." We will explore these patterns in detail in the tactical topics of Phase 1.

Around 1000 your moves can sometimes be strong and sometimes blunder a queen or king. The real work of rating improvement is reducing consistent weaknesses, not making every move glamorous. That is why this course's language stays focused on "mistake patterns," not on praise.

## Benefits and tradeoffs of this target

Keeping the goal as a number gives direction, and weekly review shows which practice improved results. A numeric goal is useful, but each individual game should not become the final proof of the rating. That is why the course also keeps measurable tactical and review goals.

Your focus will not go toward calculating everything on every move. Board scan, simple forcing lines, and safe developing moves come first; this saves time and makes blunder patterns visible in review. The tradeoff is that deep calculation arrives in detail in Phase 2; exhaustively calculating every position in the first phase is not realistic.

## What It Is NOT

A 1000 rating is not a guarantee, not a fixed personality test, and not the result of memorizing opening theory alone. It is a direction related to practical decision quality in the competitive context of Lichess rapid 10+0.

Do not treat 1000 as a guarantee that "everything becomes perfect after this number." Every opponent is different, and within one score range there can be many styles and strengths. So you should watch your trend, not turn someone else's number into your target.
