Just like a repeated reminder on a phone saves a task from being missed, a chess routine keeps decision habits from being missed too. `0.2 Baseline Test.md` gave you a starting picture; now you turn that picture into a feedback loop of daily routine and game log. A small, repeatable system gives a busy learner sustainable progress.

# 0.3 Daily Routine and Game Log

## Four-block daily routine (45 elapsed minutes)

Splitting 45 elapsed minutes into four focused blocks is more useful than scattered study. Each block has a clear purpose, and at the end of each block you know what to do next. This reduces the "what now?" moment after every game.

Reserve the first five minutes for a board-scan warm-up. On any position, look for the opponent's king, checks, captures, and threats; even without calculation yet, try to write one immediate observation. This habit should be used automatically in practice games.

The next ten minutes are for focused tactics. Choose today's pattern — for example a knight capture, a pin, or a back-rank idea — and solve 3–5 positions. Write a reason with each answer so you are not just guessing moves.

Then keep 25 minutes for practical games. Play one or two 10+0 games on Lichess, and if a game pushes past 25 minutes, complete that session with review the next day. In the last five minutes write a short log: result, one critical moment, reason, and one action for tomorrow.

## Why the game log matters

Just as looking only at the score in cricket does not show which shot was costly, in chess looking only at win/loss does not show which move became a pattern you will repeat in future games. The log gives your brain evidence of "I observed a specific problem," not just "I played a game." That evidence is what makes weekly changes possible.

A short entry per game is enough; you do not need an essay. In Lichess game review you can note the move number and a screenshot, but writing your decision clearly on the board is the most reusable feedback. Example entry: "On move 12 I gave the queen away for free; I did not see the opponent's rook capture; tomorrow I will check recapture before every exchange."

The log's purpose is not blame. Writing "played a wrong move" makes the next step clear, but writing "I am a weak player" does nothing. Turn every mistake into one observable action and one counter-action, for example replace "did not scan the board" with "I will read the opponent's last move before choosing my move."

## One practical log shape

Keep the log format simple so filling it daily stays possible. You do not need to write more than a minute per game, but do not leave reason and next action blank.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Result | Loss, 10+0 |
| Position or move | 12. Nf6, missed the queen's attack on the board |
| Reason | I did not check the opponent's rook capture |
| Habit | Giving a hanging piece away for free |
| Next action | Recapture and king-safety scan before every exchange |

At the end of the week, split entries into two categories: "what kept showing up" and "which action to test tomorrow." If every game is a different story, the specific gap of the current phase may not be clear yet. Turn a repeated phrase into a weekly goal.

## Benefits and tradeoffs

Following the routine daily protects against the "nothing happened today" feeling, because a 5-minute drill and a 5-minute log are also counted progress. Consistency improves tactical recognition and strengthens the decision routine in real games. The long-term benefit is that reliable decision habits matter more than a momentary rating number.

The tradeoff is that the routine can feel boring, and a detailed analysis of one game can pull you outside the 45-minute session. So instead of writing a full chapter per game, choose one critical moment. Do a deeper review once a week and keep quick logs on other days; both quality and quantity matter.

## What It Is NOT

The daily routine does not mean solving the hardest possible problems every day. It means maintaining a small, repeatable system — and increasing difficulty inside that system.

The game log is not a replacement for analysis. If you do not understand a move's reason without the log, relearn the concept from the engine or phase notes; just writing the result does not fix a tactical gap.

## Seven-day checkpoint

Try the routine fully for the next seven days: at least five logged games, written reasons for two positions, and a clear counter-action for one repeated mistake. If you miss the routine, bring the chain back the next day with a half-size session instead of blaming yourself. The target is consistency, not perfection.
