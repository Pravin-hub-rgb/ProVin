# 0.3 Quiz - Baseline and Study System
## MCQ

Q: What do starting time and increment mean in a Lichess `10+0` rapid game?
A. Each player gets 10 minutes and 1 second increment per move
B. Each player gets 10 minutes and no extra increment
C. The game ends at 0 seconds after 10 moves
D. Both players get 10 minutes and each move takes 0 seconds
> B. In `10+0`, each player gets 10 minutes at the start and no extra seconds are added after each move. So both time management and move quality matter.

---

Q: What is the primary purpose of the Phase 0 baseline test?
A. To prove you are permanently strong or weak
B. To predict a fixed rating that applies to every future game
C. To observe starting strengths and repeated gaps
D. To memorize all opening variations
> C. The baseline is a starting snapshot that shows what is currently strong and where practice is needed among checks, captures, safety, and patterns. It is not a rating forecast.

---

Q: Which is the most useful game-log entry?
A. Only the final result
B. Only the opponent's name
C. One critical moment, its reason, and the next action
D. A 500-word paragraph for every move
> C. One critical moment, its reason, and the next action turn the log into actionable feedback for future practice. Focusing only on the result does not help identify a repeated mistake.

---

Q: What is the balanced target for the 45-minute daily cadence?
A. Only 45 minutes of games, no study
B. Board scan, focused tactics, practical games, and short review/log
C. Only reading an opening book
D. 45 minutes of engine analysis and no game
> B. The 45-minute routine has a sequence of small scan, focused tactics, practical games, and review. This connects theory to real decisions and creates daily feedback.

## TrueFalse

Q: Following the 1000 rating target is a guaranteed result for every learner.
- True
- False
> False. 1000 is a direction, not a guarantee. Rating depends on opponents, consistency, time, and review quality, so the roadmap uses measurable habits and honest rating expectations.

---

Q: FEN is a compact notation that can represent a chess position and side to move.
- True
- False
> True. FEN, or Forsyth–Edwards Notation, records the board's pieces and side to move in text form. In this course it is used as the exact text record of an exercise position.

## ShortAnswer

Q: How do you make the baseline useful for future rating improvement?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** I treat the baseline not as a fixed judgment but as a starting snapshot. I note each baseline position's attempt and reason, then compare them with the game logs from the 0.3 routine. Wherever repeated safety, capture, or check gaps appear, I turn them into focused drills and review actions for the next week.

</details>

---

Q: How would you write a useful game-log entry after a losing game?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** I will choose one critical position along with the result, such as "on move 12 I gave the queen away for free." Then I will write where the opponent's recapture was available and why I missed it. The next action will be "count opponent replies before every capture," so the entry becomes a measurable habit target instead of blame.

</details>
