Just like removing a curtain brings both light and a view through the window, in chess removing a blocking piece can activate a line that was already open. `1.2 Fork, Pin aur Skewer.md` told the story of line-based attacks; now we see how a line opens, or how one move creates two threats. In this topic's exercises you will learn to verify the source move and the attack target separately.

# 1.3 Discovered and Double Attack

## Discovered attack

A **discovered attack** happens when you move a blocking piece and open a line to a target piece that already existed. "Discovered" means the attack comes not from the new piece's move, but from a potential line already open behind it. A discovered check is also possible, when the line reaches the king.

The simple analogy is a turning point in traffic: one car was blocking a lane, and removing it activates the flow of another lane. Before removing the blocker on the board, check whether a clear line exists behind it for a rook, bishop, or queen. If the line is only theoretical and the path is blocked, there is no discovered attack.

A discovered attack's benefit is that it gives the opponent less prepared choice, because the attack's source can be some distance from the move. The tradeoff is that you must track both the blocking piece and the target line; seeing one can make you miss the other.

## Double attack

A **double attack** means that after one move there is a practical threat on two different targets, such as queen and king or queen and rook. This is a broad umbrella and a fork can fall inside it, but the focus here is geometry: one move, two immediate consequences, and the opponent's limitation in defending both at once.

To understand a double attack, list the opponent's most valuable pieces on the board, then see how your move activates a common target square or common line against them. If one target can be saved by a forced response, the other can become an actual material or mate opportunity.

A queen and bishop combination can win material with a capture and protection through a double attack. The benefit is simple — the opponent does not have to solve everything in one move. The drawback is that if your capturing piece is unprotected, the opponent can capture straight back.

## The working difference

In a discovered attack your move activates a line already open. In a double attack your move creates one or more new attacks, and the targets can be different from each other. Sometimes one move can be a combined example of both, but first identify the order of attack.

Visualize this on the board before memorizing the distinction: first remove the blocker, then see the new line; or first create the new target line, then count the opponent's escape. "Line open" and "two targets" are not the same inspection question.

Here the white knight is a blocker in front of the rook on the h-file. Before a direct rook move, see where the knight can be sent to open the h-file.

**Common mistake:** Treating the position as a direct rook move and forgetting the knight that was blocking the line. Here the point is not a direct rook push but activating the hidden attack by removing the blocker.

## Exercise — queen and bishop combination

In this position, after the queen captures, the king and rook are attacked by the same queen move, and the bishop can protect the capturing piece. First find the target relationship, then check the recapture.

![Phase 1 tactic position 05](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-05.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `5rk1/5ppp/8/3Q4/2B5/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 2 minutes. Find the queen capture, name both attacked targets, and check the bishop's protection.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Qxf7+`

**Short main line:** `1. Qxf7+ Rxf7 2. Bxf7+`

**Why it works:** The queen on f7 attacks the king on g8 and the rook on f8. If the rook captures the queen, the bishop on c4 recaptures on f7 because the queen's departure clears the diagonal.

**Common mistake:** Seeing the double attack without checking the bishop's protection. A double attack is strongest when the first target is backed by a second piece.

</details>

## What It Is NOT

It is wrong to call a discovered attack "any check by a rook"; the blocker's presence and the line's activation matter. Do not treat a double attack as a full guarantee, because the opponent can block one threat and deflect the other.

Applying these patterns without verification is also not right. If the target is not defended, or your own king becomes exposed by the move, the calculated advantage does not survive.
