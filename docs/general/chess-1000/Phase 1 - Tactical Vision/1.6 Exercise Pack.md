# 1.6 Exercise Pack

## How to use the pack

This is the Phase 1 exercise pack, with six different legal positions for fork, pin, skewer, discovered attack, double attack, back-rank idea, and combination. Solve each position first; write the move and a one-sentence reason before opening the answer. The goal is not a six-out-of-six score, but comparing your first-try accuracy and explanation.

The positions get gradually harder, so give the first two 90 seconds, the next three 2 minutes, and the last one 3 minutes. If time runs out, mark the guess as "not sure" and move on; your independent attempt before seeing the engine answer is essential.

## Set 1 — forcing move and fork

The first position starts with Phase 1's simplest scan-and-fork pattern. The board has sparse pieces, so first look for the knight jump that targets king and queen together.

![Phase 1 tactic position 01](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-01.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `8/8/2k1q3/8/8/5N2/8/6K1 w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 90 seconds. Find the forcing knight move and write a legal reply, e.g. `Kd5`.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Nd4+`

**Short main line:** `1. Nd4+ Kd5 2. Nxe6`

**Why it works:** The knight attacks the king on c6 and queen on e6 simultaneously. Black cannot save both, so the king must move; one legal reply is `...Kd5`, after which the queen becomes available.

**Common mistake:** Capturing the queen without checking the king's legal replies. Tactical priority had to come before material desire.

</details>

## Set 2 — pin and skewer

The second pattern removes a blocker along a line to expose the king. In this position you must verify the direct consequence of the check after the capture, so do not postpone the escape calculation by one move.

![Phase 1 tactic position 02](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-02.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `Rrk5/8/2N5/8/8/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 90 seconds. Identify the blocker, capture it, and name the check that follows.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Rxb8+`

**Short main line:** `1. Rxb8+ Kd7`

**Why it works:** The black rook on b8 is pinned between White's rook and the king on c8. Removing it exposes the king directly, and the white knight on c6 protects b8, so the black king cannot recapture.

**Common mistake:** Trying to capture a different piece while ignoring the adjacent pinned blocker. The shortest legal capture is often the tactical point, but the recapture square must be protected.

</details>

## Set 3 — skewer along a file

The third position puts rook, queen, and king on one file. This time you must attack the king after removing the valuable piece from the line, so note the capture target and the blocker's role separately.

![Phase 1 tactic position 03](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-03.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `3k4/8/3q4/8/8/8/8/3R3K w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 2 minutes. Remove the queen from the king's line and calculate one legal king reply.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Rxd6+`

**Short main line:** `1. Rxd6+ Kc7`

**Why it works:** The queen on d6 blocks the rook's file. After the rook captures it, the rook checks the king on d8, which is the skewer pattern.

**Common mistake:** Treating the queen as the only target and forgetting that removing it is what makes the king attack real. Capture order matters here.

</details>

## Set 4 — discovered check

The fourth position exercises a hidden line. The knight is currently a blocker in front of the rook, so search for a knight move that removes the blocker rather than a direct rook move.

![Phase 1 tactic position 04](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-04.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `7k/8/8/8/8/8/7N/4K2R w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 2 minutes. Find a move that opens the rook's h-file and gives check.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Nf3+`

**Short main line:** `1. Nf3+ Kg8`

**Why it works:** The knight leaves h2 and uncovers the rook on h1 against the king on h8. The rook has not moved, so this is a discovered check.

**Common mistake:** Checking the rook's line but not recognizing the knight as the blocker. So ask "who is hiding behind this piece?" in the scan.

</details>

## Set 5 — protected double attack

In the fifth position, two targets matter after the queen capture, and the bishop protects the capturing piece. Looking at this position you must also verify the capturing piece's safety.

![Phase 1 tactic position 05](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-05.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `5rk1/5ppp/8/3Q4/2B5/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 2 minutes. Find the capture, name the two attacked targets, and check the recapture line.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `Qxf7+`

**Short main line:** `1. Qxf7+ Rxf7 2. Bxf7+`

**Why it works:** The queen attacks the king on g8 and rook on f8. If the rook takes the queen, the bishop on c4 recaptures on f7 along the cleared diagonal.

**Common mistake:** Playing the capture and assuming the queen is safe because it gives check. Protection by the bishop is what makes the combination work.

</details>

## Set 6 — deflection and mate

The sixth position is the phase's hardest finish. Here you must force the defender off its defensive line, and after the final check count the king's escape squares.

![Phase 1 tactic position 06](/diagrams/chess/phase-1/phase-1-tactic-06.svg)

**Side to move:** White  
**FEN:** `5r1k/7p/6Q1/4N3/8/8/8/4K1R1 w - - 0 1`  
**Time limit/task:** 3 minutes. Find the forcing combination, verify Black's reply, and list the king's escape squares after the finish.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and review</summary>

**Best move:** `1. Nf7+!`

**Short main line:** `1. Nf7+ Rxf7 2. Qg8#`

**Why it works:** The knight check forces the black rook from f8 to f7, pulling the defender off the back rank. The queen then delivers an adjacent check on g8, protected by the g1 rook; g7 is covered and h7 is occupied, so the king has no escape or block.

**Common mistake:** Jumping to the queen mate before the knight check, or forgetting to verify the rook's forced capture. Move order creates the mate.

</details>

## Phase readiness checkpoint

After completing the pack, note your score and reason quality. Four or more correct first attempts is a useful readiness signal, but not a guarantee; a lower score means drilling the weak pattern from the exact lesson again, not abandoning the course.

After the six positions, also check this: can you explain the continuation after the opponent's strongest response in at least two answers? If yes, Phase 1's tactical vocabulary is becoming solid. If not, identify your missed pattern, do a focused revision of that file, and run the next mixed set.

## What It Is NOT

The exercise pack's score is not a rating forecast, and six-out-of-six pressure can stop you from enjoying tactics. Its job is to measure pattern recall and decision quality, not to judge identity.

Saying "I could have done this" after seeing the answer is not enough. Write the reason and find a similar position, because transfer only happens when a pattern can be recognized on a new board.
