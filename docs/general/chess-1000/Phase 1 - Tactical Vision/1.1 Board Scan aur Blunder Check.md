# 1.1 Board Scan and Blunder Check

## The simple scan order

In chess you cannot calculate every move, especially in 10+0. So start with a fixed order: first the opponent's king, then checks, then captures, then the opponent's threats, and finally your normal plan. Think of this order like a traffic officer — first whether any accident is happening, then normal traffic flow.

"Check" means your current move attacks the opponent's king. "Capture" means some unprotected valuable piece can be taken immediately. "Threat" means the opponent will make a forcing move after you, even if there is no move on the board right now; seeing this in the future is part of the scan.

The scan should not run longer than 5–10 seconds. First note the immediate problem, then compare candidate moves, then choose a move. This will feel a bit mechanical, but on crowded positions it gives simple checks a chance by pausing emotion.

## What a blunder is

A **blunder** is a move that gives the opponent a clear tactical or material advantage, such as a chance to capture your piece without protection. It is not always a synonym for "wrong move," but for practical rapid rating it is a mistake worth identifying immediately.

One of the most common beginner blunders is the feeling "I moved my piece, the opponent has no force." The real test is: after the move, what is the opponent's immediate reply? If the reply is a clean capture, check, or fork, scan again before the move.

This does not mean re-reading piece movements; the focus shifts to the board after your move. A tactical move's simple job is to make the opponent's king, queen, rook, or an important square vulnerable. We will not teach detailed fork and skewer patterns yet; the next topic covers them one by one.

## Practical scan

In a rapid game you do not need full calculation on every move, but you do need a repeatable scan. It will feel mechanical at first; later you will spot the "king, check, capture, threat" sequence in a glance without an engine. We are learning to reliably notice simple wins now; deeper calculation for complex positions comes in Phase 2.

The tradeoff of the scan is that you will not immediately classify some quiet, non-tactical positions. So do not mistake every move for a forced move; after the scan, safe development or a pawn move can also be a legitimate choice. Use the tactical eye like practice with new traffic rules — not every position needs to be treated as a threat.

## What It Is NOT

The board scan does not replace an engine. It only shows you the immediate possibilities that make calculation worth starting; deep lines need more tools.

You cannot define a blunder by material count alone. Sometimes a positional loss matters too, and sometimes there is compensation after a material capture. This course prioritizes stopping clean, avoidable losses first.
