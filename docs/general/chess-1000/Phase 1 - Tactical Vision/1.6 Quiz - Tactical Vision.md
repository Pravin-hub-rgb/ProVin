# 1.6 Quiz - Tactical Vision
## MCQ

Q: What is the practical order for finding forcing ideas in a board scan?
A. Pawn structure, opening theory, king, captures
B. King, checks, captures, threats, normal plan
C. Material count, engine score, king, pawns
D. Only pieces on the back rank
> B. Looking at the king and immediate forcing moves first reduces the chance of missing the important problem on a crowded board. The normal plan and broader position are evaluated after that.

---

Q: What is the difference between an absolute pin and a relative pin?
A. In an absolute pin the piece cannot make a king-exposing move; in a relative pin such a move can be legal, but there is a material risk from capturing the protected piece
B. In both, every move of the pinned piece is illegal
C. In both, you can always capture the pinned piece for free
D. A relative pin immediately puts the king in check
> A. An absolutely pinned piece cannot make a king-exposing move. In a relative pin such a move can be legal, but the attacker can capture the protected piece and win material, so material loss is common.

---

Q: What is the basic skewer pattern?
A. Removing a valuable piece to expose the king along a line
B. Capturing the king with its own pawn
C. Exchanging every queen with a bishop
D. Parking a rook on h8
> A. In a skewer, removing a valuable piece, usually the queen, along a line gives a direct attack on the king. Counting the king's response afterward is necessary.

---

Q: What is the practical meaning of deflection?
A. Exchanging every queen immediately
B. Forcing the defending piece away from its original role
C. Permanently emptying the center of the board
D. Completely avoiding the opening
> B. Deflection lures or forces the defender away from a job like blocking a line or guarding a square. That gap creates the second attack.

## TrueFalse

Q: In a discovered attack, a line can activate far away without the attacking piece itself moving, as long as the blocker moves.
- True
- False
> True. One blocking piece moves and a rook, bishop, or queen line already present reaches the target. The attacking piece itself does not move.

---

Q: If a check is given and the king has one escape square, it is checkmate.
- True
- False
> False. Checkmate requires no legal option among king escape, blocking the check, or capturing the checking piece. If an escape square remains, the move is a check, not mate.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Explain the difference between a fork and a skewer in your own words.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** A fork attacks two different valuable targets, such as king and queen, in one move, so the opponent can save one target. A skewer first captures a valuable blocker, such as a queen, along a line and then gives a direct attack on the king behind it. A fork creates targets; a skewer removes a blocker to expose the king.

</details>

---

Q: Explain the forcing idea and mate verification in `Nf7+ Rxf7 2. Qg8#`.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** `Nf7+` forces Black's king to respond, and the black rook must capture on f7. That moves the defender off the back rank. `Qg8#` gives adjacent check; the g1 rook protects the queen, g7 is covered, h7 is occupied, and the rook has no block. So the king can neither capture, escape, nor block.

</details>
