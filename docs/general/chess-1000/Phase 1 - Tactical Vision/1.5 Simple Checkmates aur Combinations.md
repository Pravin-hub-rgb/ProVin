# 1.5 Simple Checkmates and Combinations

## Check and checkmate

**Check** means the current move has attacked the opponent's king. Not every check is mate, because the opponent can move the king, block the check, or capture the checking piece. **Checkmate** happens when all three of those response options are closed after the check.

Think of it on the board like a door being checked while no alternate exit exists. You must not only see "it is hitting the king"; you must count capture, block, and king escape. This is why sparse back-rank positions and simple endgame mates are spotted quickly even by strong players.

## What a combination is

A **combination** is a move sequence in which one tactic enables another and the final aim is a material win, checkmate, or major advantage. Its moves are usually forcing, but sometimes a quiet setup move comes first — for example bringing a piece to a better square — so that the later forcing sequence becomes possible. A single capture can also be a tactic, but in a combination move order and the opponent's replies matter. Simple early-1000 combinations are usually 2–4 moves, so you do not need to calculate long forced lines in advance.

Just like in a recipe you first fry the onion to build flavor and then assemble the final dish, in a combination the first move sets up the board and the last move finishes. The benefit of this analogy is that it teaches you to look at every move as serving the goal, not as random.

## How to calculate a simple combination

First find the candidate final move: is there checkmate, a major capture, or a strong threat? Then work backward from that final move to the forcing move required to achieve it. Now calculate the opponent's strongest legal response, and after each response verify your planned continuation.

In this process you must compare the candidate move against the opponent's strongest legal response, not the easiest one. When multiple moves reach the same goal, the simplest line is often practical; this course values clarity of calculation more than engine-style deep lines.

**Best move:** `1. Nf7+!`

**Short main line:** `1. Nf7+ Rxf7 2. Qg8#`

**Why it works:** The knight check makes the rook capture on f7, so the rook leaves its back-rank defensive job. The queen then lands on g8 with adjacent check; the g1 rook protects the queen, g7 is covered, h7 is occupied, and the rook cannot block a check from an adjacent queen.

**Common mistake:** Treating the final queen move as the whole combination and missing the forced rook capture. A combination's confidence comes not from the final pattern but from verifying the opponent's strongest response.

</details>

## What It Is NOT

Check and checkmate are not the same thing. Calling every check a mate is a common beginner mistake; the opponent may have a legal escape, block, or capture.

A combination is not a guaranteed win either. If the opponent has multiple replies and you did not calculate them, an attractive-looking line can fail in a practical game.
