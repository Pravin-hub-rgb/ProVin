# 3.5 Quiz - Opening Fundamentals and Repertoire
## MCQ

Q: What is the most practical definition of an opening plan?
A. Memorizing the exact reply to every opponent move
B. A goal and connected explanation of its practical moves, which can adjust on unexpected replies
C. Blindly copying the engine's first recommendation
D. Pushing a pawn after the first three opening moves
> B. A plan gives direction and purpose; the move sequence is not fixed. So when the opponent plays an unexpected reply, the plan can adjust with fundamental principles.

---

Q: What is the main opening purpose of `O-O`?
A. Making the king permanently attack-free
B. Developing all central pawns
C. Taking the king to a safer area and setting up future rook coordination
D. Attacking the opponent's right side
> C. Castling takes the king to a safer area and gives future coordination with the rook. It is not a permanent shield, so weak squares and open files must still be monitored later.

---

Q: What is the benefit of a deliberately small repertoire?
A. Loading expert-level theory on every opening
B. Saving time and decision clarity by understanding fewer choices
C. Beating every opponent with one move sequence
D. Skipping the opening
> B. Fewer, well-understood choices help familiar decisions and plan-following in 10+0. The result still depends on the opponent and execution.

---

Q: Why can a move like `2. Nf3` after `1. e4` be considered practical?
A. Because it is always engine-best
B. Because it activates the knight and supports future castling and development
C. Because a pawn move is always better
D. Because it immediately captures a bishop
> B. Knight development supports center plan and king-safety setup together. This is not a universal guarantee of that particular line, but the board-level reason is clear and repeatable.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: A small repertoire means you repeat only one line and ignore principles on an unexpected opponent move.
- True
- False
> False. A small repertoire rehearses limited choices, but on an unexpected reply it is necessary to return to principles of development, center, king safety, and structure.

---

Q: The practical use of pawn structure is only memorizing labels.
- True
- False
> False. The use of structure is to evaluate weak squares, open files, future targets, and king-safety consequences on the board. Labels are only the start of recognition, not the decision.

Q: How would you use development, center control, king safety, and pawn structure in a practical opening loop?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> On every move I ask which undeveloped piece can become active, which central square will be influenced, and what the king's next safe square is. After a pawn move I also note weak squares, open files, and possible opponent targets. This loop evaluates both immediate and future purpose of the move, without needing to memorize long theory.

</details>
