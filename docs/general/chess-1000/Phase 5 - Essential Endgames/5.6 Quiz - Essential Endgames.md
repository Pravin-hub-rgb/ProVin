# 5.6 Quiz - Essential Endgames
## MCQ

Q: What is the most correct beginner-level meaning of king activity in the endgame?
A. Bringing the king to every possible move's center
B. Bringing the king to a useful support square with pawn, weak square, or mating route
C. Always keeping the king in the corner, because it is safe there
D. Giving the king to the queen
> B. King activity means useful square, support, and participation, whether with a pawn, on a weak square, or in mate. Not just visual activity, the board's practical purpose matters.

---

Q: How can opposition be understood at beginner level?
A. A spatial constraint between kings that controls move order and approach
B. Only the pawn giving check
C. Bringing the queen to the center
D. The king always exchanging with the rook
> A. Opposition restricts kings' spacing and approach. It means the side to move must make an additional decision; opposition can change through a pawn move.

---

Q: What is the practical purpose of a key square?
A. Bringing the king near the pawn's useful support zone and promotion direction
B. Always keeping the pawn under the black king
C. Always parking the rook on the seventh rank
D. Always giving check to avoid stalemate
> A. A key square is a destination where the king supports the pawn and gives future promotion direction. The zone must be visualized on useful squares ahead of the pawn, and the defending king must also be considered.

---

Q: What does cut off mean in a rook-pawn ending?
A. Bringing the rook in front of the defending king to block the promotion route
B. Immediately removing the pawn from the board
C. Exchanging the queen with the rook
D. Sending the king to the opponent's opposite corner
> A. Cut off keeps the defending king away from the pawn through rook or king and reduces escape choices. Rook's active rank and king's distance must be seen together on the board.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: Stalemate is an automatic win, because the side to move has no legal move.
- True
- False
> False. Stalemate is a draw, not a win. The side to move's king must not be in check, but must also have no legal move.

---

Q: To judge whether a piece is "trapped," mobility, exits, and supporting defenders must be seen.
- True
- False
> True. "Trapped" does not mean capture is already confirmed; count legal safe exits and verify capture, exchange, or escape possibilities. Then choose a move according to the immediate board result.

## ShortAnswer

Q: How would you combine opposition and key squares into a practical king plan in a king-pawn ending?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Answer:** I will control the king's approach and move order through opposition, then make the key-square zone the destination. I will check timing and the defending king's responses so the pawn push happens with support.

</details>

Q: How would you avoid wrong-side check, trapped piece, and back-rank trap in a rook-pawn ending?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Answer:** With every check I will look at the rook's rank, the pawn's route, and the defending king's escape/capture options. I will capture a trapped piece after a mobility scan, and with a pawn wall in front of the king I will check back-rank mate against the opposing rook/queen line.

</details>
