# 5.6 Exercise Pack — Essential Endgames

First note king and piece locations on each position. Give 90 seconds to positions 1 and 6; give two minutes to positions 2 through 5. Then write the best move and one likely reply. Before revealing the solution, state your reason in a sentence — "I did this because..." — because the endgame's goal is not just the move but the decision's pattern.

## Position 1 — Basic Mate Support

Black's king is on g8, and f7, g7, h7 pawns cover the back-rank escape squares. White has queen, bishop, and a knight; now find the checkmate support chain.

![Position 1 — basic mate support, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-01.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `6k1/3N1ppp/8/7Q/8/8/2B5/7K w - - 0 12`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 90 seconds; find the mating move and name two pieces or squares that make the king's escape impossible.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Qh7#`.

**Short continuation:** `Qh7#` — there is no legal Black move after the check.

**Why:** The queen on `h7` is protected by the bishop on `c2`, the knight on `d7` controls `f8`, and the queen controls `h8`. Black's own pawns occupy the remaining escape squares, so every check component has a concrete board reason.

**Common error:** `Qf7+` is a different check, but the queen and knight relationship is easier to see when the mating queen goes to `h7`. Do not call a move mate without checking capture and escape.

</details>

## Position 2 — King and Pawn Cooperation

White's king is on e5, pawn on e4, and black king far on e8. Now find the best move to bring the king to a useful support square before pushing the pawn.

![Position 2 — king and pawn cooperation, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-02.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `8/4k3/8/4K3/4P3/8/8/8 w - - 0 20`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; bring the king toward the pawn and write one plausible black king reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Kd5`.

**Short continuation:** `Kd5 Kd7 e5`; after the king supports the pawn, the advance can be considered with a clear relationship between the two pieces.

**Why:** `Kd5` improves the king's access to the pawn and keeps the pawn from standing alone. The king can then choose a useful next square while the defending king's approach is visible.

**Common error:** Moving the king too far without connecting it to the pawn wastes the support opportunity. The pawn cannot move to `e5` in the starting position because the king occupies that square, so always check occupancy before discussing a push.

</details>

## Position 3 — Use the Opposition

White king on e5, white pawn on d5, black king on e7. There is direct opposition between the kings — now how can the white pawn's checking move change opposition and route?

![Position 3 — opposition with a checking pawn push, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-03.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `8/4k3/8/3PK3/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 30`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; find the pawn move that uses the kings' alignment and give Black's one legal response plus an alternative.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `d6+`.

**Short continuation:** `d6+ Kd7 Kf6` is one line. After `d6+`, Black must answer the check; `...Kd7` is one legal king reply, while `...Kf7` and `...Ke8` are alternatives. After `...Kd7`, `Kf6` is a possible White king move.

**Why:** The pawn move changes the kings' direct opposition and gives White a new route. `...Kd7` is not forced; the important point is to verify all legal replies and choose the continuation that supports the king's key-square plan.

**Common error:** A quiet king move may leave the pawn's central route unchanged and allow Black to hold the opposition. The pawn push is valuable because it changes both the board and the move order, not merely because it is forward.

</details>

## Position 4 — Reach a Key Square

White king on e5 and pawn on e4, black king far on a8. Now identify which key-square zone is available for White and take the king there.

![Position 4 — key-square entry, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-04.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `k7/8/8/4K3/4P3/8/8/8 w - - 0 35`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; choose the first key-square route and name the next safe king step after a possible king reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Kd6`.

**Short continuation:** `Kd6 Kb7 Ke6`; both White king moves enter the useful zone around the pawn's advance.

**Why:** For a non-rook pawn, the squares around `d6`, `e6`, and `f6` form the practical key-square picture here. Reaching one of them gives the king a better relationship to promotion and reduces the defender's freedom to create a blockade.

**Common error:** The pawn cannot be pushed to `e5` while White's king is still on `e5`; first the king must create space and support. Also, any distant black king does not mean every king move works — check the route for the defender's approach.

</details>

## Position 5 — Rook Cut-Off

White has rook on a6, pawn on c6, and king on a1; black king is isolated on h8. Now bring the rook to an active rank to control the defending king's escape directions.

![Position 5 — rook cut-off, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-05.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `7k/8/R1P5/8/8/8/8/K7 w - - 0 40`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; choose a checking move that begins a cut-off plan and give one illustrative king reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Ra8+`.

**Short continuation:** `Ra8+ Kh7 Ra7+`; the rook controls the rank and then narrows the king's remaining side route.

**Why:** The rook uses its active rank to cut the king off from a useful central area. The pawn remains available for support, and the rook's next move continues the pressure instead of simply wandering with checks.

**Common error:** A pawn push such as `c7` may be legal, but it does not use the rook to restrict the king and gives up the active tempo. Rook-pawn technique needs both pieces' purposes, not only a pawn move.

</details>

## Position 6 — Capture the Trapped Knight

Black knight is on f8; white rook on f7 can capture it directly, while white king on f6 and c6 pawn control the knight's exits. Now do a mobility scan and immediately capture the trapped piece.

![Position 6 — trapped knight, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-5/phase-5-endgame-06.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `5n1k/5R2/2P2K2/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 50`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 90 seconds; identify all knight exits, then find the best capture and one legal king reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Rxf8+`.

**Short continuation:** `Rxf8+ Kh7`; after the knight is removed, the rook continues to control the king's route.

**Why:** The rook occupies `f7`, the king controls `e6` and `g6`, the c6 pawn controls `d7`, and the rook controls `h7`; the knight has no safe exit. Capturing immediately is cleaner than waiting while the defender gets a resource.

**Common error:** A distant rook check misses the already trapped piece and can give the king a useful escape. Count exits first, then bring the closest piece to the target.

</details>

## What It Is NOT

The exercise pack is **not a complete theorem course in endgames.** These moves are recognition of basic patterns, so instead of memorizing them, learn to explain king route, pawn timing, and piece mobility on the board. Advanced theory's complete coverage or a rating guarantee does not come from this pack.

One answer is **not a claim of every possible best move.** From a learner's perspective one clear practical move, its short continuation, and reason are already valuable feedback; then calculation or a stronger move becomes the next assignment.
