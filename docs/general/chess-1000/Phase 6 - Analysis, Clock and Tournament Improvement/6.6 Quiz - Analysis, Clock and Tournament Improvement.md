# 6.6 Quiz - Analysis, Clock and Tournament Improvement
## MCQ

Q: What should you do before the engine on an important position in your game?
A. Immediately copy the engine's best line
B. Write your candidate move and its reason with the FEN
C. Just look at the final result and start the next game
D. Keep solving every possible line
> B. Writing your candidate move and reason tests your calculation. The engine gives feedback on your thought, not a shortcut ahead of it.

---

Q: What is a decisive moment in game analysis?
A. The move that always demands the longest calculation
B. The position where the game's direction clearly starts to change
C. The first move in the Lichess opening
D. Every move where the engine evaluation changes
> B. A decisive moment is the point where the position's result, plan, or practical direction starts to materially change. Not every evaluation change is equal.

---

Q: What does "10+0" mean in Lichess rapid?
A. 10-minute increment per move
B. Each player gets 10 minutes, without fixed increment
C. 10 bonus seconds per game
D. 10 games in 10 minutes
> B. Each player gets 10 minutes on the starting clock and there is no fixed increment. The side-to-move's clock runs as soon as the game starts, and after the move the opponent's clock starts. Total decision time is distributed across all moves of the game.

---

Q: What is the best approach to using the engine like a coach?
A. Treating every disagreement as the engine's mistake
B. Only remembering the engine's exact number
C. Comparing your reasoning, PV, and practical alternatives
D. Following the engine's move on every position without thinking
> C. Writing your reasoning and alternatives and comparing them with the engine's PV turns feedback into learning. Blindly copying the engine's top move is not thought development.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: After seeing the engine's best line, you do not need to analyze your earlier move.
- True
- False
> False. Writing your move and reason first is necessary, otherwise you will not develop calculation. The engine's job is to give a signal by comparing, not to replace thought.

---

Q: Choosing a suitable rated event by checking time control, duration, pool, rated status, and realistic learning value is a practical choice.
- True
- False
> True. Time control, expected duration, pool, rated status, and available time for analysis should all be checked together. Fair, realistic learning value makes an event more important than easy points.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Explain your workflow for finding your game's decisive moment and making a next-week assignment.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** I will slowly replay a recent game on Lichess and pause, write the important position's FEN, and before opening the engine note my candidate move, likely forcing reply, and alternative. Then by comparing with the engine I will understand the quality difference, place the mistake in tactical, strategic, structure, king-safety, or time category. From that category I will make a small observable assignment, such as counting loose piece defenders in the next three games.

</details>

Q: Explain two practical habits of 10+0 clock and tournament recovery.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** In 10+0 I will not accept a fixed seconds-per-move rule; the side-to-move's clock is already running when the game starts, and after the move the opponent's clock starts. I will set different stage goals for opening, middlegame, and endgame, prioritize immediate threats, and manage time with a simple plan. After a tournament I will give the result context, take hydration and a short break to reset attention for the next round, then write one or two actionable lessons from a loss. I will check results after every round, choose realistic rated events, and avoid opponent/result-pool gaming.

</details>
