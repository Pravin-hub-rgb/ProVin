In practice you often miss the same question seen twice, so you have probably learned to lock the answer after thinking 30–60 seconds first. We will do the same on chess positions here: image, side to move, FEN, and short attempt, then the solution is revealed. Reading the solution before writing your own reason is not the real work of this course; copying the move is not the goal.

In `4.5 Plan se Position tak` you learned that a goal must be tested against the board's current evidence and that updating the plan after the opponent's reply is safe practice. The six positions below increase in difficulty one by one — first central space, then open file and pawn break, and finally tactical decisions with attack and exchange.

# 4.6 Exercise Pack — Middlegame Plans

For each position, first look at the image for 20–30 seconds, compare the FEN with the board, and confirm side to move. Then in maximum two minutes write your best move and one likely opponent reply; do not read the explanation yet. After revealing the solution, compare it with your move and note the mistake in one word, such as `safety`, `space`, `break`, `support`, or `calculation`.

## Position 1 — Central Space

White has built a setup with `e4` and a developed knight in the center, but the d-pawn is still sitting at home. Now see which purposeful move opens central space and clarity; in this position the rook is on `f1`, so the goal of this exercise is not e-file rook activity.

![Position 1 — central space, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-01.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `r1bqkb1r/ppp2ppp/3p1n2/4p3/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/R1BQ1RK1 w - - 0 5`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; choose White's best plan-move and write one plausible reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `d4`.

**Short continuation:** `d4 exd4 Nxd4`; White's e4 pawn is exchanged and the knight recaptures on `d4`, so the result is a central pawn exchange and a clearer activity plan. White's rook remains on `f1`, so this does not create e-file rook activity.

**Why:** `d4` immediately claims central space. If Black exchanges with `...exd4`, White recaptures with the only knight using `Nxd4`, removing the black pawn from the centre and putting the knight on an active central square. The result is a central exchange and a more flexible position, not a rook opening on the e-file.

**Common error:** `d3` is passive here because it keeps the centre closed; a random knight jump also misses the simple space gain. The point is not that `d4` wins by force, but that it gives the position a clear direction.

</details>

## Position 2 — Open the e-file

Here White's rook is on the e-file, but the e5 pawn has kept the line blocked. Now think of a move that captures the pawn and opens the e-file for the rook, then count Black's legal replies after the check.

![Position 2 — open-file capture, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-02.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `r1bqk2r/ppp2ppp/5n2/4P3/8/2N5/PPPP1PPP/R1BQR1K1 w - - 0 5`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; find the move that removes the block, checks on the open line, and creates a practical rook target.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `exf6+`.

**Short continuation:** `exf6+ Kf8` is one legal reply; Black can also block with `...Qe7`. In either case, Black must answer the check, and the pawn move has removed the knight while opening the rook's e-file.

**Why:** Capturing the knight clears the e-file and gives the rook a direct line to the black king. `...Qe7` is legal because the queen interposes on the checking line; `...Kf8` moves the king instead. The move combines a concrete tactical reason with the long-term open-file plan.

**Common error:** `Rxe6` is not legal from this placement, while moving the rook without removing the pawn leaves the line blocked. A file is not useful merely because a rook is near it; the pawn must actually move.

</details>

## Position 3 — Pawn Break Against the Centre

White has a queenside expansion option, but the black d6 pawn is anchoring the centre. Now choose a pawn break that challenges d6 and creates queenside space on the board.

![Position 3 — queenside pawn break, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-03.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `r1bqkb1r/ppp2ppp/3p1n2/8/2P1P3/2NP4/PP3PPP/R1BQ1RKR w - - 0 8`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; choose a pawn break and give the opponent's most direct recapture or reply.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `c5`.

**Short continuation:** `c5 dxc5 Qa4+`; the recapture can be met with pressure that makes the weakened structure visible.

**Why:** The break challenges `d6`, gains queenside space and activates the c-file as a future target. It also gives White a concrete choice — press, recapture, or develop — instead of moving pawns without a reason.

**Common error:** `b3` or `b4` without a follow-up does not solve the central anchor as directly, and `e5` ignores the queenside tension. Every break has a risk, so the reply must be part of the choice.

</details>

## Position 4 — Knight Attack Setup

White's bishop already creates diagonal control on c4, and the knight from f3 can jump to a kingside target. Now find a purposeful development move that creates pressure on a target with the bishop's support; do not treat it as forcing.

![Position 4 — knight attack setup, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-04.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `r1bqkb1r/ppp2ppp/3p1n2/4p3/2B1P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/R1BQ1RK1 w - - 0 5`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; find a move that attacks a pawn, uses a developed piece, and has a clear purpose after `...h6`.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Ng5`.

**Short continuation:** `Ng5 h6 2. Nxf7` is one illustrative continuation. After the knight captures on `f7`, the bishop on `c4` protects that square along `c4-d5-e6-f7`, so Black cannot simply play `...Kxf7`; the rest of the position still needs calculation.

**Why:** The knight becomes active on the kingside and attacks `f7` and `h7`. It is not a forcing move, but it creates a concrete target, and the bishop on `c4` supplies the relevant support for a later `Nxf7`.

**Common error:** `d4` repeats a familiar central break without exploiting the already-placed bishop, and moving the king for no reason abandons safety. A good attacking move should improve activity and ask a question.

</details>

## Position 5 — Exchange the Active Knight

Black's d4 knight shows activity in the centre, and White's f3 knight has targeted it. Now decide whether immediately exchanging this active opponent piece is the right resource removal.

![Position 5 — central exchange, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-05.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `2bqkr1r/ppp2ppp/3p4/4p3/3nP3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/2BQ1RKR w - - 0 7`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; choose the exchange and write Black's recapture plus the strategic result.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Nxd4`.

**Short continuation:** `Nxd4 exd4`; the black pawn recaptures, so material is exchanged while the centre and king safety determine the next plan.

**Why:** Removing the developed knight reduces a central source of counterplay. The recapture is legal, so White must still judge the resulting pawn structure and rook activity; simplification is a decision, not a free gift.

**Common error:** `Nxe5` looks tempting because it attacks a pawn, but it can allow Black to use the d4 knight's influence instead of solving the immediate problem. A capture is not automatically a simplification if it leaves the worse piece active.

</details>

## Position 6 — Support the Attack

White's bishop on c4 can create pressure on `f7` along the diagonal, and since `f2` is empty in front of the rook on f1, the rook can support that capture. Now execute the supported checking attack with escape squares.

![Position 6 — supported attack, White to move](/diagrams/chess/phase-4/phase-4-middlegame-06.svg)

**Side to move:** White (W)  
**FEN:** `2bqk1n1/pppppppp/8/8/2B5/2N5/PPPPP1PP/3Q1RKR w - - 0 10`  
**Attempt limit/task:** 2 minutes; find the checking move, then name one legal king reply and explain what support prevents.

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

**Best move:** `Bxf7+`.

**Short continuation:** `Bxf7+ Kf8` is one legal reply. The bishop on `f7` is protected by the rook on `f1` along the clear f-file, so `...Kxf7` is not legal; Black must choose another response.

**Why:** The attack combines a material target, a diagonal and rook-file support. The point is not only to win a pawn; it is to make the king show where its safety comes from and then continue with a forcing or positional resource.

**Common error:** `Bxf7` is easy to say but easy to explain badly. If support, king escape, and recapture checks are skipped, the move can be treated as a lucky capture instead of a coordinated attack.

</details>

After completing the pack, open the board again before copying answers. If you are only looking at calculation for every move, practicing purposeful plan in 10-minute games will be useful.

## What It Is NOT

The exercise pack is **not a rating forecast.** In these six positions the answers measure your current understanding, but that does not mean a 1000 is guaranteed in the next game; the actual result depends on calculation, time, and the opponent.

Hidden solutions are **not a shortcut to copy answers.** If you do not write your own reason before opening the details, you skip the exercise's most important part — seeing your decision process. That is why noting one common error after each answer is useful.
