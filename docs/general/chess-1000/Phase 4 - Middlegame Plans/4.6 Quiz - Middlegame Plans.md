# 4.6 Quiz - Middlegame Plans
## MCQ

Q: What is the most correct beginner-level meaning of a middlegame plan?
A. Always repeating the theory line you memorized in the opening
B. Choosing a goal for the position and adjusting the route with opponent replies
C. Bringing the queen to the centre on every move
D. Avoiding trades whenever you have more material
> B. A plan describes a future goal and its practical route, not a fixed sequence. Opponent moves can change the board, so the route must be re-checked with safety and activity.

---

Q: What is the most practical use of an open file?
A. Only pushing a pawn forward
B. Giving the rook work of placing targets and creating pressure on a clear vertical line
C. Always bringing the king to the centre
D. Exchanging every bishop with a knight
> B. No pawn remains on an open file, so placing the rook on a clear line and creating pressure on ranks and weak squares becomes possible. An open line is an advantage, but rook safety and defenders must also be checked.

---

Q: What is one valid reason for simplification?
A. Trading immediately whenever a material opportunity appears
B. Capturing a bishop only because every winning side must
C. Making the position safer and calculation-friendly by reducing active threats
D. Always keeping the queen in the centre
> C. Simplification can reduce defending pieces and open threats, and the future plan of the position can become clear. An exchange is useful when final activity, king safety, and pawn structure are better.

---

Q: What is the beginner-level question that connects attack and defence?
A. Only how many pieces there are
B. Which target is vulnerable and which move blocks that entry
C. Which move looks the fanciest
D. Which pawn is on the last rank
> B. Target, entry, and defender are the practical engine of attack on the board. Counting material is helpful context, but without checking target and entry the reason for attack or defence stays incomplete.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: Having an open file automatically means the rook will mate on it.
- True
- False
> False. An open file gives the rook direction, but how strong the attack is depends on king, pawns, defenders, entry, and target. The file becomes practical when the rook's work is done; an empty file is no guarantee.

---

Q: Sometimes an exchange can be right for simplifying and making the position safer instead of winning material.
- True
- False
> True. An exchange can remove a defending piece, threat, or awkward activity. Still, final king safety, pawn structure, and the opponent's recapture must be checked, so simplification is not automatic.

## ShortAnswer

Q: How would you test your middlegame plan on the board when the opponent has covered your weak square?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Answer:** First I will note the new board evidence: the target is blocked, counterplay has appeared, or piece support has changed. Then I will choose the target, exchange, opposite-side plan, or defensive move that gives the best reason in the current position. I will not stay stuck on a fixed opening line.

</details>

---

Q: How do you make a practical decision between simplifying and increasing pieces?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Answer:** I will see which piece is active, which trapped or defending piece can be reduced, how king safety changes, and which target appears after the move. Exchange when it reduces threats or gives a safer position; relocation when the piece does work on a weak square, open line, or target. The final position decides more than piece value.

</details>
