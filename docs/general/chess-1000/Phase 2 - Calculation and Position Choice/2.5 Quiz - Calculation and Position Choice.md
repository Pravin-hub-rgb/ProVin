# 2.5 Quiz - Calculation and Position Choice
## MCQ

Q: Your position has 20 legal moves. What is the most practical approach for Phase 2?
A. Calculate all 20 moves with equal importance up to 10 plies
B. Shortlist 2–5 realistic candidates from checks, captures, threats, king danger, and important improvements
C. Blindly follow one engine recommendation
D. Choose only the move that gives check
> B. The beginner-friendly target of calculation is a limited candidate list and short replies. Giving equal weight to every legal move can waste calculation; priority according to safety and the position's need is better.

---

Q: What is most necessary for a move to be considered forcing?
A. It being a check, capture, or threat
B. The opponent's meaningful legal replies being materially restricted
C. Remembering the longest engine line afterward
D. It being engine-best in every possible position
> B. Check, capture, or threat can describe a forcing move, but do not automatically make it one. The defining test is how few and how clearly defined the opponent's meaningful choices have become.

---

Q: `Qe5` blocks the check, but the rook has `Rxe5+`. What will you do?
A. Play `Qe5` because it is legal
B. Evaluate the block with safety and choose a legal move that avoids the queen being captured
C. Ignore the king's current check
D. Wait after the move to see the opponent's reply
> B. A legal block is not sufficient safety. The `Rxe5+` counterattack shows the blocker is unprotected, so the best move must be chosen without immediate risk.

---

Q: What can be the primary purpose of a quiet move?
A. Giving an immediate check to the opponent
B. Improving a piece's activity, safety, or structure for the future when the move gives neither check nor capture
C. Making every square on the board equal
D. Always avoiding long calculation even when an opportunity exists
> B. A quiet move can look slow, but it can give a piece an active square, the king safety, or pawns a stable shape. Its value shows in the future board snapshot.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: For this course, calculation means humans must always calculate long engine lines.
- True
- False
> False. Practical calculation focuses on candidate moves, short forcing lines, safety, and immediate evaluation. Long exhaustive lines are not necessary for beginner decisions in 10+0.

---

Q: A forcing move is always the best move, even if it gives a piece away for free.
- True
- False
> False. A forcing move restricts the opponent's choices, but it is not best without safety or material evaluation. The move can expose a forcing counterattack.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Explain Phase 2's practical calculation routine in your own words: what is the order of candidate, opponent reply, safety, and evaluation?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> I first scan the king and note immediate danger. Then I choose two or three realistic candidates from checks, captures, threats, and important improvements. After each candidate I look at the opponent's strongest reply and evaluate moved piece, material, and future activity. I extend a long line only when the forcing sequence's result is clear; otherwise I pause and build a shortlist again on the new position.

</details>

---

Q: Explain the practical difference between a forcing move and a quiet move, with one example each.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> A forcing move meaningfully restricts the opponent's legal replies; in `1. Nd6+ Kf8 2. Nxc8` the check limits Black's king replies and every reply's material result must be verified. A quiet move gives neither check nor capture but can improve activity, safety, or structure; in the pawn-endgame example `1. Kd6` keeps the king on a key square. Safety and position evaluation are necessary after both.

</details>
