# 7.4 Quiz - Rating Readiness
## MCQ

Q: How should this course's rating target be honestly defined?
A. Seeing 1000 in one game means guaranteed progress
B. Reviewing a 20 comparable rated games course checkpoint and making a provisional readiness decision, no rating guarantee
C. Target complete only on winning a tournament
D. Only engine evaluation average
> B. These 20 comparable rated games are this course's practical checkpoint, not a scientifically validated minimum or a definition of a stable rating. A pattern of multiple games and decision evidence can be the basis of provisional readiness; no plan guarantees a rating.

---

Q: What is the capstone's extra focus compared to the ten-game training block?
A. Only increasing total score
B. Integrated protocol, ten-game tracked sample, written review, and final readiness decision
C. Copying the engine's number
D. Choosing the weakest opponents
> B. The capstone integrates tools into real, comparable games and collects documented evidence. Fair selection and honest review are part of it.

---

Q: What is the minimum useful written record for every reviewed game?
A. Only result
B. Only opponent rating
C. FEN, your move and reason, forcing reply, alternative, and next cue
D. Only engine percentage
> C. The minimum record connects problem, thought, comparison, and next action. This makes review usable in future practice.

---

Q: When should you choose to repeat the block in the final readiness decision?
A. When a lucky rating peak comes
B. When stability, time routine, or review evidence is still weak and a targeted block can bring improvement
C. When no game improves
D. When the engine's number is not perfect
> B. Repeat is meaningful when data shows a targeted improvement gap and the plan can be adjusted to that gap. An unlucky result is not an automatic reason.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: Completing the 10-game training block is completely useful even without review, equivalent to just playing 10 games.
- True
- False
> False. The block's learning output comes from focused review and written assignment after every game. Without review, volume can increase, but reliable evidence of decision quality is less.

---

Q: The honest basis of rating readiness is a 20 comparable rated games course checkpoint and written review, more than one lucky peak.
- True
- False
> True. Multiple comparable games and documented decisions give broader evidence. The 20 games are a course checkpoint, not a scientifically validated minimum; the decision is provisional and there is no rating guarantee.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Which metrics will you track in the ten-game capstone and final review, and how will you choose the readiness label?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** I will track every game's rating snapshot, opponent rating, result, time-trouble flag, primary error, written review status, and next cue. In final review I will look at 20 comparable rated games course checkpoint snapshots, time-trouble pattern, recurring error categories, and review quality, and choose `continue around 1000`, `repeat the 10-game block`, or `slow down and rebuild`. The choice will remain provisional on the rolling sample; 20 games are not a scientifically validated minimum, and there is no rating guarantee.

</details>

<details>

I will fit warm-up, focused practice, game or position work, review, and logging into the daily 45-minute routine; the goal is to build evidence of the rolling sample, not a false promise of rating.
