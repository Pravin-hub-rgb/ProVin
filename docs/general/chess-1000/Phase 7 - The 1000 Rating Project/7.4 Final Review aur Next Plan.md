# 7.4 Final Review and Next Plan

You must stay honest in this review. Some games will be good, some average, and some weak; this mixed evidence is useful. You will look at the complete ten-game capstone record, not only best games or a lucky streak, and extract the next action from it.

## Capstone readiness checkpoint

After attempting all six decision/plan cards of the Phase 7 pack, you can fill a checkpoint. Its purpose is not to score every card, but to see whether you can write your move and plan and compare with the engine or answer, or whether you are still just looking at the result and moving ahead.

You can mark these observable signs as `yes`, `not yet`, or `needs review`:

- On at least four of six cards you wrote your move, one alternative, and a practical reason without a hint.
- After every card you compared your choice and real-game plan, not just copied the answer.
- In training block and capstone logs you tracked rating snapshot, time trouble, primary error, and review status.
- In written review, decisive moment, missed alternative, and next-week cue are all clearly readable.
- You can choose the `continue`, `repeat`, or `rebuild` label from the 20 comparable rated games course checkpoint sample; this decision is provisional.

If two or more signs are `not yet`, instead of saying "I am ready," repeat the targeted block. This is not failure, but an honest reason for the next evidence; and if the signs are `yes`, you can review the provisional case for continuing around the 1000 target, without a rating guarantee.

## Final review sheet

Now review training block's 10 games and capstone's 10 games as a comparable rated sample. If a game had a different time control or pool, or was unrated, mark it separately; do not quietly add it to the rated sample of the 20-game course checkpoint.

Write your readiness decision by looking at these five fields together:

- **Rating picture:** Does a practical pattern around 1000 show in the course checkpoint snapshots of 20 comparable rated games or not.
- **Decision quality:** Are decisive errors in one or two repeated patterns, or does every game feel random.
- **Time routine:** Did time-trouble games become fewer, predictable, and manageable?
- **Review quality:** Was FEN, your move, alternative, and next cue written for every game?
- **Next need:** What is most necessary now — tactical practice, king safety, endgame conversion, or clock work?

Do not compress these fields into only "good" or "bad." Write an evidence example under every field, such as game number, FEN, or recurring phrase. This way the final decision should be review-based, not feel-based.

## Three honest final decisions

If you see stable performance around 1000 in the 20 comparable rated games course checkpoint sample, recurring errors are manageable, and the review routine can continue, you can choose the **around 1000 continue** option. These 20 games are not a scientifically validated minimum; the decision is provisional on a rolling sample, and there is no rating guarantee.

If results are in a range, time trouble is frequent, or decisions deteriorate outside review, **repeat the ten-game block.** In the new block, target not only adding games but one or two measurable triggers. Sometimes an extra block exposes a weakness; this is not failure, it is measurement.

If there are foundational blind spots where even the core rule of tactic, king safety, or endgame conversion is unclear in every game, use the **slow down and rebuild** label. This does not mean leaving the 45-minute schedule, but splitting its goals into concrete chunks of tactics, review, and fundamentals.

You must also write your label with an honest reason. Writing "I am ready" to justify a lucky streak is not readiness evidence. "I will repeat the block because the same knight-defense error was in games 4 and 8" is a useful, actionable decision.

## What It Is NOT

Final review is **not a certificate or guaranteed outcome.** It organizes your evidence, it does not lock a future result.

Final review does **not treat every weak rating as proof of failure.** A weak sample is a signal; you need to improve fundamentals, time, or review in a targeted way.

Final review is **not just celebrating the past.** Its output is the next schedule, trigger, and readiness label. Your next goal should be observable, realistic, and in your control.
