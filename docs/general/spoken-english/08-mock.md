# Phase 8 — Mock Sessions
### Goal: Put everything together. Simulate real situations. Record. Review. Improve.

---

## What This Phase Is

This is not learning — this is testing. By now you've covered:
- Shadowing and the recording habit (Phase 1)
- PREP framework (Phase 2)
- Call center English (Phase 3)
- Explaining clearly (Phase 4)
- Meeting English (Phase 5)
- Interview English (Phase 6)
- Filler control (Phase 7)

Now you use all of it at once, under simulated pressure, without any notes in front of you.

The rule for this phase: **no looking at your notes while speaking.** You can prepare beforehand. But once you hit record, it's live.

---

## How to Use This Phase

Do one mock session at a time. Don't rush through all four in one day. Space them out — one every 2-3 days. After each session, spend time reviewing the recording before doing the next one.

After you finish all four mocks, repeat them again a week later. Compare. Improve.

---

## Mock 1 — Full Call Center Call

**Time:** 5-8 minutes
**Setup:** Read the scenario. Hit record. Handle the full call from greeting to close.

**Scenario:**

A customer calls in. They say:

> "Hi, I've been a customer for two years and I've never had a problem before. But last month I was charged for a premium plan that I never signed up for. I want this refunded immediately and I want to know how this happened."

Handle the full call:
- Greet them professionally
- Acknowledge their frustration
- Ask for the details you need
- Explain what you're doing
- Offer the resolution
- Confirm everything
- Close the call warmly

**What to check when listening back:**
- Did your tone stay warm even when they were upset?
- Did you acknowledge before jumping to the solution?
- Were your sentences short and clear?
- Did you confirm the resolution before closing?
- Filler count — how many?

---

## Mock 2 — Project Explanation to a Client

**Time:** 4-5 minutes
**Setup:** You're on a call with a client who is not technical. They've hired you to build something. They want to understand what you're doing and when it will be ready.

**Client says:**

> "I just want to understand — what exactly are you building for me, how does it work, and when will I see something?"

Answer this completely. Use Simple → Detail → Example. Then give them a clear timeline.

You can use any real project you've worked on — the options chain tool, a landing page, anything.

**What to check:**
- Did you start with a simple one-sentence explanation?
- Did you avoid unexplained jargon?
- Did you give a concrete example of how it works?
- Did you give a clear timeline with next steps?
- Did you check if they had questions at the end?

---

## Mock 3 — Team Meeting Contribution

**Time:** 4-5 minutes
**Setup:** You're in a team meeting. The manager opens with this:

> "Okay team, we need to decide — do we launch the product next week as planned, or do we delay by two weeks to finish the extra features the client requested? I want to hear from everyone."

Speak your full contribution to this meeting. Include:
- Your opinion (with reasons and an example)
- A response to an imaginary colleague who disagrees with you
- A clarifying question for the manager
- A closing statement summarizing what you think the team should do

**What to check:**
- Did you use meeting phrases naturally?
- Did you disagree respectfully?
- Did you ask for clarification clearly?
- Did you sound like someone who belongs in the room?

---

## Mock 4 — Full Job Interview

**Time:** 15-18 minutes
**Setup:** This is a real interview simulation. Answer all 8 questions back to back, as if you're actually sitting in front of an interviewer. No notes. No pauses between questions.

The 8 questions:
1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why do you want this job?
3. What are your strengths?
4. What is a weakness you're working on?
5. Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you handled it.
6. Where do you see yourself in 2 years?
7. Why should we hire you?
8. Do you have any questions for us?

**What to check:**
- Was your "tell me about yourself" clear and under 90 seconds?
- Did you use PREP for the challenge question?
- Did you sound confident or hesitant?
- Were your answers specific or vague?
- Filler count — was it lower than your first recording weeks ago?

---

## Reviewing Your Recordings — What to Listen For

After each mock, use this checklist:

**Clarity**
- [ ] Were my sentences complete and easy to follow?
- [ ] Did I explain things in a logical order?

**Confidence**
- [ ] Did my voice stay steady?
- [ ] Did I pause calmly instead of filling with "uh"?

**Structure**
- [ ] Did I use PREP where relevant?
- [ ] Did I open and close properly (calls and meetings)?

**Tone**
- [ ] Did I sound warm and professional?
- [ ] Was I too flat, too fast, or too quiet?

**Vocabulary**
- [ ] Did I use any of the words from the vocabulary list naturally?
- [ ] Were there moments where I reached for a word and couldn't find it?

## After the Four Mocks — What Next?

You're not done. Speaking is a skill that degrades if you don't keep practicing. Here's what to do going forward:

**Weekly habit:**
- One 60-second filler count exercise (Phase 7)
- One shadowing session with a new video
- One recorded free-speech session on any topic

**Monthly habit:**
- Redo Mock 4 (the full interview)
- Compare to your previous recording
- Note 3 improvements and 1 thing to keep working on

**When you get a real interview or start a real job:**
- Do all 4 mocks again in the 2 days before
- Record yourself the night before and listen back once

---

## Final Note

You started this course speaking decent English with confidence issues and filler habits. By the time you finish all 4 mocks seriously — recording, reviewing, redoing — you will speak differently. Not perfectly. But significantly better.

The difference between you now and you after completing this course is not grammar knowledge. It's hours of recorded, reviewed, deliberate practice. That is what changes how you speak.

Keep going.
