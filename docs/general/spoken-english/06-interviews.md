# Phase 6 — Interview English
### Goal: Answer common interview questions confidently and clearly. Sound prepared without sounding rehearsed.

---

## What Interviewers Are Actually Looking For

Most people think interviews are about giving the "right" answer. They're not. Interviewers are looking for three things:

1. **Can this person communicate clearly?** — Do they answer the question or ramble?
2. **Do they have self-awareness?** — Do they know their strengths and weaknesses honestly?
3. **Do they seem confident?** — Not arrogant, but sure of themselves.

You don't need perfect English to do all three. You need structure (PREP), honest content, and calm delivery.

---

## The 8 Most Common Interview Questions

These come up in almost every interview — for call center jobs, tech jobs, any professional job. Practice all 8.

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why do you want this job?
3. What are your strengths?
4. What is a weakness you're working on?
5. Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you handled it.
6. Where do you see yourself in 2 years?
7. Why should we hire you?
8. Do you have any questions for us?

---

## Detailed Answer Guide — With Examples

### 1. Tell me about yourself

This is always the first question. It's your opening statement. Keep it to 60-90 seconds.

**Structure:** Who you are → What you do → What you're looking for

> "My name is Vin. I've been working in tech for a few years — primarily as a freelance developer building websites, dashboards, and automation tools for clients. I've worked with small teams and handled everything from client communication to actual development. Right now I'm looking for a role where I can apply my technical skills and also grow my professional communication. I'm focused, I learn quickly, and I work well under pressure."

**Your version:** Write and practice your own version of this. Make it true to your actual background.

---

### 2. Why do you want this job?

Interviewers ask this to see if you've actually thought about it or if you're just applying everywhere.

**Structure:** What interests you about this specific role → What you bring to it

> "I'm interested in this role because I want to work in a structured professional environment where I can improve my communication skills while contributing my technical knowledge. Call center work specifically interests me because I enjoy problem-solving and helping people reach a resolution. I think my background in client-facing freelance work has prepared me well for this."

**Don't say:** "Because I need a job" or "Because the salary is good." Even if true — don't say it.

---

### 3. What are your strengths?

Pick 2-3 real strengths. Then give an example for at least one.

> "I'd say my top strengths are that I learn new things quickly and I stay calm under pressure. For example, I recently had to deliver a project using an API I had never worked with before — I figured it out in a week and delivered on time. I don't need a lot of hand-holding to get started on something new."

---

### 4. What is a weakness you're working on?

This question catches people off guard. The wrong answer is "I work too hard" or "I'm a perfectionist" — everyone knows that's not real.

Give a real weakness. Then immediately say what you're doing about it.

> "Honestly, my spoken English confidence is something I've been actively working on. I'm technically strong but I sometimes felt less confident in verbal communication. That's actually why I've been doing structured speaking practice — including this course — because I want to be as comfortable speaking as I am building."

This answer is honest, self-aware, and shows initiative. That's impressive.

---

### 5. Tell me about a challenge you faced

Use PREP here. This is the classic behavioral interview question.

> **P:** "One challenge I faced was managing a client who kept changing the requirements mid-project."
> **R:** "This was creating delays and extra work because every change meant rebuilding parts of what was already done."
> **E:** "What I did was create a simple one-page requirements document and asked the client to sign off on it before I started each phase. Any new change after sign-off would go into a separate list for after delivery."
> **P:** "After that, the project ran smoothly and the client was actually more satisfied because expectations were clear from both sides."

---

### 6. Where do you see yourself in 2 years?

They want to know if you have ambition and if this job fits into your growth.

> "In two years, I'd like to have grown into a senior role where I'm not just executing tasks but also contributing to decisions. I want to be someone the team relies on for both technical knowledge and communication. This role feels like the right foundation for that."

---

### 7. Why should we hire you?

This is your closing argument. Be direct and confident.

> "Because I bring a combination of technical ability and the willingness to work hard on things I'm not yet perfect at. I won't come in and pretend I know everything — but I will come in, learn fast, and deliver. I'm reliable, I communicate proactively, and I take ownership of my work."

---

### 8. Do you have any questions for us?

Always have questions. Never say "No, I think I'm good." That signals you're not genuinely interested.

**Good questions to ask:**
- "What does a typical day look like in this role?"
- "What are the most important qualities for someone to succeed in this position?"
- "How is performance measured in the first 3 months?"
- "What does the team structure look like?"

---

## Things That Make You Sound More Confident in Any Answer

| Instead of this | Say this |
|---|---|
| "I think maybe I could..." | "I'm confident I can..." |
| "I'm not sure but..." | "Based on my experience..." |
| "I don't know, like, I just..." | "The way I see it is..." |
| "It was kind of difficult..." | "It was a real challenge, and here's how I handled it..." |
| "Sorry, um, let me think..." | "That's a good question. Give me a moment." |

---

## The Pause Technique

If you're asked something unexpected and your mind goes blank — don't panic and don't start saying "uh uh uh" while you think.

Instead, say one of these:

> "That's a great question. Let me think about that for a moment."

> "I want to give you a proper answer — just give me a second."

Then pause. Breathe. Think. Speak.

A 3-second pause feels long to you but sounds completely normal to the interviewer. It actually signals that you're thoughtful, not unprepared.

---

## Full Mock Interview — Do This Out Loud

Answer all 8 questions back to back without stopping. Record the entire thing.

Set a timer. Try to keep your total answer time under 12 minutes for all 8.

After listening back, check:
- Which answers were strong?
- Which felt weak or vague?
- Where did your confidence drop?
- Where did you use too many fillers?

Do the full mock again 2-3 days later. Compare the recordings.
