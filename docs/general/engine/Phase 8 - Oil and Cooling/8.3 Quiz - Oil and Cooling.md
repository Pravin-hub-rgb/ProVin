# 8.3 Quiz - Oil and Cooling
## MCQ

Q: What is the oil's first job?
A. To cool the cylinders directly
B. To sit as a thin film between every pair of rubbing metal surfaces, so the parts ride on oil instead of touching
C. To clean the intake air
D. To make the engine quieter
> B. The chain is metal moving on metal under load; the film moves the sliding into a liquid, where it costs far less force and shaves off nothing. Friction is not removed - it is made cheap.

---

Q: What does the oil filter do?
A. It cools the oil before it returns to the pan
B. It traps the microscopic metal particles and soot the oil picks up, so the oil can keep circulating clean
C. It pressurizes the oil for the pump
D. It removes water from the oil
> B. The oil washes wear particles and combustion soot off the surfaces it touches; the filter, a replaceable canister in its path, catches that load. When the oil saturates faster than the filter can compensate, both are replaced.

---

Q: Why is the radiator mounted at the very front of the car?
A. Because coolant must enter the engine at the front
B. Because its job is giving heat to passing air, and the front is where the car moves through the most air - with a fan covering the stopped case
C. To balance the weight over the front axle
D. Because the exhaust needs a pre-cooler there
> B. The radiator is a grid of narrow tubes and fins - maximum surface for air contact. Moving air pulls the heat from the coolant; when the car is stopped, an electric fan pulls air through instead.

---

Q: An engine runs with its coolant gone. What is the correct chain of failure?
A. Fuel stops flowing, the engine simply quits cleanly
B. The metal overheats and expands past its clearances, the oil overheats and stops protecting, the head can warp, and the engine can seize dead
C. The exhaust valve melts first and the engine backfires
D. Nothing - the radiator is only for winter
> B. The heat arrives with every bang and nothing carries it away. Metal expands with heat and grips, the oil film breaks down, the cylinder head's sealing face can warp, and the moving parts can lock - often permanently.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: The coolant's job is to cool the incoming air before it reaches the cylinders.
- True
- False
> False. The coolant cools the engine's own metal - the block and head around the cylinders. The incoming air is a separate subject entirely; turbo engines even heat their intake air by compressing it, which is the intercooler's job (file 7.2).

---

Q: Oil carries some of the engine's heat away from its hottest rubbing points.
- True
- False
> True. It is the oil's second job: soaking up heat at the piston undersides and bearings as it passes and releasing it on its way around the loop. Its share is small - the larger exits are the exhaust and the coolant loop.

---

Q: The exhaust carries a large share of the combustion heat out of the engine.
- True
- False
> True. The exhaust stroke sweeps the hot burnt gas out, and much heat leaves with it down the tailpipe. File 7.2's turbo recycles part of that stream's energy before it escapes.

---

Q: Oil is replaced because the engine slowly consumes it.
- True
- False
> False. The oil loads up: it carries wear particles and soot to the filter, and over time it saturates until it can no longer keep the surfaces clean or protect them. Fresh oil and a new filter restore the film and the cleaning capacity.

---

## ShortAnswer

Q: Trace the heat of one bang from the cylinders to the outside world, naming all three exits and what carries each.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** The burn releases heat, which splits three ways. One exit rides the exhaust: the exhaust stroke sweeps the hot burnt gas down the tailpipe, and a turbo can catch some of that stream's energy on the way (file 7.2). A second exit is the oil, which soaks up heat at the hottest rubbing points and releases it as it circulates - a small share. The largest remaining share soaks into the block and head, where the coolant picks it up from passages around the cylinders; the water pump pushes that heated coolant to the radiator at the front, passing air between the fins pulls the heat out, and the cooled liquid returns to repeat the loop.

</details>

---

Q: A running engine survives only because two support systems run continuously. Name each system, its one-line job, and the part that drives its loop.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** The oil system - a film between every pair of rubbing surfaces so metal never touches metal, plus heat carrying, cleaning through the oil filter, and rust prevention - driven by the oil pump lifting oil from the bottom pan and forcing it through channels to every bearing and wall. The cooling system - carrying the heat that soaks into the block and head out to the air - driven by the water pump pushing coolant from the passages around the cylinders to the radiator's fins and back.

</details>
