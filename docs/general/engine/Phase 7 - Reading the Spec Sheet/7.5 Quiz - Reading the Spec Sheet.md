# 7.5 Quiz - Reading the Spec Sheet
## MCQ

Q: A "1.5 L" engine - what does the 1.5 litres measure?
A. The engine's total weight
B. The total volume all pistons sweep per full cycle - about 375 cc per cylinder if there are four
C. The fuel tank size
D. The exhaust's volume per minute
> B. Displacement is the sum of the cylinders' swept volumes: cross-sectional area times stroke, added across all cylinders. 1.5 L is 1,500 cc, split across the cylinder count.

---

Q: What spins a turbocharger's turbine?
A. A belt from the crankshaft
B. The engine's own exhaust gas
C. An electric motor
D. The incoming intake air
> B. The turbine sits in the exhaust path, spun by the hot, pressurized gas leaving the cylinders - energy the engine was already wasting. A belt-driven compressor is a supercharger, the different device.

---

Q: Why can a turbocharged 1.5 L engine out-power a 2.0 L engine without one?
A. It burns hotter spark plugs
B. Displacement is not power - the turbo pressurizes the intake so the same 1.5 L breathes like a larger engine
C. It revs past its redline
D. It uses more cylinders
> B. Power depends on breathing, revs and tuning, not capacity alone. Boost packs denser air into the same displacement, letting more fuel burn per cycle - the cylinders never grew.

---

Q: What is turbo lag?
A. The delay between demanding power and receiving it, because weak exhaust flow at low revs spins the turbine slowly
B. The time the intercooler takes to cool the air
C. The clutch's engagement delay
D. The fuel pump's response time
> A. The turbine is driven by exhaust flow; at low engine speeds that flow is weak, so boost - and the power it enables - arrives only as revs climb. Twin turbos reduce the delay by spooling faster.

---

Q: What is the blow-off valve's job, and what is the sharp *pssh* sound on a lifted throttle?
A. It vents exhaust pressure to cool the turbine
B. It releases the boost trapped between compressor and snapped-shut throttle, so the trapped air cannot push back against and drag down the spinning compressor wheel
C. It dumps fuel to cool the cylinders
D. It is the turbo spooling up
> B. Lift off mid-boost and the throttle disc shuts, but the exhaust-fed compressor keeps pushing air into the now-closed pipe. The spring-loaded blow-off valve vents that trapped air out - the *pssh* - so the wheel that took so long to spool keeps spinning freely.

---

Q: What does 3,000 rpm mean?
A. 3,000 turns per second
B. 3,000 full crankshaft turns per minute - 50 turns every second
C. 3,000 piston strokes per hour
D. 3,000 km of engine life
> B. RPM is revolutions per minute of the crankshaft. 3,000 rpm is the middle of an engine's everyday range, and it means every piston stops and reverses 100 times per second.

---

Q: The tachometer's red band marks what?
A. Where the engine makes peak power
B. The redline - the maximum rpm the engine is built to sustain
C. Where to shift for fuel economy
D. The idle speed zone
> B. Past the redline, the reciprocating parts' forces - pistons stopping and reversing twice per stroke - grow toward failure. The band is the engine's built stress limit.

---

Q: Why is a power figure always written as "110 hp at 6,000 rpm"?
A. Because the engine makes 110 hp everywhere in its range
B. Because output varies across the range - the rpm states where in the power band the peak lives
C. Because 6,000 rpm is the idle speed
D. Because horsepower is measured per minute
> B. An engine is weak at idle, builds through its power band, and peaks near the top before the redline. Without the rpm, the number hides whether the peak is broad or a narrow top-end sprint.

---

Q: In the sheet "1.5 L, 4-cylinder inline, DOHC 16-valve", what does the 16-valve count describe?
A. 16 camshafts
B. 16 cylinders
C. Four valves per cylinder - two intake and two exhaust - a breathing-friendly head
D. The number of gears
> C. Sixteen valves across four cylinders is four per cylinder. With DOHC - two overhead camshafts, one per valve set - the field describes an engine built for breathing.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: Displacement is a direct measure of an engine's power.
- True
- False
> False. Displacement is breathing capacity per cycle - a 1.5 L turbo can out-power a 2.0 L without one. Power depends on breathing, revs and tuning as well as capacity.

---

Q: Torque (Nm) is the turning force available at the crankshaft, which the gearbox's ratios then trade against speed.
- True
- False
> True. Nm measures the twist the flywheel carries into the clutch - the engine-side force from file 6.1's trade. Peak torque usually sits in the mid-range, which is why engines feel strong in everyday revs.

---

Q: A diesel engine has no knocking problem from a turbo's hot, dense intake, because diesel is designed to self-ignite from compression heat.
- True
- False
> True. Petrol's early self-ignition is the fault turbocharged petrol engines guard against with intercoolers. For diesel, compression heat is the ignition method itself - so denser air simply burns proportionally more fuel, which is why turbos became universal on modern diesels.

---

Q: "6-speed manual" means the gearbox has six free-spinning gears and no clutch.
- True
- False
> False. It means six selectable gear pairs behind a driver-operated clutch - six ratios to hold the engine in its power band, from the low first pair for launch force to the tall sixth for cruising.

---

Q: "NA" on a car meet stands for naturally aspirated - an engine with no turbo or supercharger, breathing only on its own suction.
- True
- False
> True. Suction alone fills the cylinders only at ordinary atmospheric pressure - displacement times outside air density, the cap file 7.2's turbo exists to attack. NA is the meeting shorthand for the engine that accepts that cap.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Take the line "1.5 L, 4-cylinder inline, DOHC 16-valve, turbo petrol, 110 hp at 6,000 rpm, 150 Nm at 3,500 rpm, 6-speed manual, front-wheel drive" and decode each field in one phrase.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** 1.5 L - the pistons sweep 1,500 cc total, about 375 cc each. 4-cylinder inline - four cylinders in a row (Phase 5's basic layout). DOHC 16-valve - four valves per cylinder, two overhead camshafts at half crank speed, built to breathe. Turbo petrol - exhaust-driven forced intake, spark ignition, intercooler-guarded. 110 hp at 6,000 rpm - peak power rate, high in the band. 150 Nm at 3,500 rpm - peak turning force, in the everyday mid-range. 6-speed manual - six selectable pairs behind a driver-operated clutch. Front-wheel drive - the differential feeds the front axle.

</details>

Q: List the full drivetrain chain from fuel to road, and name the course phase that built each station.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** Fuel and air burn in the cylinders (Phases 0-3: steam to four-stroke to fuel systems). The push converts to rotation via piston, rod and crankshaft (Phases 0 and 1). The flywheel smooths it and starts it (Phase 4). The clutch switches it (Phase 4). The gearbox picks its ratio (Phase 6). The drive shaft carries it to the axle. The differential splits it between two wheels while letting them differ (Phase 6). The wheels deliver it to the road.

</details>
