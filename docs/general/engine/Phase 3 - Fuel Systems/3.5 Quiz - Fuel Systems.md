# 3.5 Quiz - Fuel Systems
## MCQ

Q: What does a diesel engine's intake stroke pull in?
A. Air mixed with diesel mist, like a petrol engine's mixture
B. Only air, with no fuel at all
C. Pre-warmed air from the exhaust
D. Air only in winter, mixture otherwise
> B. A diesel pulls in pure air and compresses it much harder than a petrol engine compresses its mixture. Fuel is kept out of the cylinder until the very end of compression, when the injector sprays it into the hot air.

---

Q: Why can diesel not be mixed into the intake air early, the way petrol is?
A. Diesel is too thick to flow through the intake path
B. Diesel self-ignites the moment it meets hot compressed air, so it would burn mid-compression at the wrong moment
C. Diesel would freeze in the intake tract
D. Diesel needs more oxygen than the intake provides
> B. Petrol resists self-ignition and waits for the spark; diesel ignites on contact with sufficiently hot air. Early mixing would give the engine no control over timing, so the diesel design keeps fuel out until the injector's chosen instant.

---

Q: In a petrol engine, what decides the exact moment the fuel burns?
A. The injector's spray timing
B. The spark plug
C. The exhaust valve opening
D. The compression ratio alone
> B. Petrol is mixed in early and waits for the spark plug, which fires at the end of compression. In a diesel it is the reverse: the injector's spray timing does the spark's job.

---

Q: Why is fuel sprayed as a fine mist instead of poured in as liquid?
A. Mist looks cleaner in diagrams
B. Burning happens at the surface where fuel meets air, and tiny droplets have vastly more total surface area, so the fuel burns fast and completely
C. Liquid would damage the injector
D. Mist evaporates before compression
> B. A liquid lump burns slowly from its outside inward, and much of it never finds oxygen in time. A mist of droplets puts essentially all the fuel's surface next to air, so the burn can consume it all in the instant the cycle allows.

---

Q: What does the accelerator pedal physically move in a carburetor engine?
A. The fuel bowl's petrol level
B. A flap in the air path, letting more air rush through the narrow spot so more fuel is pulled from the bowl
C. The spark plug's position
D. The timing belt tension
> B. Pressing the pedal opens a flap; more air passes the narrow spot, and faster-moving air pulls proportionally more petrol out of the bowl. In an injector engine the pedal is only a sensor input to the computer.

---

Q: Which of these is NOT one of the factors that made carburetors unable to fine-tune the mixture?
A. Air temperature
B. Altitude
C. Engine speed
D. The fuel's colour
> D. The ideal mix changes with temperature, altitude, engine speed and pedal position - all at once - and a carburetor only reacts to air speed. The computer-controlled injector handles all four, which is why it won from the 1980s onward.

---

Q: What does a glow plug do in a cold diesel?
A. It sparks like a spark plug to light the fuel
B. It electrically preheats the air inside the cylinder before cranking, so compression can reach ignition temperature
C. It heats the fuel lines to prevent waxing
D. It pressurizes the injector
> B. The glow plug is a heating element in the cylinder that warms the air before starting, giving compression a warm baseline on a freezing morning. It does not create a spark - the diesel still has none.

---

## TrueFalse

Q: Modern petrol engines use injectors too.
- True
- False
> True. Nearly all modern petrol cars use fuel injectors - spraying petrol as mist during intake, with the spark plug still choosing the burn moment. The carburetor was the older alternative, pushed out by emissions rules from the 1980s onward.

---

Q: Compressing air harder than a petrol engine does is what makes diesel self-ignition possible.
- True
- False
> True. Squeezing a gas forces its molecules into less space, raising their average speed - which is temperature. The diesel compresses air until it is hot enough that injected fuel ignites on contact.

---

Q: Knocking happens when a petrol engine's mixture self-ignites early, at the wrong instant.
- True
- False
> True. If petrol is squeezed past its self-ignition point, it lights mid-compression while the piston is still rising, causing harsh, damaging stress. That is why petrol engines compress less than diesels.

---

Q: Diesel engines are built lighter than petrol engines because they have fewer parts.
- True
- False
> False. Diesels compress far harder every cycle, putting higher forces on the piston, rod and cylinder, so they are built heavier and stronger throughout - which is why they weigh more.

## ShortAnswer

Q: Write the full comparison of petrol and diesel ignition in four lines: what each intake pulls in, what each compression does, what commands the burn, and why the fuel's personality forces the design.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** Petrol intake pulls in air mixed with petrol mist; diesel intake pulls in only air. Petrol compression squeezes the mixture moderately; diesel compression squeezes air much harder, heating it to self-ignition temperature. Petrol's burn is commanded by the spark plug; diesel's burn is commanded by the injector's spray timing at the end of compression. The fuels' personalities force it: petrol resists self-ignition and can wait for a spark, while diesel ignites on contact with hot compressed air and therefore must be withheld until the chosen instant.

</details>

---

Q: A diesel that runs perfectly in summer fails to start on a freezing morning. Give the two cold-weather problems, their causes, and the part that fixes the engine-side problem.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

> **Sample Answer:** Problem one is fuel: diesel's heavy molecules thicken and form waxy crystals in the cold, which can block fuel lines and filters - fixed by winter-formulated diesel. Problem two is ignition: the design depends on compression heat, but a freezing cylinder air charge and a freezing engine block steal that heat, so the air may never reach ignition temperature - fixed by glow plugs, electric heaters that preheat the cylinder air before cranking.

</details>
