The plumbing is finished: a sealed crankcase storing fresh mixture below, a transfer passage bypassing the piston, and two ports on the cylinder wall at the 4 cm mark that open only near the bottom of the stroke. Now run the machine through its complete cycle - which, as promised, is exactly two strokes - and watch each stroke do two jobs at once.

# 2.4 The Two Strokes and Their Double Jobs

## The up-stroke: compression above, intake below

Start with the piston rising from its lowest position. Follow both sides of it at the same time, because both sides are working.

**Above the piston:** the previous cycle's fresh mixture is sitting in the chamber. As the piston rises, the ports close behind it (the piston's body covers them, per file 2.2's numbers), sealing the chamber. The rising piston squeezes that trapped mixture into a small space - **compression**, exactly as in the four-stroke. At the very top, the spark plug fires and the mixture burns.

**Below the piston, at the same time:** the rising piston makes the sealed crankcase bigger - it is leaving the space, after all. A sealed space that grows drops in pressure, and that vacuum pulls fresh mixture in from outside through the **intake port** on the crankcase wall. So while the piston above is compressing, the piston below is *inhaling*.

That is the up-stroke: **compression and intake, simultaneously** - the piston's body compressing on one face while its departure refills the tank under it.

## The down-stroke: power above, exhaust and refill below

The spark has fired at the top; the burning mixture expands and shoves the piston down. Now the same double act runs in reverse.

**Above the piston:** the expanding gas drives the piston down - **power**, the only real push of the cycle, spinning the crankshaft. In this design that push happens on *every* rotation, which was the whole goal of file 2.1.

**Below the piston, at the same time:** the descending piston crushes the sealed crankcase into a smaller space, and the fresh mixture stored in there - inhaled during the up-stroke - gets **pressurized**. Sealed in with nowhere to go, it waits under pressure for an exit.

**Near the bottom, the window opens:** the piston's top face drops past the 4 cm mark, and the two wall ports stand open at the same time:

- The **exhaust port** opens first into the chamber's reach, and the burnt gas escapes through it.
- The **transfer port** opens, and the pressurized mixture from the crankcase **shoots up the transfer passage** and into the cylinder.

The incoming fresh mixture does more than refill: it helps **push the leftover smoke out** through the exhaust port. Fresh gas arriving under pressure and burnt gas leaving - both through holes open at the same time, in the same short window near the bottom of the stroke.

Then the piston starts rising again, its body covers both ports, the fresh mixture is sealed in, and the up-stroke's compression begins. The loop is closed.

## The cycle in one table

| Stroke | Above the piston | Below the piston |
|---|---|---|
| Up | Compression of trapped mixture; spark at the top | Crankcase grows; vacuum sucks fresh mixture in through the intake port |
| Down | Power - burning gas shoves the piston down | Crankcase squeezed; stored mixture pressurized; near the bottom, ports open - smoke out, fresh mixture shoots in |

Four jobs, two strokes, **one bang per full rotation** - double the power pushes per turn of the four-stroke, delivered by the overlap that file 2.1 promised.

## One consequence you can already smell

Look at the down-stroke once more. Fresh mixture and burnt gas share the same open window, on their way in and out at the same time.
