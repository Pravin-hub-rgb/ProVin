## Chapter 2.3 — Internet Safety & Best Practices

### Strong Passwords

A weak password can be cracked in seconds. A strong password takes years to crack.

| Weak Passwords ❌ | Why They're Weak | Strong Passwords ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| `password123` | Too common | `Tr0ub4dor&3!` |
| `rahul2000` | Uses personal info | `Sch00l@Oct#2024` |
| `abcd1234` | Simple pattern | `MyN@me1sR@hul!` |

**Rules for a strong password:**
  - At least **8 characters** (12+ is better)
  - Mix of **uppercase** + **lowercase** + **numbers** + **special characters** (`!@#$%`)
  - **No personal info** — don't use your name, birthday, or phone number
  - **Different password** for every account — if one gets hacked, others stay safe
  - Never share your password — not on email, not on phone, not with anyone

> **💡 Tip:** Use a **password manager** like Google's built-in password manager or Bitwarden. You only need to remember one master password — the manager remembers the rest.

### Phishing — Fake Emails That Steal Information

Phishing is when someone sends you a fake email that looks real, trying to steal your password, bank details, or personal information.

![Phishing Warning — Fake vs Real Email](/diagrams/iknowcomputers/phishing.svg)

#### How to Spot a Phishing Email

| Sign | What to Look For |
|---|---|
| **Unknown sender** | You don't recognize the email address |
| **Urgent or scary subject** | "Your account will be closed!" "Payment overdue!" |
| **Fake links** | Hover over the link (without clicking) — the URL has spelling mistakes or a weird domain |
| **Unexpected attachment** | You weren't expecting a file from this person |
| **Poor grammar** | Spelling mistakes, awkward sentences |
| **Requests personal info** | Bank, email, or password details — real companies NEVER ask for these via email |

> **⚠️ Golden rule:** No legitimate company will ever ask for your password, bank details, or personal information by email. If you're unsure, call the company directly using their official phone number — don't use any number from the email.

### Safe Browsing Habits

| Do This ✅ | Don't Do This ❌ |
|---|---|
| Use **HTTPS** websites (lock icon in the address bar) | Enter passwords on HTTP sites (no lock) |
| Download from official websites only | Click "Download" on pop-up ads |
| Use **Incognito mode** on shared computers | Save passwords in the browser on shared computers |
| Keep your browser updated | Ignore browser update warnings |
| Log out of accounts on shared computers | Stay logged in when you're done |

> **💡 Tip:** If a website looks suspicious, check the URL carefully. Phishing sites often use addresses like `g00gle.com` (with zeros) instead of `google.com`.

---

### General Office Safety Rules

  - **Lock your computer:** Press `Windows+L` every time you leave your desk — even for a minute
  - **Don't save passwords in the browser** on shared office computers
  - **Backup important files:** Copy important documents to Google Drive or an external drive weekly
  - **USB drives:** Some offices don't allow USB drives for security — check the policy
  - **Personal use:** Keep personal browsing and social media to a minimum on office computers
  - **Think before you click:** If an offer looks too good to be true, it probably is

### 🧠 Chapter Summary

> ✅ **Strong password** = 8+ chars, mix of cases + numbers + symbols, no personal info  
> ✅ **Different password** for every account — use a password manager  
> ✅ **Phishing** = fake emails trying to steal your info — check sender, links, urgency  
> ✅ **HTTPS** (lock icon) = safe browsing. HTTP = not safe for sensitive info  
> ✅ **Lock your computer** (`Windows+L`) every time you leave your desk  
> ✅ **Think before you click** — if it seems suspicious, don't click

### 🎯 Real Task

1. Check your Gmail Spam folder. Pick 2-3 spam emails and identify the phishing signs: unknown sender? Urgent subject? Suspicious link? Fake attachment? Note them down.
2. Go to a website you use often (like your bank or social media). Check the address bar — does it have the HTTPS lock icon? If yes, click the lock and see the connection details.
3. Create **one strong password** using the rules above. Write it on paper (not in a digital file) and keep it safe — or add it to a password manager if you use one.
