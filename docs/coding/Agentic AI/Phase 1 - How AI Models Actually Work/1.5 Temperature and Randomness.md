Back in the API request we looked at, there was a parameter called `temperature`. You might have seen it in your AI tool's settings and wondered what it does. Let's demystify it.

**Temperature controls how "creative" the model's predictions are.**

Remember: a language model works by picking the next most likely token, one at a time. But "most likely" isn't the only option. Behind the scenes, the model assigns a probability score to every possible next token — a ranked list of candidates. At temperature 0, the model always picks the single highest-probability token. Every time. Same input, same output. Deterministic.

As you turn up the temperature, the model starts rolling dice. It still prefers high-probability tokens, but it occasionally reaches a bit lower down the list and picks something less obvious. The higher the temperature, the more often it takes those less-likely paths.

Think of it like this:

- **Temperature 0** — The model plays it safe. "The cat sat on the..." → "mat." Every single time.
- **Temperature 0.7** — The model might pick "rug," "floor," or "windowsill" instead. Same sentence, different flavors.
- **Temperature 1.0+** — The model might pick something surprising like "spaceship" or "stock market." Still grammatically correct, but less predictable.

**Low temperature (0–0.3): You want consistency.** This is what you use for coding, data extraction, following precise instructions, or anything where correctness matters. You want the model to do the obvious right thing every time. Most coding agents default to a low temperature — usually around 0.3 — because you don't want your AI to get *creative* with your production database schema.

**Medium temperature (0.4–0.7): Balanced.** Good for general conversation, explaining concepts, writing documentation. The model has room to vary its phrasing without going off the rails.

**High temperature (0.7–1.0): Creative mode.** Useful for brainstorming, creative writing, generating multiple options, or coming up with alternative approaches. The trade-off: the model is more likely to hallucinate, make mistakes, or go off-topic.

Some models let you go above 1.0, but that's usually a bad idea. At that point, the model is actively avoiding obvious choices and the output quality degrades quickly.

Here's the key insight: **temperature doesn't add randomness like chaos. It adds controlled variation.** The model still only picks from tokens that make grammatical sense. Temperature just shifts whether it picks the safest choice or something more adventurous from the probability distribution.

For your coding workflows, keep temperature low when you want the right answer. Dial it up when you want the model to suggest multiple approaches or think outside the box. But remember: if you ask the same question twice at high temperature, you'll get two different answers — neither of which may be correct. Temperature is a creative tool, not a fact-finding one.
