You've used AI coding tools. You type a prompt, hit send, and something intelligent comes back. But what actually *is* the thing you're talking to?

Let's get one thing straight right away: **a language model is not a brain.** It doesn't think, understand, or have intentions. It's a mathematical pattern-recognition machine trained on an absolutely enormous amount of text — basically the public internet. And what it does is surprisingly simple: it predicts the next word (or really, the next *token*, but we'll get to that).

Here's the mental model you should keep in your head:

> A language model is autocomplete on steroids.

You know how your phone suggests the next word when you're typing? "I'm going to the..." and it guesses "store" or "gym"? A language model does exactly that — except it's been trained on trillions of examples, has billions of internal parameters, and can carry the thread for thousands of words instead of three.

When you send a prompt, the model looks at everything you've said so far and asks itself: "Given this text, what word is most likely to come next?" It picks one. Then it asks again: "Now what comes after *that*?" One word at a time, it builds a response.

This means the model doesn't "plan" your answer like a human does. It doesn't think "I'll write three paragraphs, first covering X, then Y." It just keeps picking the next most probable token until it reaches a stopping point. The coherence you see is a side effect of the pattern-matching being really, *really* good — not actual reasoning.

Think of it like this: a human writes from meaning to words. A language model writes from words to words. The meaning is something *you* project onto it.

That might sound limiting, but here's the magic: when you train this simple "predict the next word" game on the entire internet, the patterns it learns encode an incredible amount of knowledge — facts, writing styles, code syntax, reasoning patterns, even chain-of-thought approaches. It's just that the model doesn't *know* it knows any of this. It's a giant autocomplete that got so good it looks like a mind.

Now, I said the model predicts the next "word" — but that's not quite right. It actually works with something smaller and more flexible: **tokens**. That's what we're looking at next.
