**Reality check first:**

CS fundamentals (OS, DBMS, CN, OOP) — they don't carry as much weight as DSA in most places, but you can't completely skip them. Especially when your formal internship/college project background is a bit thin (if you came via the freelance route), interviewers use these questions to check whether your fundamentals are clear or you're just a tutorial-copy-paste developer.

**Where they ask the most:**

- **DBMS** — asked the most in full-stack roles, since you already use Postgres/Supabase in DukaanOS. Normalization, indexing, joins, transactions, ACID — these are practical and directly useful for your work.
- **OS** — asked less in full-stack (more in backend/infra-heavy roles), but they expect process vs thread, deadlock, memory management basics.
- **CN (Computer Networks)** — HTTP/HTTPS, TCP vs UDP, DNS, REST basics — directly relevant for web dev.
- **OOP** — concepts (encapsulation, inheritance, polymorphism) + being able to give a real code example is important.

**Priority order for your case:**

1. **DBMS** (highest priority — directly connects to your project)
2. **CN basics** (relevant to web dev)
3. **OOP concepts** (you'll already know some from TypeScript)
4. **OS** (lowest priority for full-stack roles, just basics)

**How deep to go:** No need to study full "GATE-level OS". Just get clear on 10-15 common interview questions per topic — that's enough. More than depth, "can you explain it confidently" is what matters.

**When to do it:** Not parallel to DSA phases — wait until DSA settles a bit (say around Phase 2-3), then in a short burst — 1-2 weeks total for DBMS + CN + OOP. OS last, or skip it if time is tight.
