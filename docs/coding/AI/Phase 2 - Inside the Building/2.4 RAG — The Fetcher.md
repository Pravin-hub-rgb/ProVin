The building is labeled, and the middle floor has three strings: memory, RAG, tools. Memory got pulled last time — the table, history re-sent every turn, compaction when it fills. Now the second string, the one you've heard most in the wild. It fits in one sentence:

> **RAG is the string that fetches knowledge.**

When you ask a question, the controller first looks up relevant information — your documents, your database — and puts it on the table with your question. Then the LLM answers using that fresh material. RAG fetches. The LLM reads and answers.

Easy to tangle with the string we just pulled — memory. Memory is the *conversation*: what was said earlier, re-sent each turn. RAG is different: *knowledge the model never had at all*. Your leave policy isn't something the model forgot — it's something it never saw. Different strings, different jobs.

The name is three words doing three jobs: **Retrieval Augmented Generation**. *Retrieval* — fetching information first. *Augmented* — boosted, added to. *Generation* — the LLM producing the answer. Retrieve first, generate with what you retrieved. The order in the name is the order in the flow.

Now the example. You ask an LLM-powered app: *"What's our company's leave policy?"* The LLM never saw your company documents — alone, it would guess: fluent, confident, possibly wrong. Limit two in action. With RAG: the controller searches your documents, finds the leave policy page, puts that text on the table next to your question, and the LLM reads it and answers correctly. Looking things up before answering — fetching the right material so the answer has something true to stand on.

There's a tempting picture here, and you had it: the LLM sees the question, notices it's missing data, raises its hand — *"I need this, fetch it"* — and RAG obeys. That's a real picture, but it's the picture of an **agent**, where the model decides it needs a tool. Classic RAG works the other way round, and the difference is the key point: **in classic RAG, the LLM doesn't ask first. The controller acts before the model ever sees your question.**

The order: question arrives → controller searches the database → controller bundles → one package goes to the LLM: your question plus the retrieved text → the LLM reads the package and writes the answer. Three things fall out of that order. The LLM never knew data was missing; it just reads one package. The search happens in the database, before the LLM — no searching, no sorting inside the model. And the LLM *does* look through the retrieved text — it reads the package carefully, pulls out the relevant part — it just never touches the database itself.

Your sharp question came next: if RAG found the policy page, why do we even need the LLM? Because **RAG only fetches.** What it brings back is raw pieces of documents — a stack of pages, maybe the right paragraph buried among three wrong ones. The LLM reads the pages, understands your actual question, pulls out the relevant part, writes a clear answer in plain language. **RAG is the librarian who brings the right books. The LLM is the reader who explains what's in them.**

And one boundary so the map stays honest — your GitHub question settled it. Things fetched live from the internet are not in anyone's fingerprint database; that's a different door. RAG searches *your stored collection* — documents loaded ahead of time. **Tools** bring in live stuff — the web, an API, a repo — as plain text straight to the LLM. Two doors: RAG for your library, tools for the live world. The tools door itself gets pulled in Phase 3.

The nots: RAG is *not* memory — that's the string we pulled before it. It's *not* the LLM asking for help — the controller moves first, no request, no back-and-forth. It's *not* a search engine over the whole internet — classically it searches your own stored collection. And it's *not* a product — it's an idea; you can build it with LangChain, other libraries, or by hand.
