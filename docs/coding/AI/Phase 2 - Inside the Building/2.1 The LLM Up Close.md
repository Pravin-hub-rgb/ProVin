## The LLM's One Skill

Strip away every use case and an LLM does exactly one thing: **look at some text and predict what comes next.**

That's it. That's the whole machine. And because that's all it does, every impressive-looking behavior is that same move in a different disguise:

- Answer a question? Continue the text "Question: ... Answer:"
- Write code? Continue the text of a program.
- Summarize an article? Continue the text with a shorter version at the top.

Writing, answering, coding, translating — all "continue this text", dressed up differently.

---

## The Locked Definition

Here's the sentence to keep, word for word:

> **An LLM is a model trained on a very large amount of text data, and it uses what it learned to predict the next text.**

Three parts, all already on your map:

- **model** — the trained result, the big file of parameters (1.2)
- **trained on a very large amount of text data** — that's the *large* and the *language* (1.4)
- **predict the next text** — the one skill from the section above

You can say that sentence to anyone and be correct. No other name is needed — no "something", no mysterious box.

---

## Flavors, Not Categories

You've met several LLMs — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini — and they *do* feel different: one is sharper at code, another at writing, another at reasoning. Does that mean different *kinds* of model?

No. Different LLMs differ in a few ordinary ways:

1. **What data they trained on** — different piles of text, different strengths.
2. **How big they are** — parameter count and training scale.
3. **How they were tuned afterward** — extra passes that shape behavior (that last one gets its own phase later; for now, just know it exists).

They're all still LLMs — same category, same one skill. Different flavors of the same thing, not new species on the diagram.

## The Setup for What Comes Next

Here's the friendly twist that organizes everything after this file. On its own, an LLM is powerful and limited at the same time. It does its one skill brilliantly — and it has three hard limits that no amount of training removes.

Those three limits are the reason everything else exists. The middle layer of the building, RAG, tools, memory — each one is a fix for a specific limit.

We take them one at a time, starting in the next file. But the headline to hold: **the buzzwords you've been hearing — RAG, tools, agents — aren't random additions. They're answers to the LLM's three limits.**

---

## What the LLM Is NOT

- **Not a database or a search engine.** It predicts text from learned patterns; it doesn't look anything up (that's a job for the layer above it — later files).
- **Not "doing things on its own."** It's doing *one thing* very well: predicting what text comes next. Answering, coding, writing all come out of that single skill.
- **Not a new species per brand.** Claude vs ChatGPT vs Gemini are flavors of the same category, not different entries on the diagram.
- **Not unlimited.** Three limits, coming right up — they're the whole reason the rest of this course exists.
